## The Project

A property developer in Hove commissioned us to carry out the full plumbing and central heating installation for a new-build four-bedroom detached property in the Hove Park area. The specification included underfloor heating to the entire ground floor and a conventional radiator system to the first floor — a combination that requires careful heat loss calculation and system design.

## Design and Heat Loss Calculation

Before any work began, we carried out a room-by-room heat loss calculation using SAP assessment data provided by the architect. The combined heating load for the property — 180m² with high-spec insulation — was calculated at approximately 14kW. A 36kW combi boiler was specified to handle the domestic hot water demand alongside the heating load; on a four-bedroom property with two bathrooms, DHW demand is the dominant sizing factor.

Ground floor UFH pipe spacing was designed at 150mm centres for living areas and 200mm centres for the hallway — producing different surface temperatures appropriate for each use.

## First Fix

First fix plumbing ran in the first two weeks of the construction programme, coordinated with the structural stage. UFH pipe was laid in the slab prior to the screed pour — 450 metres of 16mm PEX-Al-PEX pipe serving six ground floor zones connected to a stainless manifold in the kitchen utility cupboard.

Boiler position, flue route, and gas meter position were agreed with the building control inspector at the first-fix stage inspection.

## Second Fix

Second fix completed after plastering. First-floor radiators — eight designer panel radiators specified by the interior designer — were hung and connected. Boiler, manifold, and controls were commissioned once the screed had cured to dryness (approximately six weeks after pour).

The UFH thermostat and first-floor smart controller were wired by our team and connected to the client's smart home system.

## Commissioning

System commissioning took half a day. Each UFH zone was flow-balanced at the manifold. First-floor radiator lockshield valves were set using a flow meter to achieve balanced distribution. Boiler output was checked with a combustion analyser.

Building regulations notification and Gas Safe certificate issued on completion.
