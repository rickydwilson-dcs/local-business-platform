## The Repair vs Replace Decision

When a boiler breaks down, especially in the middle of a cold East Sussex winter, the pressure to make a quick decision can lead to the wrong one. Paying for a repair on a boiler that's going to fail again six months later is expensive. Replacing a boiler that has years of reliable service left is equally wasteful.

Here is a structured way to think through the decision.

## The Age Rule of Thumb

Modern condensing combi boilers have a typical reliable service life of 10–15 years when properly serviced. If your boiler is:

- **Under 8 years old:** Repair is almost always the right call, unless the fault is catastrophic (heat exchanger failure, irreparable PCB damage).
- **8–12 years old:** Consider the repair cost as a fraction of replacement cost. If a repair costs more than 25–30% of a new boiler installation, replacement is worth serious consideration.
- **Over 12 years old:** Factor in that further breakdowns are increasingly likely. A series of £300–500 repairs can quickly exceed replacement cost. Replacing now avoids those costs and delivers improved efficiency.

## Fault Types That Point Toward Replacement

Some faults are inherently worth repairing regardless of age — a failed pump, a faulty diverter valve, a pressure sensor. Others signal that a boiler's life is effectively over:

**Heat exchanger failure** is the most significant. Heat exchangers are expensive to replace and their failure often indicates that other components are at a similar stage of life. The repair cost of a heat exchanger can be 60–80% of a new boiler installation.

**Persistent pilot light or ignition failure** on an older boiler often means the PCB (printed circuit board) is failing. PCB replacements on older models can be difficult to source and expensive.

**Persistent pressure loss** that cannot be traced to a specific leak often indicates internal corrosion. Corrosion-related pressure loss is a sign that the boiler's integrity is compromised.

## Running Costs: The Efficiency Argument

A boiler installed before 2005 may have an efficiency rating of 70–80%. Modern A-rated condensing boilers operate at 90–94% efficiency. For a typical East Sussex home spending £1,500/year on gas, that efficiency gap represents £150–360/year in additional running costs.

Over 10 years, an inefficient older boiler can cost £1,500–3,600 more to run than a modern replacement. That cost should factor into the repair vs replace calculation.

## What a Gas Safe Engineer Will Tell You

A reputable Gas Safe registered plumber will give you an honest assessment. If we visit a boiler and find a straightforward fault on a relatively young appliance, we'll repair it. If we find an older boiler with a secondary fault alongside the primary fault — a combination that suggests general deterioration — we'll tell you that clearly and give you both options with costs.

Be wary of any engineer who reflexively recommends replacement without explanation, or conversely one who repairs without flagging that replacement may be more economical in the medium term.

## What to Look for in a New Boiler

If replacement is the right call:

- **Combi vs system vs regular:** Most modern homes in East Sussex with a single bathroom suit a combi boiler (no tank, no cylinder). Larger homes with multiple bathrooms may benefit from a system or regular boiler with a hot water cylinder.
- **Output rating:** Sized to the property — not automatically the largest available. An oversized boiler cycles on and off more frequently, reducing efficiency.
- **Brand and warranty:** Major brands (Worcester Bosch, Vaillant, Baxi, Viessmann) offer 7–10 year warranties on parts and labour when installed by registered engineers.

## Annual Servicing is Not Optional

Whichever decision you make — repair or replace — annual servicing is the single most important thing you can do to extend boiler life and maintain warranty validity. A service catches developing problems before they become breakdowns. At DCS Plumbing, we recommend booking the service in late summer, before the heating season begins and before engineers are occupied with emergency calls.
