  - question: "What type of fencing is most durable?"
    answer: "Close board (featheredge) panel fencing on concrete posts and gravel boards is the most durable standard option — the concrete base components will outlast multiple fence lifetimes, and individual boards can be replaced without replacing the whole run. For a more attractive finish, a capped close board with arched top rails is the premium version of the same system."
  - question: "What is the maximum height I can fence without planning permission?"
    answer: "In most cases, fences up to 2 metres are permitted without planning permission. Fences adjacent to a highway (including a driveway) are usually limited to 1 metre. Conservation Areas and listed properties may have additional restrictions. We check the applicable rules for your site before installation."
  - question: "Do you replace old fence panels and posts?"
    answer: "Yes. We remove and dispose of old fencing as part of the installation. Where existing concrete posts are sound and correctly positioned, we may be able to retain them — we assess this on site. Rotten timber posts are always replaced."
  - question: "Can you install electric or automated gates?"
    answer: "We install timber gates and can prepare gate posts and framework to accept automation. We work with specialist automation companies for the electronic components and motor fitting. We advise on gate weight and swing clearance at the design stage to ensure automation is viable."
  - question: "Who is responsible for a boundary fence — me or my neighbour?"
    answer: "Boundary responsibility is defined in your title deeds — the 'T' marks on the plan show which boundary each party is responsible for. If your deeds are unclear, we can advise on how to check, and where there is a shared boundary, on how to approach the conversation with your neighbour."
