  - question: "What is crown reduction?"
    answer: "Crown reduction reduces the overall size of a tree's canopy — height, spread, or both — while preserving the natural shape and structure. It is used when a tree has outgrown its position, is affecting light to a building, or is creating pressure on a boundary. All cuts are made to the correct pruning points to minimise regrowth and stress to the tree."
  - question: "Do I need permission to fell a tree?"
    answer: "This depends on location. Trees in Conservation Areas and trees covered by a Tree Preservation Order (TPO) require planning permission before any work. We check TPO and Conservation Area status for your property before quoting and advise on the correct process. Felling a protected tree without consent can result in significant fines."
  - question: "What is stump grinding?"
    answer: "Stump grinding uses a rotating cutting disc to grind the stump and surface roots down below ground level. The resultant wood chip can be used as mulch or removed. Grinding is the quickest and least disruptive way to remove a stump after felling — it avoids the upheaval of full stump extraction."
  - question: "Are you insured for tree surgery?"
    answer: "Yes. We carry full public liability insurance to £5 million, covering all tree surgery work including aerial operations and tree felling. Insurance certificates are available on request."
  - question: "Do you remove the timber and wood chip after felling?"
    answer: "All timber, brash, and wood chip arising from tree surgery is removed from site as standard. Logs can be left if you would like them for firewood — just let us know at the time of booking."
