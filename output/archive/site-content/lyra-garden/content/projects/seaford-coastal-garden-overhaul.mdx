## The Situation

A Seaford clifftop property had a rear garden that had resisted all attempts by the owners to establish a conventional lawn and traditional planting. The combination of salt spray, chalk-based free-draining soil, and exposure to prevailing south-westerly winds had defeated multiple attempts over the years. A conventional panel fence had partially collapsed in a winter storm.

The owners had reached the sensible conclusion that fighting the conditions was not the right strategy. They wanted a redesign that worked with the coastal environment rather than against it.

## Design Philosophy

The design approach was to reduce the amount of garden that required intensive management and focus on robust, appropriate plantings in sheltered raised beds rather than ground-level borders exposed to full wind and spray.

**Paving as the primary surface:** Replacing 65% of the garden area with Indian sandstone eliminated the failed lawn maintenance challenge, created a usable outdoor space in all but the worst conditions, and provided a hard-wearing surface that would withstand coastal conditions indefinitely.

**Slotted-board fencing:** A solid panel fence creates a low-pressure zone on the leeward side and then a wind tunnel effect beyond it — often more damaging than no fence at all. Slotted-board fencing (boards with gaps between them) allows wind to pass through while still providing a privacy and structural function. We specified boards in hardwood that would weather naturally in coastal conditions.

**Raised gravel beds:** Two curved raised beds containing a free-draining gravel mulch over improved soil provided planting areas that would not waterlog and where wind-tolerant plants could establish protected by the fence line.

**Planting selection:** Species chosen for salt tolerance and wind resistance — sea thrift (Armeria maritima), red fescue, Pittosporum, Euonymus japonicus, Phormium, and Lavandula angustifolia. All are proven performers in exposed coastal conditions in East Sussex.

## Works

The project ran over two weeks. Existing failed fencing and lawn were cleared in the first three days. Sandstone paving laid over the main area with a concrete edge course. Slotted-board fencing installed on timber posts set in concrete. Raised beds formed in timber railway sleepers and filled with a 70% topsoil / 30% sharp grit mix for the required drainage. Gravel mulch applied and planting installed.

## Result

The completed garden transformed the owners' relationship with the outdoor space. In the six weeks following completion, they reported using the paved area regularly for the first time. The planting was establishing well, with the phormiums and sea thrift looking particularly vigorous.
