## The Starting Point

A family in Hailsham had purchased a property where the rear garden had been left unmanaged for approximately four years. What had once been a functional garden with a lawn, borders, and a patio had become a largely impassable space dominated by four-year growth of coarse grass, brambles, self-seeded sycamores, a collapsed timber fence, and a 2.5-metre hedge that had gone unpruned for at least four of those years.

The clients had a clear brief: they wanted a usable family garden where their children could play and they could sit out in summer. They had a realistic budget and wanted the project completed before the autumn school term.

## Scope Assessment

Before quoting, we spent an hour walking the garden and identifying:

**Trees and significant growth:**
- Three self-seeded sycamores, the largest approximately 4 metres
- A large buddleja that had become a woody multi-stemmed shrub approximately 3 metres in diameter
- A row of overgrown privet along the back boundary that had grown to approximately 2.5 metres and lost its formal shape

**Surface and structure:**
- The original lawn was present under the grass and weed growth but would need complete renovation
- An original patio of concrete slabs was buried under debris and growth — potentially salvageable
- A 12-metre section of timber panel fence had partially collapsed

**Volume of waste:**
We estimated 8–10 tonnes of green waste requiring removal — a significant factor in project cost and logistics.

## Project Plan: Five Working Days

**Day 1 — Tree removal and structure:**
The three sycamores were felled and the main stems chipped on-site. The buddleja was cut back hard to a 30cm framework — it will regrow vigorously the following year. The collapsed fence panels were removed and set aside.

**Day 2 — Clearance:**
The bramble growth across the borders and along boundaries was strimmed and dug out root and all. Bramble roots are persistent — surface clearance alone is insufficient. Roots were traced and removed to a depth of approximately 20–30cm where they were accessible. The bulk of the cleared material was loaded for waste removal.

**Day 3 — Hedge trimming and patio salvage:**
The privet hedge was reduced to approximately 1.5 metres and shaped to a formal profile — it will require two further growing seasons to fully recover its density and shape. The concrete slab patio was uncovered, cleaned with a pressure washer, and assessed — all slabs were intact and reusable. The area was usable again by end of day.

**Day 4 — Lawn renovation:**
The original lawn area was rotavated to break up the compacted grass and weed root system, then raked to a fine tilth. A lawn pre-seeder fertiliser was applied, and the area was overseeded with a hard-wearing family lawn mix — appropriate for a garden with children. The area was raked in and watered.

**Day 5 — Fence, waste removal and handover:**
The clients had arranged new fence panels. We dug new post holes where existing posts were damaged, set new concrete posts, and fitted the new panels. Final green waste was removed from the site — a 12-tonne load — and the garden was raked clear.

## The Outcome

At handover on day 5, the garden was unrecognisable from its starting condition. The patio was clean and accessible, the lawn was seeded and would establish over the following 3–4 weeks, the hedge and borders were managed and clear, and the fence was secure.

The lawn took approximately four weeks to establish sufficiently for light use. The clients seeded a 2m × 4m vegetable bed in the border area themselves in the weeks following.

**Illustrative project cost:** A garden clearance and renovation of this scope — approximately 80m² of garden — would typically cost £1,800–2,800 including waste removal, depending on the specific site conditions and regional rates.

## What Makes These Projects Work

**Realistic scope assessment:** Going in with a clear picture of what needs removing and what can be salvaged avoids surprises for both client and contractor.

**Waste removal logistics:** Green waste removal from a large clearance project requires a licenced waste carrier and correct disposal. This is a significant cost but a legal requirement.

**Telling clients what won't look good immediately:** The lawn takes four weeks to establish. The hedge takes two to three seasons to fully recover. Setting honest expectations avoids disappointment.
