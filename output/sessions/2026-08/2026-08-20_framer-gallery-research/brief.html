How the two rounds fit together

What each half answered

The rounds targeted different halves of the original complaint, so the combined brief has two distinct spines.

Round one — the gallery

→ "components weren't elevated"
  • Real published sites across agency, business, SaaS/AI, landing page, portfolio and featured.
  • Produced the component vocabulary and the only measured mobile evidence.

Round two — the templates

→ "engaging yet functional animation"
  • 159 agency templates, 85 demos driven with real wheel events.
  • Produced the motion policy and the anti-pattern ledger. Desktop only.
The connection worth naming

Round two found the dominant defect across this entire design world is content held at opacity: 0 until an IntersectionObserver fires. That is very likely a contributor to our own prototypes' "dreadful mobile" — it isn't a layout bug, it's a content-visibility bug, and it shows up worst on slow connections and small screens. Part 1 exists so we don't inherit it.

Part 1

Motion policy

Non-negotiable. Nine of twenty-seven demos in one batch showed the visitor a blank screen on arrival. Paid templates at $49–$129 render nothing at all.

  1. Content renders at full opacity by default. Never author opacity: 0 as the resting state. Animate transform from a visible state, or opacity 0.6 → 1 so the intermediate state stays legible.
  2. Never gate anything above the fold. The hero is complete at first paint.
  3. Arm hidden states from JS only after support is confirmed. A dead script must leave a readable page.
  4. Latch reveals — one-shot, then unobserve. One template blanks its About section when you scroll back up.
  5. Wrap motion in prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference, with the un-animated state being the complete one.
  6. Acceptance test: screenshot with JavaScript disabled. If that isn't a complete page, the build is wrong.

Banned outright

Count-up numbers

Your instruction, and twelve were caught mid-flight showing false figures — 0+ years of experience, Awards 0, one frozen permanently at 01+. Sylven rendered 0% / 0% / $0.

Preloaders

Three demos never finished loading at all (16+ seconds). One used a percentage counter as the preloader.

Welcome gates

A full-screen "scroll to explore" panel between the visitor and the homepage; a modal fired before you've read anything.

Scroll-jacking

Wheel remapped to a horizontal filmstrip; a hero that refuses to scroll; a carousel that captures the wheel and never releases.

Incomplete text fills

Per-character scroll-fill that leaves the tail of every headline ghosted at rest — Virtual Reality Encounte. Motion that makes content less readable when it stops.

Fake live data

One nav clock ran backwards. Another hardcodes "Available — EARLY FEB 2025", stale 18 months. Wire it to a real source or make it static text.

Allowed — motion that carries information

The test: remove it, and does the visitor lose something they need? All reproducible in Next.js. None needs a proprietary scroll engine.

Part 2

Component vocabulary

Abstracted patterns, mostly from round one. Build any of these fresh — nothing here is "clone site X".

The in-card mock

A flat tinted canvas. Grey skeleton bars for text that doesn't matter. Exactly one element in full colour — the thing the caption is about.

Three agents reverse-engineered this independently. ~20 lines of CSS, and it never dates the way a product screenshot does. Real screenshots go in full-bleed slabs, never inside a card — verified at 390px, where the abstract mock stays legible and the real screenshot becomes a grey smudge. This is a mobile technique, not just an aesthetic one.

The "not done yet" row — a greyed line with a hollow radio dot — is the cheapest way to make a static mock look live. For DCS the single full-colour element is an Enquiry received toast, a Site live badge, or a £0 due today chip.

Services — four alternatives to the card grid

Four agents independently concluded that a flat card is often worse than no card. "Elevated" may mean removing container chrome rather than decorating it.

Audience segmentation

the component DCS most obviously needs
  • Persona tab switcher — Trades · Salons · Garages · Professional swapping the block below. Plain text tabs with the active in accent is the quieter version.
  • Sector card grid — two-tone line illustration + sector + Learn more ›, linking to sector pages our MDX routes already support.
  • Sticky audience rail — two-column by construction; needs the mobile chip-scroller treatment.

Process / how it works

sequence, legibly
  • Numbered rows — numeral, one-word label, body, thumbnail, hairline between. Reads as one table, not four cards.
  • Two-tile stacks — numeral tile above, name+copy tile below; the set assembles diagonally, colour-coded per step.
  • Duotone photography inside the step card — the fix for not controlling client photography; makes mismatched sources look like one set.
  • Numeral inline on the title's baseline, small. A giant ghost numeral is the generic choice.

Pricing

survive the phone; qualify the click
  • Vertical selector list, not columns — the only pricing construction verified intact at 390px. It was never columns, so there's nothing to squeeze.
  • Retainer + project shape — one retainer card, reassurance strips outside the cards, then a separate horizontal project card at a "from" price.
  • Objection-led tiers — open each with the customer's own objection in quotes.
  • Make the price the button — From £95/month → as the CTA itself.
  • One loud button only. Own icon per feature row, not repeated ticks.

Proof — replacing the stats bar

since count-ups are out
  • Accreditation rows — seal + name + tags + year on hairlines. Gas Safe, NICEIC, Checkatrade, ICO.
  • Stats as a two-column table (New clients ——— 15) — reads as a table, so nobody animates it.
  • Marquee of authored literals — motion, but every figure is DOM text, always correct.
  • Testimonial with the client's own logo, plus town and year for local proof.
  • Spec table — company number, years trading, coverage area, response time. "Who you're actually hiring."

Nav and status

look staffed and reachable
  • Superscript numerals that are real counts — Projects⁰⁷, populated from MDX frontmatter.
  • Avatar in the CTA — a real photograph set into the left edge of "Book a call".
  • Status cluster — dot + Based in East Sussex + real local time + availability.
  • Bottom-pinned pill nav — thumb-reachable by construction, not by media query.

Contact and lead capture

the highest-value component on the page
  • Photo-upload dropzone — "send us your current site or a screenshot of the problem". Worth more than any amount of form styling.
  • Channel tiles instead of a form — phone, email, WhatsApp, book a call.
  • Pre-qualifying select plus a reassurance line under the button.
  • Symptom checklist — pill cards naming a problem the visitor recognises in themselves ("looks fine on your laptop, broken on a phone"), not a feature you sell.

Section furniture — the "elevated" vocabulary

cheap, static, responsible for most of the perceived craft
  • Section header bar on a hairline: [01] · // WHAT WE DO · © 2026. Or the two-slot version: label hard left, (01) hard right.
  • Corner metadata framing the hero — fill the slot with proof copy, not decoration.
  • Two-tone headings — line 1 near-black, line 2 at 40% grey. Free hierarchy, no extra type sizes. Use the static version, not the scroll-driven fill.
  • Mono uppercase eyebrow chips — square/bordered reads "spec sheet"; pill reads "product".
  • Hand-drawn marker — a rough ellipse round one phrase. Found independently on two unrelated sites, so a live trend, not one designer's tic. Once or twice per page, maximum.
  • Diagonal-hatch placeholder tiles — images that haven't loaded degrade to a designed box, not white space.
Part 3

Mobile rules

Half the original complaint. Only four sites were measured at a true 390px viewport — everything else is construction-based inference, and is flagged as such.

  1. Pricing is a vertical selector, never columns. Verified.
  2. Per-card CTA survives stacking — flat service blocks each keeping a full-width Enquire now footer bar stack cleanly, and every service stays thumb-reachable. Verified.
  3. Abstract mocks survive; screenshots don't. Verified side by side.
  4. Anything hover-only needs an authored tap equivalent — not a reflow.
  5. Anything constructionally two-column (sticky rails, split screens) needs an explicit mobile design.
  6. Never put display type at low opacity over a photograph — a hero at ~35% white over a busy image was illegible at 390px, worse than desktop.
Two traps for whoever builds this

The floating-nav containing block. A centred dock built with translateX(-50%) and/or backdrop-filter becomes the containing block for any position: fixed mobile dialog inside it. Centre with left/right/margin-inline and portal the dialog to document.body. Already in CLAUDE.md — this pattern is exactly where it bites.

Testing. resize_window does not work in our Chrome tooling — the screenshot tool pins a viewport override and reports success while innerWidth stays at desktop. Inject the page into a same-origin <iframe width="390"> instead. And reveals don't fire on programmatic scroll, so drive motion with real wheel events.

Part 4

Three directions to prototype

Deliberately divergent, so the comparison is informative. Paste each into a design skill, then append the shared constraints block.

A — Spec sheet

Design a homepage for DCS, a small East Sussex firm that builds and maintains
websites for local businesses (trades, salons, garages, small professional firms).
Aesthetic: engineering/blueprint restraint. Off-white ground, near-black type, one
accent used no more than four times on the page. A repeating section-header bar on a
hairline — index left, mono uppercase label centre, year right. Services as a
typographic table: name at full contrast left, right-aligned stack of deliverables.
Process as numbered rows in a four-column grid with hairlines between. Proof as an
accreditation table, not a stats bar. A spec table giving company number, years
trading, coverage area and response time. No cards, no shadows, no rounded corners.
Motion limited to a reading-position highlight on the services list and a sticky
section label.

B — Poster

Design a homepage for DCS (as above). Aesthetic: agency poster. Near-black ground,
heavy condensed grotesk display type at viewport scale, two flat accent colours plus
bone. Hero is a statement line with project imagery interleaved in z — some cards
passing in front of the letterforms, some behind — drifting at different scroll rates.
Process as four two-tile stacks: a tall tile carrying only the numeral at ~200px, a
second tile beneath with a plain-spoken step name and one paragraph; the four assemble
diagonally, each offset down-right, colour-coded per step. Services as flat zero-radius
colour blocks, each with a full-width "Enquire now" bar as its own footer. Proof as a
testimonial with the client's own logo, town and year.

C — Quiet

Design a homepage for DCS (as above). Aesthetic: expensive without loud. Greyscale
plus a single desaturated accent, enormous white space, one strong photograph doing all
the visual work. Services as a two-column typographic table, no cards or icons. Feature
panels use a tinted canvas holding an abstract UI mock — flat tint, grey skeleton bars,
exactly one element in full colour — with the heading and copy sitting outside and below
the panel on the page ground, no container. Pricing as a vertical selector list driving
one detail panel. A status cluster in the nav: location, real local time, availability.
Motion limited to an accordion for services and a scroll-spy rail for the process.

Append to every direction

Build in Next.js + Tailwind using theme tokens only — no hardcoded hex. All content
renders at full opacity by default; animate transform from a visible state, never gate
opacity above the fold, latch reveals with unobserve, and wrap motion in
prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference. Ship no count-up animations — author every figure
statically. Design the 390px layout explicitly rather than reflowing the desktop one;
pricing must be a vertical selector, not columns. The page must be a complete, readable
document with JavaScript disabled.
Provenance

What this rests on, and what it doesn't

  • Round one — gallery. 6 agents, ~450 tiles skimmed, 78 sites opened, 18 shortlisted, 4 measured at 390px. Six findings files in the session folder.
  • Round two — templates. 5 agents, 159 templates listed, 85 demos opened, 27 shortlisted, 12 count-ups photographed. Five findings files.
  • ~60 Featured-shelf templates were never opened — Trending-ordered, so they're the ones Framer itself promotes. The obvious next sweep; slugs are listed in the batch-E file.
  • No mobile pass in round two, and no hover or mobile-menu pass in either. Every mobile claim not marked "verified" is inference from construction.
  • One findings file is a summary only — the landing-page agent's detail was lost to a blocked write and only its returned summary survives.
  • Demo URLs are unreliable — Framer serves across three hostnames and about a third of slugs don't match. One template was wrongly reported dead. Treat any "Site Not Found" as unverified.
