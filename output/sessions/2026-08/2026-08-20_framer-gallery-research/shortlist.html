Framer Component Sweep

Six agents swept the Framer community gallery for component craft that survives a small business site — and for layouts built for a phone rather than reflowed onto one.

6 SEGMENTS ~450 TILES SKIMMED 78 SITES OPENED 18 SHORTLISTED 4 MEASURED AT 390PX

The one finding

Three agents, working separately in different categories, reverse-engineered the same construction for the in-card UI mock — the thing your prototypes don't have. It is not an illustration budget. It is a rule.

A flat tinted canvas. Grey skeleton bars for the text that doesn't matter. Exactly one element in full colour — the thing the caption is about.

Never white, never a real screenshot, never more than one saturated element per card. It is roughly twenty lines of CSS, it costs nothing to maintain, and it never dates the way a product screenshot does.

The corollary matters as much: real screenshots are reserved for full-bleed slabs, never put inside a card. I confirmed why at 390px — on Billow's phone layout the abstract skeleton mock stays perfectly legible while the real screenshot directly above it collapses into a grey smudge. The recipe isn't only an aesthetic choice; it is what makes a card survive a phone.

A fork you should settle first

The sweep did not converge on one answer. Two coherent positions emerged independently, and they lead to different homepages. Worth deciding before anyone builds anything.

POSITION A — SOFT SURFACE

Better cards

Rounded 20–24px, soft shadow, off-white ground, white cards, a mock inside each one. This is the default-studio.com world and most of the SaaS segment.

Argument: familiar, warm, forgiving of mixed content quality, and the mock recipe above gives every card something to hold.

POSITION B — FLAT / ANTI-CARD

Fewer cards

Zero radius, no shadow, flat colour blocks or no container at all — captions sitting directly on the page ground, sections divided by hairlines instead of boxes.

Argument: four separate agents landed here without prompting. A flat card is often worse than no card, so "not elevated" may be solved by removing container chrome rather than decorating it.

Position B's advocates: Poch Studio (vertical hairlines replace service cards), Numenu (square corners, no shadow, still reads expensive), Trowel Craft (flat zero-radius colour blocks), Mimo and Planhat (mock on a tinted canvas, caption below on bare ground, no container).

The shortlist

Twelve, strongest first. A green badge means more than one agent surfaced it independently — the closest thing to an objective signal this exercise produced.

01

Trowel Craft

trowelcraft.com.au
3 agents Mobile verified Trades

A Melbourne family plaster-repair firm — almost exactly a DCS client — and the most component-confident thing in the entire sweep. Three agents found it separately from three different categories. It is also the clearest argument for Position B.

  • Service cards — flat chartreuse, zero radius, condensed title top, copy bottom, then a 1px rule and a full-width Enquire now bar as the card's own footer.
  • Nav — links split 2/2 around a black hexagonal seal that overhangs below the bar.
  • Contact — photo-upload dropzone: "send us a picture of the job".
  • Testimonial — full-bleed owner portrait, giant condensed quote, hand-drawn ellipse badge.
Reservations The hero headline is ~35% white over a busy photo with no scrim — it fails contrast on desktop and is genuinely illegible at 390px, which I confirmed. Its standards carousel wheel-jacks the scroll and never releases. Its stat row is an animated count-up showing wrong figures mid-animation.
02

Billow

billow.so
3 agents Mobile verified

The cleanest single example of the in-card mock recipe, and it sells to service businesses rather than engineers. This is the reference to hand a developer.

  • Getting-started cards — skeleton bars with one bar tinted blue, plus one full-colour element floating over it: an AI Import pill, a 2 min badge, a £14,000 tooltip.
  • Persona pill tabs — outlined, active gets a coloured border and text.
  • Eyebrow chips with a leading icon labelling every section, giving the page rhythm.
  • FAQ accordion with a tinted square plus-button, and honest "Soon / On roadmap" chips.
Reservations Heavy display serif plus soft gradient blobs — a look, not a neutral system, and the blobs will cost paint performance on cheap Android. Nearly everything is centred, which flattens hierarchy. Its count-up is the false-figure trap.
03

Onmoon

onmoon.fr
Mobile verified Best pricing

The answer to "pricing dies on mobile", and the one component I was able to prove rather than infer. At 390px it is three stacked rows that work perfectly — no columns to squeeze, no horizontal scroll, no table.

  • Pricing as a vertical selector — a list of plan rows driving one detail panel. Active row tinted with a filled black tick; inactive rows carry an outlined circular arrow. "Best seller" sits inline as a small pill.
  • Persistent circular call button pinned bottom-right — the right CTA for a trade.
  • Scroll-linked method rail that fills with colour as you pass week chips.
Reservations Its booking widget uses a fake-scarcity countdown. That's a dark pattern — take the pricing selector, leave the timer.
04

Luna UI

lunaui.co
Same business as DCS

Sells websites on a monthly retainer — the same offer DCS sells. Worth studying for how it structures a retainer commercially, not just visually.

  • Pricing without tiers — one retainer card (with a "cancel anytime · 7-day notice" micro-line) plus one wide card whose right half is a "common projects" from-price list.
  • Before/after slider as a draggable card inside the services bento.
  • Workspace mock — layer tree, breakpoint bar, property panel, two labelled multiplayer cursors.
  • Testimonial tied to the actual project, with a ←/→ counter.
Reservations Black starfield ground with low-contrast body copy. Take the component structure, invert the palette.
05

Mimo

mimohq.com
UK buyerPosition B

Sells to UK professional-services firms — roughly your buyer — and solves the "card that isn't icon + heading + paragraph" problem with the least engineering of anything found.

  • No card at all — a tinted canvas with a faint dotted grid holds one floating white fragment; heading, description and a Explore → link sit outside and below it on the page ground.
  • The fragments are just lists — a task checklist, a category list, three payable rows each with a small action button and a role chip. Each maps onto a DCS story.
  • Announcement bar — dark full-bleed strip above the nav, one line, dismissible.
  • CTA slab in a single deep colour with a tonal button rather than white.
Reservations The hero lifestyle photo does no work, and there's a lot of dead vertical space under the captions at 1440px.
06

Midu Studio

midu.design
Closest structural twin

A studio selling retainers and projects — the same two-shaped offer as DCS — with the pricing composition to match.

  • Three-tier composition — two plan cards with a 3D keycap breaking the top-right corner, then two full-width reassurance strips outside the cards, then a separate horizontal project card at a "from" price with 50/50 terms.
  • Availability counter in the nav — "1/5 slots for July" always on screen.
  • Service cards using a 1px rule and a 12px Info micro-label above the paragraph, deliberately offset column to column.
07

Bureau Dimanche

bureaudimanche.com
Solo studio · SMEs

A one-person studio selling to small businesses. Its pricing does something none of the others do — it leads with the customer's words instead of yours.

  • Objection-led pricing — each tier opens with the client's own quoted objection ("we need a brand identity") rather than a feature blurb.
  • Featured plan raised with a 1.5px border, not inverted to dark — a quieter way to signal the recommended option.
  • Non-repeating tiers — tiers 2 and 3 open with "everything in 1/2", so nothing repeats.
  • Bottom-centre floating contact pill with circular mail and calendar buttons; doubles as the mobile CTA.
08

Delphi

delphi.ai
Widest component set

Five genuinely different card shapes that still read as one system, and warm rather than cold — the best evidence that a component system can have range without fragmenting.

  • Pricing on a staircase — each tier's top edge sits ~30px lower than the one left of it; full-width pill CTA under the price, above the feature list; only one tier gets a filled button.
  • A bespoke icon per feature row instead of repeated ticks — the single cheapest way to kill the templated-checklist feel.
  • Persona tab switcher as plain text, active in the accent — no pill, no underline.
  • Numbered steps with the numeral inline on the title's baseline, not floating above it.
Reservations The display serif reads "wellness brand" unless paired with much plainer body copy, and the two-clause headline mannerism repeats in every section.

Rise from Within

  • Symptom checklist — white one-line pill cards flanking a central image, each naming a problem the visitor recognises in themselves.

Reiseservice Jeremias

A one-woman travel consultancy in a small German town — the closest thing in the sweep to an actual DCS client doing everything right. Warm cream and serif, a deliberate counterpoint to the tech look.

  • The price is the button — service cards make the from-price a full-width pill CTA.

Ballpark

The mobile pass

You said mobile was the worse of the two problems, so it got its own pass rather than a glance. A note on method, because it changes how much weight these carry: only these four were measured. Every other mobile remark in this document is a structural inference by an agent that never saw a phone viewport.

Trowel Craft
Stacks cleanly

Service cards become full-width blocks and each keeps its own Enquire now footer bar, so every service stays a thumb-reachable conversion point. But the hero headline is illegible over the photo — worse on the phone than on desktop.

Onmoon
Best in class

Pricing survives intact because it was never columns. Three stacked selector rows, active one tinted with a filled tick, "Best seller" inline, plus a persistent circular call button. This is the pattern to copy.

Billow
Proves the recipe

The abstract skeleton mock stays legible at 390px while the real product screenshot above it becomes a grey smudge. Direct evidence for "abstract mock in cards, screenshots only full-bleed".

All Framer sites
Reveal gating is the risk

Scroll-reveal animations don't fire on programmatic scroll — pages render as blank white viewports. Whatever we take, take the layout and not the animation policy.

Traps found

Things worth not copying, several of which confirm problems already in your notes.

Animated count-ups publishing false figures

Found on Billow, Lumenda, Trowel Craft, ReadyToLaunch and Resolve Healthware — the last two ship frozen counters permanently reading "0+ years of design experience". This is the exact trap already in your project memory. If we use a count-up, the true value must be the authored markup and the animation purely decorative.

Scroll-reveal gating that blanks the page

The single most common flaw across all six sweeps. Litefirm and Planhat render as blank white until each block scrolls in. On a slow connection or a failed script this is a white page, and it is a plausible part of why "mobile was dreadful" on the prototypes too.

Wheel-jacking and fake scarcity

Trowel Craft's standards carousel captures the scroll and never releases it. Onmoon's booking widget runs a fake countdown timer. Both are worth naming so they aren't absorbed along with the good parts.

What this didn't cover

  • Coverage is a sample, not a census. The gallery holds ~12,000 sites behind a virtualised infinite scroll. We skimmed roughly 450 tiles and opened 78. Treat this as a good sample, not the best of the gallery.
  • The Awards tab doesn't exist. It renders as a disabled "coming soon" button and its URL 404s — I sent an agent after something that isn't there. Framer also offers only Trending and Newest sorts, with no most-liked option, and Featured caps at 12 per sort.
  • Only four sites were measured at 390px. Everything else labelled "mobile" is inference from construction.
  • Hover, cursor and menu behaviour is largely unassessed. No mobile menus were opened and few accordions exercised — a real hole given that behaviour was half the original brief.
  • No footer worth stealing was found in the SaaS segment, and no pricing components exist at all in the portfolio segment.
  • One agent's detailed findings were lost to a blocked file write and only its summary survives; the Landing Page file is thinner than the other five. Two domains were blocked by browser permissions, and several promising sites — notably flighty.com, likely the best mobile craft in the SaaS pool — were never opened.
