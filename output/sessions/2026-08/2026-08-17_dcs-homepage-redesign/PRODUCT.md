## What it is

Digital Consulting Services (DCS) designs, builds, hosts and manages websites for small
businesses of any kind. Founded 2019, based in Polegate, East Sussex, working UK-wide. Run
by one person: Ricky Wilson.

## Who it is for

**Small owner-run businesses across every sector** — usually 1–20 people, where the owner
makes the decision and pays the invoice. Retail and eCommerce; studios and practitioners
(yoga, tuition, therapy, coaching, salons); professional and property services; creative and
B2B; and trades and contractors.

Trades are a large and valued part of the client base, **not the definition of it**. The
real portfolio spans fabrics, made-to-order goods, tuition, yoga, signage, automotive,
property and scaffolding. Positioning that leads with trades understates the business and
narrows the audience.

They are time-poor — running the business is the job. Not technical, and have no wish to
become technical. They have no site, or one they are quietly embarrassed by, and have been
putting it off for years. Price-conscious but will pay properly for something that works
and is looked after. They want to look as credible online as they know they are in person.

## The core truth

**Work that competes with London and New York agencies, for a fraction of the cost and a
tiny fraction of the client's effort.**

All three parts matter. The work is genuinely comparable to a big-city studio's — not "good
for a small operation". The price is a fraction. And the client does almost nothing.

The site itself is the proof of the first claim. A page that genuinely looks like it came
out of a London studio _is_ the argument; an ordinary-looking page makes the claim
unsupportable however well written. This is why design ambition here is strategic, not
decorative.

Two constraints on expressing it: never invent a competitor's prices to dramatise the
comparison (no sourced figure exists, and a fabricated one is a false claim), and never lead
with the smallness — being small is what makes it personal and affordable, not an excuse
for the work.

Most agencies expect a client to arrive with a brief, copy, images and a clear idea of what
they want. DCS does not. It asks the right questions, writes the content, makes the design
decisions, builds it, hosts it, and keeps it running.

Differentiators, strongest first:

1. You're hiring a person, not a company. Nothing outsourced, nothing offshored.
2. Agency-quality output — professional design, well-written copy, fast and secure builds.
3. Low effort for the client; everything extracted through conversation, not a brief.
4. Content handled. Clients don't need to know what to say.
5. No CMS, no login, no dashboard to learn.
6. All-in pricing covering hosting, security, updates and support.
7. A managed service and an ongoing relationship, not a hand-off.

## Voice

Plain-spoken, no jargon, no agency-speak. Confident, not apologetic. Personal — "I" is often
better than "we". Reassuring, honest, warm but composed.

**Elevated, not matey.** Closer to a good architecture or brand studio than to a contractor.
Assured, quiet, precise — the reader should feel they are dealing with someone whose taste
they can borrow.

Never: "synergy", "bespoke solutions", "leveraging", "transform your business", "in today's
digital landscape". Avoid trade slang as decoration. Never mention the tech stack.

## The surface being designed

The DCS homepage. **Persuade** mode: the studio's own shop window, where the visitor decides
and acts and the design _is_ the product. If it looks ordinary, nothing else about the pitch
survives.

## Visual constraint (hard)

The target is an elevated design studio. **Ruled out:** hi-vis yellow and black, hazard
tape, chevrons, stencil type, and every under-construction signifier; site-notice, job-sheet,
docket, work-order, scaffold-board and road-sign metaphors; anything reading as a building
site, a merchant's counter, or industrial safety signage. Round 1 produced six of these and
they are not wanted.

Aim instead at restraint, generous space, real typographic craft, a confident colour
position, considered motion, detail that rewards a second look. Bold and saturated colour is
welcome; industrial safety palettes and construction signifiers are not.
