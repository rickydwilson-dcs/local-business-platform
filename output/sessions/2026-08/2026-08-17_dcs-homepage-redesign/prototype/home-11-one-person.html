§ 01 — The opening

Why the usual way of buying a website never quite happens.

You’ve probably had one of these offers before. Someone will build you a website, then sends over a twelve page brief and asks for your logo files, your photos, and a rough idea of what each page should say. That’s the bit that kills it. You’re up a ladder, or under a van, or halfway through a rewire, and the homework sits in the inbox for eight months.

So I do it the other way round. We have a conversation, twenty minutes on the phone is usually enough, and I take it from there. I ask the questions. I write the words. I choose the colours and the pictures. I build it, put it live, host it, keep it patched, and I answer the phone when something needs changing.

There’s no login for you to learn and no password for you to forget.

The work itself is proper agency work. Scroll down and judge it for yourself, because that is the only fair test. The part that’s different is that there’s one name on it and it’s mine. Nothing gets passed to a junior, nothing gets sent offshore, and you never get handed to an account manager who wasn’t on the first call.

Sat here in Polegate, I know most of the trades I build for by name. That’s not a business model I chose to sound friendly. It’s just what happens when one person does the work.
