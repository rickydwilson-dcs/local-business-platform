Five ways to fix
the phone.

Direction 52 is settled on the desktop — every section fits one viewport, the colour and type decisions are made. The phone was never designed. It inherited the desktop grid collapsed to one column, which is why it reads as 16,077 pixels of identical boxes.

These are five different arguments about what to do about that. Each one changes the phone only. Below 861px the layout is rebuilt; at 861px and above every one of them is byte-for-byte the page you already approved — measured, not assumed, at six desktop sizes.

What each one argues

M1

Swipe

Doctrine · card decks for peer sets

Nine work tiles read one per row is 2.6 screens of identical rectangles, and the reader loses that it was ever a set. A rail keeps the set as one object and puts a counter on it, so you know there are nine without scrolling to find out.

What measurement changed here. The first version made everything a rail, including the claim cards and the testimonials. 16px DM Mono is ~9.6px a character, so a 390px phone tops out near 36 characters even full width — any card narrower than the screen falls under the 35-character floor. Rails are now reserved for sets whose cards carry short text; anything with a paragraph in it stays full measure.

  • Rails: work tiles, sectors, price tiers — label-and-a-line content
  • Stacked: claims, testimonials, services — paragraph content
  • Progress rule and an n / 9 readout under every rail
  • The recommended price tier leads its rail
The tradeHorizontal scroll inside a vertical page is a pattern some people never discover, and ui-ux-pro-max flags it under gesture-conflicts. The counter, the peek and scroll-snap-stop pay for it — but it is the thing to judge on a real handset.

M2

Screens

Doctrine · one idea per screen

No horizontal regions at all. Every long set is cut into pages sized to the visible band — the viewport minus the top bar minus the bottom action bar, 718px — and each page is centred and snapped. The section heading is pulled into the top of its own first page rather than costing a screen of its own.

Page one of a chapter carries fewer items than the pages after it, because it is also carrying the heading. Those counts come from measurement: at 16px body they had to come down from the 15px version.

  • 23 pages, every one measured against the 718px band
  • Snap set to proximity, not mandatory — it guides, it does not trap
  • A 2 of 3 marker so a split chapter reads as one
  • nth-child colour systems frozen before splitting, or the palette resets per page
The tradeThe longest pass at 21,357px — a third more than the baseline. It does not make the page shorter; it makes every stopping point deliberate. Worth it only if you believe fitted screens beat fewer of them.

M3

Dense

Doctrine · progressive disclosure + a tight rhythm

This pass was rebuilt, and its name changed. The first version got its height by shrinking type to 11–14px and putting prose into two-up columns. That is the readable-font-size and line-length-control violation the audit flagged, and it is the single biggest reason it read as cheap.

Type is not a compression device. The compression now comes from three legitimate places instead: the tight end of the 8pt scale rather than the loose end, six services folded into an accordion, and shorter media crops — which cost no words at all.

  • Services become a disclosure list — six titles on one screen, copy one tap away
  • Claim index becomes an inline prefix instead of its own stacked row
  • Spacing drops from the 24/32 tier to the 8/16 tier, same scale
  • Every sentence stays at 16px and at full measure
The tradeOnly −8% now, against −28% for the version that broke the type rules. That is the honest price of the fix: most of the old saving was borrowed from legibility.

M4

Editorial

Doctrine · the printed spread

This pass does not try to make the page shorter. It argues the real problem is not length but rhythm: every element the same width, every gap the same size, so nothing signals what matters. A magazine is also long; it does not read long, because it alternates.

Measurement changed this one's mind too. Its original argument was that the type ramp should go up. It cannot: an 18px mono body in the 350px column a 390px phone allows yields 32 characters, under the floor. 16px yields 36, which is the most this face can give on this screen. So the generosity now comes from space, leading and full-bleed media — not from size.

  • Every image and colour field breaks the gutter and runs edge to edge
  • Sector captions alternate left and right down the column
  • Claim numerals ghosted at display scale into the field behind them
  • Leading at 1.65 and the 32/48 spacing tier carry the reading feel
The trade18,752px, 17% longer than the baseline it replaces. Everything about it bets that a phone visitor who is interested will read, and one who is not was leaving anyway.

M5

Bento

Doctrine · pick and reveal

M1 spends the horizontal axis, M2 the vertical, M3 density. This one spends interaction. Where a set is a list of options the reader only wants one of at a time — six sectors, two ways to pay — it becomes a picker, and the set costs the height of one item instead of six.

The mosaic half did not survive its own rule. Two-up work tiles were kept on the grounds that a label and a name fit a half column. They do — but the descriptive line under them measured 16 characters, less than half the floor, and it was the worst measure anywhere in these five. Cutting the line is not an option, so the mosaic went and the pass now stands on the pickers, which were the part that worked.

  • Six sectors become a tab strip — arrow keys, roving tabindex, real tabpanels
  • Pricing becomes an Option A / Option B toggle over one table
  • Tab targets raised to 44px; labels read out of the existing markup
  • Everything with a paragraph in it takes the full measure
The tradeThe honest one: the six sectors are no longer all visible at once, and a visitor who does not tap sees one. The strip names all six without opening any, which is the mitigation, but it is a real reduction in what a passive scroll shows.

The foundation all five share

Four defects on a real phone

No safe-area handling anywhere. The viewport meta had no viewport-fit=cover, so env(safe-area-inset-*) resolved to zero and the fixed bottom action bar sat under the iPhone home indicator — both its controls partly untappable on every notched device. Invisible in an emulator, which is why it survived this long.

24 hover rules, no hover:hover query. On touch, hover fires on tap and then latches until you tap elsewhere: the work tiles invert and stay inverted.

Every browser surface was default — selection, caret, scrollbar, focus ring, underline offset. They belong to no design system and are the cheapest tell that a page was assembled rather than built. All now themed from the palette.

Type and touch. 15px body against a 16px floor; targets under 44px; no touch-action, so every tap on the page paid roughly 300ms.

Two patterns the craft floor bans

The eyebrow above a heading. Eleven of them, plus the hero's. On a 390px screen the hero eyebrow measured 88px tall against an 87px headline — a label outweighing the thing it introduces. The heading carries its own weight.

Section numbers where the sequence carries nothing. The four process steps are a real sequence and keep theirs. The three claims, six services and four "you skip" items are unordered, and their numerals were furniture.

Both still ship on desktop, where the heading rail gives them room to work. This is a mobile composition decision, not a content change — no sentence, figure or caption was removed anywhere.

The hero was rebuilt. Its display type is now sized from the longest unbreakable word: at 13.5vw PROFESSIONAL measured 400px inside a 350px column, and overflow-x:clip was silently cutting it. One primary action; the call button became a link because it duplicated the bar that carries the number permanently.

What the measurements decided

16px DM Mono runs ~9.6px a character, so a 390px phone tops out near 36 characters even full width. Three consequences, applied rather than argued around: two-up text columns are impossible (they give ~17); rails only work for cards carrying short text; and M4's original "bigger type" thesis is dead — 18px yields 32 characters, under the floor.

  1. Desktop drift zero at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1440×810, 1366×768, 1366×690 and 1280×720.
  2. Detector: 51 findings → 22–27, all warnings. flat-type-hierarchy eliminated in all five; cramped-padding 35 → 11–15. What remains is inherited desktop design.
  3. No faux bold; the mobile nav is untouched and still opens full-viewport; no horizontal overflow at 320–860.

Still open: 1366×768 overflows on the desktop baseline, before any of this. All five inherit it identically.
