The arrangement

You'll spend about an hour on this. I'll spend the rest of it.

That isn't a slogan, it's how the work is actually divided. The reason people put a website off for years is that every studio they ring hands them a homework assignment. This one doesn't.

Your side

  • One conversation: a call, or a few messages, whichever suits.
  • A look at the drafts, and any changes you want.

My side

  • The questions worth asking about how your business actually works.
  • Every word on the site, written for you.
  • Structure, imagery, colour, type. All the decisions.
  • The build, and the search setup that goes with it.
  • Hosting, SSL, the domain, security updates, uptime monitoring.
  • Someone to ring when you want something changed.

Who this is for

Range is the point.

Twenty-plus sites since 2019, and no two of these businesses have anything in common except their size and their owner's diary. Move through them.

Retail & eCommerce

Shops with stock to sell, whether or not there's a till involved. The site has to load fast on a phone, take money without drama, and be something you can add to yourself, or ring me about, which most people prefer.

  • Product pages that don't need a photographer on retainer
  • Checkout, delivery rules and stock that stay out of your way
  • Found for the things people actually search for

Studios & practitioners

Yoga, tuition, therapy, coaching, salons. Almost always one person whose diary is the business. The site's job is to fill it without adding admin: people book and pay before they ever ring you.

  • Booking and payment handled on the site
  • Timetables that are easy to change and hard to get wrong
  • Enough warmth that a nervous first-timer gets in touch

Professional & property services

Consultancies, lettings, luxury property. Here the site is the credibility check that happens before anyone picks up the phone, so it has to be as composed as you are in the room.

  • A tone that reads senior without reading stiff
  • Case work and credentials presented properly
  • Enquiry forms that qualify before they land in your inbox

Makers & made-to-order

Fabric, furniture, signage, print. Anything where the customer chooses the specification and you build it after they've paid. The interesting problem is usually the ordering, not the website.

  • Configurators that price a made-to-order item correctly
  • Orders that arrive as a work sheet you can actually use
  • Shipping that works beyond the UK when it needs to

Hospitality & food

Cafés, kitchens, small venues. Nine in ten visits are somebody standing outside on their phone deciding whether to come in. Everything else on the site is secondary to that moment.

  • Menu, hours and location legible in three seconds
  • A map link that opens the right app on the right phone
  • Photographs that survive being seen in sunlight

Trades & contractors

Electricians, scaffolders, builders, automotive, removals. A large and valued part of the work, and one sector on this list, not the whole list. The site earns its keep by ranking in the towns you cover.

  • A page for every service, in every town you work
  • Accreditations and insurance where people look for them
  • Ringing you takes one tap, on any page

How it goes

Four steps, and you're only needed for two of them.

No brief to write, no forms to fill in, no login to remember afterwards. Numbered because the order genuinely matters — nothing starts until the conversation has happened.

  1. 01 A conversation A call, or a few messages. No brief needed and nothing to fill in. I'll ask the questions, and they'll mostly be about how the business works rather than about websites.
  2. 02 We write it and design it Copy, structure, imagery, colour: all handled here. You don't need to know what to say, which is the part that stops most people from ever starting.
  3. 03 You review Unlimited revisions during the build until you're happy. Say "the green's wrong" and that's a perfectly good piece of feedback. You don't have to know why.
  4. 04 It goes live, and we keep it running Hosting, updates, monitoring and support from then on. This is a managed service, not a hand-off. You won't be emailed a password and wished luck.
