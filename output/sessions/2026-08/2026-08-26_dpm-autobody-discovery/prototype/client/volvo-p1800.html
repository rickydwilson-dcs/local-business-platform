02 The owner's specification

What the owner
asked for

Five changes, exactly as the owner listed them. A concours restoration is not always a return to standard — more often it is a considered argument with the factory, and the argument belongs in the record.

  1. i Candy Red colour A translucent colour laid over a metallic base. It reads black in shadow and lifts to a deep red where the sun catches it, so the car is never quite the same colour twice.
  2. ii Wire wheel conversion Splined hubs in place of the factory bolt pattern, and a chrome wire wheel at each corner.
  3. iii Power steering conversion The one change on this list the owner feels rather than sees.
  4. iv Smoothed chrome bumpers Over-riders and their fixings removed, the shells filled, levelled and re-plated. Chrome shows every thousandth you leave behind.
  5. v Lowered Ride height set down from standard, which resets every panel gap and arch line you have just spent months getting right.

Four entries,

Nobody writes about a job they did not stand in front of, and the difficult weeks stay in — a restoration story with no problem in it is an advertisement.

Stage 09 — Out in the daylight

We photographed it more than we painted it

D Dave — paint and finish

Over 2,000 photographs went into the file for this one car. Not for the website. For the owner, and for whoever has it in forty years and wants to know what is underneath the paint before they cut into it.

People assume the photographs are the marketing and the car is the work. It is the other way round. The photographs are the only part of the job that survives after the next owner has had it repainted by somebody else.

I walked round it twice before it went up the ramps onto the transporter. That is not for the owner's benefit, it is for mine. Once it has gone I cannot do anything about what I have missed.

Stage 07 — Colour, decided in daylight

Candy is the one you cannot go back into

P Paul — colour, mixing, polish

A candy is a see-through colour over a metallic ground. The depth you are looking at is not pigment, it is distance — light going down through the colour, off the base, and back out at you.

Which means the colour is built by how much you put on, not by what is in the tin. Two extra passes on one wing and that wing is a shade darker than the one next to it, for ever. You cannot blend into a candy and you cannot spot-repair one. If you get it wrong on a door you do the whole side again.

So it is done in one go, same gun, same pressure, same distance, same speed of arm, every panel, and nobody talks to you while you are doing it.

Stage 04 — Metal first

The bumper came back from the platers with a shadow in it

E Ellis — metalwork and prep

Smoothing a bumper is mostly making holes disappear. The over-rider fixings come out, the slots get filled, and then the shell has to be levelled by hand until there is nothing left to find.

Chrome does not hide work. It magnifies it. Paint sits on a panel and fills a little; plating goes on in microns and follows every single thing underneath it, and then polishes it up so it catches the light.

The first one came back from the platers looking right in the workshop and wrong in the doorway. Held up to daylight there was a soft dark band about four inches long across the face of it, where I had taken a whisker too much out and left a hollow you could not feel with a thumb. Nothing measured it. The sky measured it.

Stripped, filled again, levelled with a long file rather than a block this time, and back to the platers. Three weeks. I would rather find it in the doorway than have the owner find it on a lawn at a show. It was not a good fortnight and I am not going to write it up as one.

Stage 05 — Straight before shiny

Nothing over looked. Nothing left untouched.

D Dave — paint and finish

That is the line we already write under our own photographs, and it is not a boast, it is a working rule. It means that if a panel is off the car anyway, it gets done properly, even when nobody is ever going to look at it.

Thirteen hundred hours is not one heroic month. It is a year of afternoons where the visible progress is that a gap is now the same at the top as it is at the bottom.

People ask what takes the time. The honest answer is that the last five per cent of a car takes about a third of it, and there is no way of buying your way past that bit.

08 The paint on this car

Candy Red,
and what that actually means

A specification for one car, not a chart of swatches. Candy is the reason this car photographs the way it does, and the reason it could not be repaired if anybody ever kerbs it.

Black in shadow, deep red in sun. That is the whole of what a candy does, and it is why the car looks like two different colours in the photographs above.
