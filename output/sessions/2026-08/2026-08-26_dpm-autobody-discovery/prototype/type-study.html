Ground

01 The question

The prototype sets every headline in Archivo, a grotesque. The instinct is that a shop restoring concours cars wants a serif — and the counter-instinct is that DPM’s own mark is a wide geometric sans, so a serif headline sitting directly under it may read as two identities rather than one.

Every specimen below is real copy at the exact sizes the prototype uses. Pick a pairing, then scroll: the section that matters most is 03, where the mark and the headline share a frame.

03 The mark and the headline, in one frame

Concours restoration  ·  Berwick, East Sussex

Artists of Automotive Restoration

Not a slogan. It is engraved on the plaque we rivet into every car before it leaves.

What to look for. The mark’s M is a sharp unbracketed vee and its D is a perfect half-circle — geometry with no calligraphic memory in it at all. A headline face with strong stress or bracketed serifs argues with that; one with restrained, flat serifs sits under it. Judge the pair at this size, not at specimen size.

04 The mark, large — where a traced outline shows

This mark is traced, not drawn. DPM hold no vector artwork; this is tools/trace-logo.mjs’s trace of the 1955×849 PNG on their own site, and its curves are short straight segments rather than Béziers. It measures 1.9% different from the source raster, which is invisible on a screen at any size and wrong to hand to a signwriter. Getting real artwork is still an open item.

05 A section head, at working size

Lot 02  ·  Finished and delivered

Bentley S3
Continental

A Continental gives you the longest unbroken panels we ever paint, and near-black gives you nowhere to put a mistake. There is a chrome waistrail that runs the whole length of the car, and either it is straight along every inch of the door and the wing or it is not.

Look at the shut line as the light crosses it. The gap is even top to bottom, and the reflection carries across it without stepping. That is the part that takes the months; the colour on top of it takes days.

07 Numerals — the comma test

1,300Hours of labour
2,000Photographs
57,000Views
26282Chassis
37:32Film
£1,995A price, for the test

Read the commas, not the digits. A face with tabular figures — or any monospaced face — gives the comma a full digit advance, so £1,995 sets as £1 , 995. It is invisible in the markup and obvious on screen, and it corrupts a price. This page and the prototype both declare font-variant-numeric: normal explicitly for that reason. If a candidate shows a gap above, it is disqualified for headline figures whatever else it does well.

A view, for what it is worth. The three faces that hold up
      against the mark are Instrument Serif, which is tight and
      modern and has almost no calligraphic stress to argue with the geometry;
      Libre Caslon Display, which is English and old and reads as
      provenance rather than fashion; and Newsreader, which is
      already carrying the body copy, so choosing it makes the site one family and removes the
      clash question entirely. The Didones — Playfair, Bodoni — look expensive on
      their own and fight the mark badly at 04.

    
The counter-argument for keeping Archivo. The current
      pairing is a grotesque headline over a serif body, which is the arrangement all three
      reference sites use, and it matches the mark exactly because the mark is a geometric sans.
      The serif is already doing the elegant work in the lede and the prose. Switching the
      headline to a serif makes the page more obviously “luxury” and less obviously
      DPM.
