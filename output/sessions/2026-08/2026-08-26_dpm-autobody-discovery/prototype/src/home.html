Volvo P1800
Candy Red

No. 02  ·  Finished and delivered

Bentley S3
Continental

A Continental gives you the longest unbroken panels we ever paint, and near-black gives you nowhere to put a mistake. There is a chrome waistrail that runs the whole length of the car, and either it is straight along every inch of the door and the wing or it is not.

Look at the shut line as the light crosses it. The gap is even top to bottom, and the reflection carries across it without stepping. All of that is settled in bare metal, long before there is any colour to look at.

A second S3 is on the jig now.

The full record of the Bentley

Real work, real photograph The 1963 Bentley S3 Continental is DPM’s own restoration — there is a sixteen-minute film of it on DPM TV, and a teaser cut. All three plates below are crops of the single 4032×3024 photograph DPM publish on their own site, which is why the light matches across them. No hours, chassis number or coat count are claimed here, because DPM have never published any for this car.

No. 03  ·  In the booth

Jaguar
Aston Martin Sea Green

The owner wanted a colour from another marque entirely. Aston Martin Sea Green goes down looking almost grey under the booth lamps, and only turns green when it is dry and the light starts moving across it.

Mixed here, sprayed here, judged here, under the same lamps every time. The strip light overhead runs as one unbroken highlight from the front wing to the door, and that unbroken run is the only proof there is that a panel underneath is straight.

This car is still in our hands, so its record stops at the paint. We would rather show you the surface as it is than wait until there is a finished photograph to show you.

The Workshop and Contact sections moved to their own pages on 3 September — workshop.html and contact.html, off this scroll, at Ricky’s call. The workshop page also carries DPM’s own “NEW WORKSHOP OF DREAMS!” film and a still pulled from it.

The mark is now real artwork, not a trace. Until 6 September the wordmark was a polygonal trace of a 1955×849 PNG off DPM’s own site, a single-colour silhouette that recoloured per surface (bone in the header, bone-dim in the footer). Ricky supplied a proper vector (Pixelmator export, two colours: #67635f warm grey for “DPM AUTOBODY,” #c5cbca cool grey for the “Artists of Automotive Restoration” line and the swash) and asked for those colours used exactly as supplied, not simplified. Contrast note, not a colour judgement: against the masthead’s near-black gradient the warm-grey “DPM AUTOBODY” line measures roughly 3.3:1, which clears the 3:1 minimum for graphical objects but not the 4.5:1 minimum for text — worth knowing since the mark now carries fixed colour rather than the header’s ink variable, so it no longer lightens on hover the way the old trace did. See the type study for how the wordmark sits against the headline faces under consideration.
