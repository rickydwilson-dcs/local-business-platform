01Jaguar XK150 S Roadster  ·  1959

Eleven gaps, re-cut by hand.

01

The body came off a rotisserie straight and came back to us anything but. Every shut line on this car was opened, leaded and re-cut to a gap we set ourselves — tighter than Browns Lane ever managed — before a drop of colour went near it.

Colour is the original BC.6, mixed in-house against a door-shut sample that had never seen sun. Ten coats, flatted four times, polished by hand over three days.

02Austin-Healey Sprite Mk II  ·  1962

Flake laid so it only shows in low sun.

02

A small car that had been painted four times before it reached us, each coat hiding the one below. We took it back to bare steel and started again.

The owner wanted the factory Tartan Red but with the depth a modern clear allows. Colour over a ground coat, flake in the mid, six coats of clear. In flat light it reads as a solid. At six in the morning it does something else.

What we do

Paint is the last thing done to a car and the first thing anyone sees.

Most restoration shops treat paint as the finish. We treat it as the reason. Everything that happens before it — the metal, the lead, the gaps, the primer, the flatting — exists so that the surface has somewhere true to sit.

It is why other restorers send their cars here to be sprayed, including one of the best-known names in the country.

Everything is hand-crafted in house. Panel work, lead loading, filler, primer, colour, flatting, polishing — all of it happens in Berwick, by the same four pairs of hands, on the same floor.

Two things go out: engines go to an engine builder, trim goes to a trimmer. We say so plainly because everyone else's "full in-house restoration" means the same thing and does not admit it.
