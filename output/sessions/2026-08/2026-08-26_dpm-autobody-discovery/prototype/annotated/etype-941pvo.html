02 Provenance

A forty-seven-year
promise

Brian Bray bought 941 PVO in 1978 for £410, with his nine-year-old son Matthew, as its third owner. It was already sixteen years old and needed a full restoration. The engine was rebuilt, the body stripped and sand-blasted — and then, as families and decades do, the project stalled. It sat dismantled in boxes for the best part of forty years.

By 2017 Brian had retired to a farm in Leicestershire and Matthew was forty-eight. Reluctantly, the family decided to sell, and the car went under the hammer at H&H Auctions, Duxford, on 29 March 2017. Mark Antwis was the fourth bidder and the winner.

He already had a Ferrari 308 and a self-restored Aston Martin V8 in the workshop; the E-type completed what he calls his “dreamed-of stable of classics”. His method, in his own words: “conserving and restoring rather than replacing”.

Sourced from the magazine The whole of this section is drawn from the E-Type Owners Club Magazine feature, pages 15–16 — the £410 purchase price, the auction date and venue, and the direct quote on his restoration method are all taken from the PDF, not paraphrased from memory. There is no photograph of the barn-find car or the auction available to this page: the only images of that stage of the story are print-magazine scans, not separate image files, so none are used here.

Two sources, one shown — 5 September Our own /metalwork page gives 440 hours for the panel work alone (before any primer); the magazine gives 450 hours for the whole job through to final polish. Both are real, but showing both figures side by side on a client-facing page read as the site arguing with itself over which number to believe, rather than as evidence. 450 is shown because it is the more complete measurement (matches home.html’s #proof row); 440 is not hidden, it is just here rather than on the page. Finish, as described on the owner’s own site “Primer application, additional flatting and levelling, followed by colour coat application in Opalescent Silver Blue and five full lacquer coats, completed with machine polishing” — paraphrased from etypefhcseries1.com/metalwork, not a direct quote, and no coat count is published for the colour coat itself, only the lacquer. Do not assert a colour-coat number this page has not seen.

The one thing to know before this goes anywhere

The owner has cleared the images. David’s 2026-09-05 email relayed Mark Antwis’s own words: the photographs and the magazine material are “free to use”.

He has not cleared the writing. Mark asked to approve the article once it is written, before anything built from it launches. Everything on this page — the headings, the framing, the sentence pairing his Aston Martin win to the E-type job — is DPM’s own first-draft writing, not his, and none of it has been shown to him yet. David still has to take this page (or a summary of what it says) back to Mark before it is shown to anyone outside this review.
