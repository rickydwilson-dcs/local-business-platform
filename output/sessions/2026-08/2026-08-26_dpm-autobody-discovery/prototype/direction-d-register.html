04 The workshop

What we do,
and what goes out

Bare-metal restoration and concours paint on classic and performance cars, in one building in Berwick, East Sussex. Panel work, lead loading, filler, primer, colour, flatting and polishing — all of it by the same hands, on the same floor.

A restoration here starts with the car photographed exactly as it arrived, and ends with a plaque riveted into the engine bay carrying its chassis number. Between those two things sits a file that the owner keeps: every panel, every gap, every decision, photographed as it happened.

Two things leave the building, and we would rather print that than let “full in-house restoration” do the work of not saying it. Everybody else's means the same thing and does not admit it.

  • Panel work, fabrication, weldingIn house
  • Lead loading and metal finishingIn house
  • Primer, block work, guide coatsIn house
  • Colour mixing and sprayingIn house
  • Flatting, cutting and polishingIn house
  • Photographic record of the buildIn house
  • Engine buildingOut of house
  • Trim and upholsteryOut of house

Please read — about the photography and the copy

The Volvo P1800 photographs are real. Every P1800 image on this page and on the record of Lot 01 is DPM Autobody's own photograph of DPM Autobody's own work, published by them. They are used here with that credit and are not licensed for a live public site until DPM — and, on a car this identifiable, its owner — confirm.

The P1800 figures are real. Candy Red, a year, 1,300 hours, the five specified changes, over 2,000 photographs and chassis 26282 all come from DPM's own published record and the plaque in the engine bay. Nothing has been added to them.

Plates marked “AI art direction” are synthetic. Lots 02 and 03, and every photograph in them, are invented for this prototype. They show the light, crop and register we would commission from a photographer. They are not DPM's work, not DPM's cars and not DPM's hands, and on a live site each is replaced one-for-one by a real shoot. Lot 03 deliberately has no finished-car frame at all: DPM own no photograph of a finished car in daylight away from the workshop, and inventing one here would have hidden the single clearest argument for commissioning a shoot.
