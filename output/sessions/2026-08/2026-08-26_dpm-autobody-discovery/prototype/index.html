DPM Autobody · Berwick, East Sussex · Prototypes, August 2026

Four directions

Four homepage prototypes. The first three are deliberately different arguments about what the business is; the fourth is the one Ricky asked for after reviewing them, and it is where the work now sits. D is now two pages — a homepage and the P1800 project record it was carrying.

Each is a working page — scroll it, resize it, use a keyboard on it.

  • D

    The Register

    Near-black · the documented car · scroll moves the light

    The merge, revised after the client review of 28 August. B's ground, typography and scroll-moved specular highlight, fused with C's register discipline. The macro-light-into-finished-car transition the client singled out is now the organising device for all three showcased cars, and the page takes its accent from whichever car's paint you are scrolled into. The Candy Red Volvo P1800 leads, using DPM's own photographs of their own work.

    What changed at review: the hero is lit on arrival and its sweep completes inside one viewport; a project page's worth of depth moved off the homepage into the P1800 record; every photograph now carries its own caption and credit; and the paint index has gone — the re-skin just happens as you scroll, with nothing to hover or click.

    The Catalogue

    Rejected by the client · 27 August 2026

About the photography — this differs between D and A/B/C. In A, B and C every image is AI-generated art direction, made to show the light, crop and register we would commission. None of it is DPM Autobody's work and none of those are their cars; the lots, chassis numbers, dates and figures there are invented too.

In D, The Register, the fourteen Volvo P1800 photographs are DPM's own photographs of DPM's own work, and the P1800 figures are DPM's own published record. AI plates still appear for the two secondary lots and are labelled on the page. Invented copy is labelled too.

The AI plates exist because the asset audit found no photograph of a finished DPM car in daylight away from the workshop, and everything reachable is capped at 1280px. Each is a placeholder for a real shot, and doubles as the visual half of the photography brief.
