# Interview with David — replacing our words with his

**Purpose.** The prototype pages carry two kinds of copy. Most of it is DPM's own, verbatim from
their Instagram caption, their plaque and their own site. Some of it is **ours** — written to show
what a section does, because there was nothing real to put there yet. This document lists every
piece of the second kind and gives the question whose answer replaces it.

**Status:** not yet run. Written 2026-08-30, after Ricky's call to leave the invented copy on the
page so David can see the sections and understand they are being rewritten.

**Format.** One conversation, worked through on screen with the page open — **not** a form to email.
People write badly in forms and well when talking. Record it if he is happy to be recorded; the
whole point is to get his phrasing, and paraphrase loses exactly the thing that makes the section
worth having. Twenty to forty minutes.

**Read `research/facebook-reviews.md` before section F.**

---

## A. Say this first

Before showing anything. It is short, and it should not be softened — the alternative is David
discovering it himself, halfway down a page that appears to quote his own staff.

> Two sections on this page are written in your people's voices, and those are our words, not
> theirs. Nobody has said any of it. They are there so you can see what the section does and tell
> me whether it is worth having. If it is, I want to replace every word of it with what Dave, Paul
> and Ellis actually say — which is what these questions are for.

Then be specific about the one that matters most:

> The bumper story under Ellis's name describes something going wrong and being redone. **We
> invented that incident.** I need to know what actually went wrong on this car, because a
> restoration story with no problem in it reads as an advertisement — and that is the whole reason
> the section exists.

## B. The nine stages — page section 07

The page presents nine stages with a name and a duration each, under the line _"These are our names
for them, not the trade's. Durations are the range across the cars in the shop, not a quotation for
yours."_ **Every name and every duration is ours.** The durations are the more serious half: they
read as operational data from his business.

Work down the table with him. Two questions per row — _what do you call it?_ and _how long, fastest
to slowest?_

| #   | Our name                         | Our duration       | What to ask                                                       |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01  | In, and photographed as she came | 1 day              | What happens on day one, and what do you call it?                 |
| 02  | Off it comes                     | 1–2 weeks          | Stripping to a shell — your name for it, and the realistic range? |
| 03  | Down to what is really there     | 2–3 weeks          | Blast or chemical strip — which, when, and how long?              |
| 04  | Metal first                      | 3 weeks – 5 months | Is this really where the time goes? What is the honest spread?    |
| 05  | Straight before shiny            | 3–6 weeks          | Block work over bare metal — what do you call this stage?         |
| 06  | Primer, and the guide coat       | 2–4 weeks          | How many primer stages, and is the guide coat done twice?         |
| 07  | Colour, decided in daylight      | 2 days             | Is colour genuinely judged outside the booth? How long?           |
| 08  | Clear, cut and polish            | 2–3 weeks          | How long does clear harden before you go back to it?              |
| 09  | Out in the daylight              | 2–4 weeks          | What is the last thing that happens before a car goes?            |

**Ask as well:** are there nine stages, or is nine our invention too? If DPM think in six or in
twelve, the section should be six or twelve.

## C. The log — page section 06

Four entries, each in the first person, each signed by a named man with a job title beside him. **All
four are ours.** The names are real (they are on DPM's current site); the job titles beside them are
ours; the words are entirely ours.

| Entry | Attributed to                  | Stage | The claim we invented                                                                             |
| ----- | ------------------------------ | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Dave — paint and finish        | 09    | That he walks round the car twice before it goes on the transporter, "for mine, not the owner's"  |
| 2     | Paul — colour, mixing, polish  | 07    | That candy is sprayed in one go, same gun and arm speed, and nobody talks to him while he does it |
| 3     | **Ellis — metalwork and prep** | 04    | **A fabricated incident** — see below                                                             |
| 4     | Dave — paint and finish        | 05    | The reading of "Nothing over looked. Nothing left untouched." as a working rule                   |

### The questions

**First, before any of the four:** are those the right three men, and are those their jobs? The
homepage says "Dave, Ellis and Paul are who you will meet". `research/asset-audit-dpm.md` found
**five** named in DPM's own copy — Dave, Paul, **Simon**, **Kallum**, Ellis. Who is in the shop now,
and who did what on this particular car?

**Entry 3 — Ellis, and the thing that went wrong.** This is the one to spend real time on.

> On the P1800 the bumpers were smoothed and re-plated. Did anything on that car have to be done
> twice? Not necessarily the bumpers — anything. What was it, who found it, what did it cost you in
> time, and how did you find it?

If nothing went wrong on the P1800, ask what went wrong on the Bentley, or on the Jaguar. The
section's premise is that the difficult weeks stay in. **If DPM will not put a real problem on
their own website, the section should be cut rather than filled with a safe one** — a log of four
things going well is an advertisement, which is the thing this whole design is trying not to be.

**Entry 2 — Paul and the candy.** Our text asserts that a candy cannot be blended into or
spot-repaired, and that the depth comes from the number of passes rather than the pigment. That is
broadly true of candy systems, but we are putting it in Paul's mouth as his own account of this car.

> Talk me through spraying the P1800. What is different about a candy, what happens if you get it
> wrong, and can it be repaired afterwards?

**Entry 1 — Dave and the photographs.** The 2,000-photograph figure is DPM's own and is safe. The
reading we built on it — that the file is for the next restorer in forty years, not for marketing —
is ours.

> Who is the photographic file actually for? What happens to it when the car leaves?

**Entry 4 — Dave on the tagline.** _"Nothing over looked. Nothing left untouched."_ is genuinely
DPM's, captioned under their own photographs. Only the gloss is ours.

> You write that line under your own photographs. What does it mean in practice on a car — what do
> you do that somebody else would not?

### What DPM actually call things

Section names on the page are ours by necessity, but where DPM already have a word for something we
should use theirs. One is already known and worth confirming, and it prompted a rename:

- **The paperwork.** Section 01 was called **"The register"** until 30 August. Nobody at DPM uses
  that word — it came from the auction-house framing that named Direction D, and as the first item
  in the nav it read as a verb, i.e. _create an account_. It is now **"The record"**, which the
  page's hero label and homepage CTA already used. But DPM's own word, verbatim from their caption,
  is **"portfolio"**: _"Backed up with a full restoration portfolio with over 2000 photos."_
  **Ask:** what do you call the file you hand the owner — the portfolio, the record, the file,
  something else? Whatever he says wins.
- Worth asking the same of the other section names while the page is open: **specification**,
  **the log**, **stages**. If DPM have trade words for any of them, use those.

## E. Two numbers that would close a real gap

The paint section currently answers both of these with **"In this car's file"** — which is honest,
and is a shrug where a number should be. Both are almost certainly written down somewhere.

1. **How many coats** went on the P1800 — colour and clear.
2. **The paint manufacturer and code** for that Candy Red.

Also worth asking while the file is open: **is the Bentley a drophead?** The photograph shows a
fabric roof, up. `research/asset-audit-dpm.md` calls it a Drophead; the page deliberately does not
commit, and says only "Bentley S3 Continental".

And: **what is the Sea Green Jaguar?** The page does not name the model because DPM never state it
and it is not identifiable from the footage with enough certainty to print.

## F. Permissions — none of these have been asked for

**1. The three quoted customers.** All three are public Facebook posts, but quoting a customer on
their restorer's own site is DPM's permission to seek, not ours to assume: **Liam Hunt**, **Craig
Mayhew**, **Chris Bulmer**.

**2. The quotes as they currently appear are edited, and that needs fixing whatever David says.**
Both were trimmed without ellipses. Craig Mayhew actually wrote:

> Excellent service, great quality of work to a very high standard **and always a quick turnaround**.
> I'm very fussy and they are spot on, got my business from now on.

The page drops "and always a quick turnaround" — cut because speed is the register of a good local
bodyshop and works against the concours positioning. That is a defensible editorial reason and it is
still altering a named man's words silently. Restore them verbatim, mark the cut with an ellipsis,
or drop the quote.

**3. Halcyon.** DPM does their paintwork. The prototypes state the credential without the name —
_"One of the best-known restoration houses in the country sends its cars here to be painted."_
Naming them would be the single strongest proof on the site. Are Halcyon happy to be named? Any
other trade clients?

**4. ~~The Jaguar film.~~ Not a question any more.** Ricky's instruction, 2026-08-30: treat every
DPM Instagram and YouTube video as DPM's to use however they wish. **Do not raise film rights with
David.** Third-party stills are a separate matter — the `@rpautomotivephotography` NEC files still
need a licence.

**5. Restoration testimonials.** The strongest content win left. Every recoverable review is a repair
or modification customer and the newest is September 2020 — the whole visible corpus predates the
concours work the site is built around. Three or four restoration clients willing to say something
would replace it outright.

## G. What happens to the answers

- **Stages** → straight into the section 07 table, names and ranges as given.
- **Log** → each becomes one entry, in his or their own words, lightly cut for length and nothing
  else. If a man will not be quoted, his entry goes rather than being written for him.
- **Section D answers** → corrections applied in `prototype/src/`, then `node prototype/build.mjs`,
  then `./prototype/publish.zsh` to push it live.
- **Anything he cannot answer** → the block comes out. The page is better one section shorter than
  one section invented.

Log the answers into this file under each question rather than into a new document, so the record of
what was ours and what became his stays in one place.
