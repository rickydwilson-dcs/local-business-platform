# DPM Autobody: why the site looks like this

**For David Pearce-Martin. Prepared by Ricky Wilson, September 2026.**
The prototype is at dpm-autobody.vercel.app. Your work is already far ahead of how your current
website portrays it. This is the argument behind every decision in the prototype, and what would
make it stronger.

---

## 1. What you asked for

Your brief was to elevate the website. We took that to mean aspirational: a site that reflects the
world of the people who own these cars, and the standard of the work that leaves your workshop. You
sent three sites you admire, Eagle, P&K Thornton and Halcyon.

The people who commission this work are buying a standard, and you already have 1,732 Instagram
posts doing a different job well. So what is the website for?

I've watched every series of Fast N' Loud (well, until Aaron left, because it went a bit downhill
after that), and Gas Monkey is the clearest way I can put the answer.
Most of an episode is the shop: the crew, the argument over the budget, the part that turns up wrong,
the scramble before the deadline. Then the last few minutes are the car, finished, out of the
workshop, shot somewhere else, and it feels different on purpose. The build is the people. The reveal
is the car.

Your Instagram is the build. The website is the reveal.

## 2. What we did

We took your three reference sites apart page by page, and audited your own library: every reachable
photograph, the 1,732 posts, the current site, the Facebook reviews. Then we widened it, because
three sites is a small sample to bet a brand on: roughly 80 sites in all, including 12 UK restoration
houses, 14 UK paint specialists, 13 US concours shops, and live lots at Gooding and Bonhams.

## 3. What we found

**All three are beautifully photographed, and their websites don't yet do that photography
justice.** Halcyon's homepage is 30.7MB, 86% of it one hero video. That is slow to load on a phone,
and Google takes loading speed into account when it decides who to show. Eagle's largest text
anywhere is 26px. The pictures are doing the work on their own.

That split the job. Art direction is a photography commission and costs money. The website itself is
where we can put real distance between you and the field: what we build will be well ahead of
anything else on offer in this trade.

**All three point at documented restoration, and none of them shows it.** Thornton's page describes
digital documentation and links to nothing. Halcyon's Collection has no car pages. Neither shows a before and
after. Nor does the wider set: across roughly 80 sites, not one publishes a proper record of a single
car. The best presentation of a classic car anywhere is the auction lot page, and nobody in this
trade has borrowed it.

## 4. The proposition

**One car, one record.**

Most restoration sites are a list of what a workshop can do, with a gallery. Yours is built the other
way round: every car you finish gets a page of its own, documented the way an auction house documents
a lot. On the evidence of 80 sites, that ground is unoccupied.

It also means the site grows with the work. Every car you finish adds a record, so the site is worth
more in two years than on the day it launches.

That needs two levels. The homepage carries a chosen few, and a separate archive holds every car you
have finished, so the homepage stays the same length while the archive fills up behind it. Three
decisions follow.

### 4.1 The site is about craftsmanship. Instagram is about the craftspeople.

The same split as the show. Instagram is where the people are: the day to day, Dave and Ellis and
Paul, the car that came in this morning. Live, frequent, 4,777 followers, captions that are genuinely
well written.

The website is where the standard is, and the evidence for it. Slower, fewer words, permanent. That
is the elevation you asked for, with Instagram carrying the personality it already carries well.

### 4.2 We lead on paint and finish

Engines and trim go out of house. Panel, metal, prep, colour, flatting and polish stay in.

One of the best-known restoration houses in the country sends its cars to you to be painted. Nobody
in your reference set can say anything of the kind, and right now the website can only hint at it.

Of 13 paint specialists we could check properly, one uses photography to prove a finish, and macro
and raking light appear on none of them. The visual argument of the site is surface, and almost
nobody is making it.

### 4.3 The owner's file becomes the thing you sell

You rivet a plaque into the engine bay of every car that leaves, carrying the chassis number and the
words Artists of Automotive Restoration. That is a signature. It says these cars were authored, and
it goes where nobody will read it for forty years.

The P1800 also left with over 2,000 photographs, and there is a 37 minute film of its rebuild on
DPM TV. The photographs are in the owner's file, the film is on YouTube, and none of it is gathered
anywhere a prospective customer would think to look. The website is the same claim as the plaque,
made where a customer can see it. One business in 80 sells
documentation as something a customer receives: Aston Martin Works call it Assured Provenance. Give
yours a name and it becomes part of what someone is buying.

## 5. How that shows up in the prototype

- **The homepage carries a selection,** numbered like lots. Three at the moment. No slider, no
  service grid.
- **The Jaguar is unfinished and published anyway,** its record stopping at the paint.
- **The P1800 page is the auction lot page, done for a restorer.** Identity block, your five changes,
  the plaque, paint and chrome, the underside where it's judged, then the log.
- **The log is signed.** Of 12 UK restoration houses, two name an individual against specific work.
- **The photographs are doing an argument,** like the Bentley shut line with the waistrail carrying
  straight across it.
- **The workshop page lists what goes out of house,** which makes the rest of the list believable.
- **The archive is still to build.** The prototype has three cars and the homepage
  holds all three. Once there are ten, they need somewhere of their own to live.

## 6. One thing to say out loud first

Some words on the prototype are ours, written to show what a section does. The four log entries and
the nine stage durations are our phrasing in Dave's, Paul's and Ellis's mouths, and one describes an
invented incident. Two Facebook quotes are real but trimmed. All of it gets replaced with your words
when we sit down.

## 7. What would make it more solid

**Proof: what other people say about your work**

Everything on the site today is DPM talking about DPM. Three things would change that, and all three
are asks of other people rather than of you.

1. **Three or four lines from restoration clients.** Your 24 Facebook recommendations are warm, and
   they are repair customers praising speed, the newest we could read from September 2020.
   Restoration clients
   talking about standard is what this site can use.
2. **Concours entries and placings, with dates.**
   [CKL Developments](https://www.ckl.co.uk/) in Battle published
   [a dated page](https://www.ckl.co.uk/blog-1-1/best-in-show-at-2026-royal-automobile-club-concours)
   for the 1953 Jaguar C-type they restored, which took Best in Show at the 2026 Royal Automobile
   Club Concours. Named car, named event, named date. Anything of yours that has been judged is
   worth stating the same way.
3. **Permission to name Halcyon.** A strong credential as it stands, and a much stronger one if we
   can use the name.

**Distance: the material that separates you from the field**

4. **A name for the owner's file.** One business in 80 sells documentation as something a customer
   receives. Three candidates, and I'd take the first:
   - **The Chassis File.** Ties the document to the plaque, which already carries the chassis number.
   - **The Build Record.** Plainest of the three, and says exactly what it is.
   - **The Owner's File.** What it already is, in your own words.

5. **Dated stages.** The log currently reads as a finished account. With dates it becomes a record
   that can grow while the car is still with you, which is the one thing nobody in this category
   offers.

   ```
   Now        Stage 04 · Metal first · Ellis
   With date  Stage 04 · Metal first · 11 March 2026 · Ellis, metalwork and prep
   ```

   If you have dates for the P1800, we can build it now. If not, it's worth logging on the next car.

6. **A shooting order for every car.** Auction houses order a gallery the same way every time, and it
   is what makes 50 photographs readable instead of a pile.

   ```
   01-06  Exterior: three-quarter front and rear, both flanks, head on
   07-12  Engine bay
   13-18  Interior
   19-24  Underside, on the lift
   25+    Details: shut lines, chrome, badges, wheels
   ```

   Free while the car is with you, unrecoverable once it has gone.

7. **Before and after, done properly.** No site in the paint sweep has any, nor do the three you sent.
   Matched pairs from a fixed mark, same angle, same light. Your library is 80% strip-down and prep,
   so you are better placed for this than anyone.

## 8. Photography

This is the one thing on the list we won't be doing. Photography isn't our gig, and the people who
can do it are already around you. Your brother's firm shot the P1800 film. And Rachel at RP
Automotive Photography already photographed three of your cars at the NEC in 2023, and she is the
one who introduced us in the first place.

The gap is worth naming precisely, so whoever picks it up knows what to aim at. You have a deep
library of the build: shells, primer, masking, the work itself. What it doesn't have yet is the
reveal, a finished car in daylight away from the workshop, which is what all three of your reference
sites lead with.

Three frames would change the site:

1. **A finished car in daylight, off site, at low sun.** The frame the site is missing.
2. **Paint under raking light, close.** Where the argument in 4.2 lives.
3. **Matched before and afters from a fixed mark**, on the next car in.

Same goes for the film. Brief the shoot before it happens: silent footage, slow moves across a
reflection, one scene outdoors in daylight, 4K, delivered as a short loop with a clean still frame.

One separate thing on assets. The logo is worth redrawing as a true vector at some point. Ours traces
well enough for screen, and a signwriter or a plaque needs the real thing.

## 9. Choices we've made for you

A few calls we took rather than putting them to you, because the site needed an answer to build
against.

- **Restoration and paint lead the site.** No servicing or general repair section. Every credible UK
  site in this tier keeps the two apart, and mixing them pulls the price signal down.
- **Insurance and accident work is off the site for now.** Reversible, and there are good ways to
  bring it back if that revenue matters.
- **Paint and finish carry the visual argument.** See 4.2.

One question rather than five: are you happy with restoration only?

## 10. Later

- **A living record**, the stage log updating while the car is with you. One site in 30 offers it.
- **A results page**, dated and by event.
- **The films.** You have 29 and the site mentions one. Video is a named section at the top of the
  market, and most of your regional peers have none.
- **Questions and answers**, for a buyer who has never commissioned a restoration.

One thing not to build: pages targeting "classic car restoration Eastbourne". Of 30 sites almost
nobody does, and the ones that get found are found on the marque and the model.

## 11. What we need from you, in order

1. Brief the video shoot before it happens.
2. Sit down so the prototype's words become yours.
3. Ask three or four restoration clients for a line.
4. Concours entries and placings, with dates.
5. Ask Halcyon whether they're happy to be named.
6. Confirm you're happy with restoration only.
7. Talk to Rachel about the three frames in section 8.
