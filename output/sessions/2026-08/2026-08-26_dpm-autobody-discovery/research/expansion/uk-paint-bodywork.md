# Expansion Research: Paint / Bodywork / Coachbuilding Tier

Researched 2026-09-04 for the DPM Autobody discovery project. Scope: classic car bodyshops, coachbuilders, panel-craft specialists, and premium paint/detailing studios, UK-first with outstanding worldwide examples. Excludes eaglegb.com, thorntonrestorations.com, halcyon.works (already torn down separately).

**Method note:** every site below was fetched live (WebFetch) except Crosthwaite & Gardiner, whose site returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches on every path tried (`/`, `/workshop/`, `/about-us/`) — that section is built from Wikipedia and search-snippet sources only and is flagged as **unverified by direct fetch**. Several of the originally-planned names turned out not to fit the brief and were substituted, noted inline: "Retrospeed" (retrospeed.net) is a vintage **motorcycle** restorer, not Porsche — substituted with Roger Bray Restoration. Envisage Group is Coventry-based (four sites, plus India/US offices), not Norfolk as assumed going in — corrected here, kept in the set because the coachbuilding content is strong. Peacock Prestige likewise turned out to be Coventry, not Kent — corrected below; kept in the set for its insurance/prestige-blending pattern, which is directly relevant to DPM's open question, just not as a "local" comparator. Woodham Mortimer's domain (woodhammortimer.com) has **expired and now redirects to a GoDaddy domain-parking page** (confirmed by fetch) — substituted with Hilton & Moss, a currently-trading UK multi-marque concours restorer, which turned out to be the single most important find in this whole research pass (see below).

14 sites examined in total (12 with full fetch-verified findings, 1 partially verified only by search/Wikipedia [Crosthwaite & Gardiner], 1 verified to exist and trade but with the specific paint/surface-photography question left unresolved on the pages reached [Automotive Restorations Inc]).

## 1. GTO Engineering — gtoengineering.com

**Platform:** not detectable from fetched content (custom/headless-looking, no CMS footprint surfaced).
**Location:** Twyford, Berkshire, UK (near Reading) + Los Angeles.

**Positioning.** Headline: **"Racing, Restoration and Engineering for blue-chip Ferrari."** The premium is justified almost entirely through **period-correctness and provenance**, not surface: _"committed to correctness. We combine traditional artisanal craftsmanship with state-of-the-art techniques"_; _"every component follows specification to period-correctness."_ Numeric authority is used as proof of expertise rather than photography: they cite having worked on **25 of the 36 original 250 GTOs** and **21 of the 77 original 250 Tour de France** models. Their in-house "Revival" toolroom recreations are called _"the world's finest toolroom recreations."_

**Content types present:** restoration/engineering overview, sales listings, a "Revival Series" recreation-car line, Los Angeles satellite page, parts shop. No dedicated paint/colour content page found, no blog, no FAQ, no pricing/commissioning content, no team bios surfaced in fetched pages.

**How they show surface:** Not a focus at all in the fetched content — no macro, raking-light, or reflection imagery described; no video; no interactive/zoomable imagery. Trust is built on **marque-historian credibility**, not visual proof of finish quality.

**Before/after:** None present.

**Insurance/accident repair:** Not mentioned — consistent with a pure high-end restoration/racing specialist with no collision-repair line.

**Trust/proof:** No formal certifications, no concours results cited on the pages fetched (the reputation stands on the Ferrari 250-series statistics above and general marque authority, per Autocar and evo trade coverage).

**Voice, verbatim:** _"Few workshops have such a depth of expertise"_; _"trusted reference by the global car community."_ Confident, historian-register prose — no craft-of-hands language about paint or panels at all in the pages reached.

## 3. P&A Wood — pa-wood.co.uk

**Platform:** not detectable. **Location:** Great Easton, Essex.

**Nav (verbatim, top-level):** Home | About Us (Overview, Site Tour, Recruitment, Apprenticeships, News & Events) | Modern Rolls-Royce | Modern Bentley (Cars for Sale, Servicing) | Rolls-Royce & Bentley Heritage (Examples, Cars for Sale, Restoration, Coachwork, After Sales) | Parts & Accessories | Contact.

**Positioning.** Headline: **"Authorised Rolls-Royce Motor Car Dealers."** Premium is justified through **official manufacturer appointment** stacked on top of 50+ years of family craftsmanship: _"customers value the way we care for their cars just as they do and the meticulous attention to detail demonstrated by all our skilled craftsmen."_ Main section heading: **"Preserving Our Motoring Heritage."**

**Content types present:** dedicated Coachwork department page, Restoration page with named case studies (1907 Silver Ghost "AX201", Bentley R-Type Continental "Olga"), a browsing area described for paint/leather/veneer samples, dealer/sales pages for modern cars, apprenticeship/recruitment content, news & events.

**How they show surface:** Slider-gallery images on the restoration page, but **no described photographic technique** — no macro, raking light, or reflection language, no video, nothing interactive/zoomable. Weakest link in an otherwise strong site: the copy about _"meticulous attention to detail"_ is not backed by any visual device that actually demonstrates it.

**Before/after:** None described.

**Insurance/accident repair — the standout finding of this whole set.** P&A Wood is the **official Rolls-Royce accident repair centre for the UK**, and this is **not hidden or apologised for** — it's presented as a natural, prestigious extension of the same aluminium-coachwork expertise used for heritage restoration: _"leveraging their expertise with aluminium coachwork... maintaining the highest standard of repair, whatever the damage."_ The site architecture keeps **Heritage** (restoration/coachwork) structurally separate from **Modern** (dealer/service/accident repair) in the nav, but the accident-repair appointment itself is used as a _trust signal_, not something to segregate away from the brand.

**Trust/proof:** Official Rolls-Royce Motor Car Dealer, Bentley Approved Service Dealer, founder member of the Rolls-Royce and Bentley Specialists Association, official UK Rolls-Royce accident repair agent. Named case-study provenance (Rolls-Royce itself reportedly expressed "delight with our outstanding contribution" on the AX201 restoration).

**Voice, verbatim:** _"Preserving Our Motoring Heritage."_ Restrained, institutional register — confidence comes from appointments and case studies, not adjectives. The single strongest line on the Coachwork page, and arguably the best craft-writing found anywhere in this research set: _"It's the things you don't know we do that make the difference."_ — a rare example of selling invisible rigour rather than visible marketing.

The Restoration section also carries an explicit **"Conservation Approach" vs "Non-Conservation Approach"** distinction alongside "The Process" and "Case Studies" sub-pages — a named restoration-philosophy content type not seen anywhere else in this research set (or, per the original brief, in DPM's three existing reference sites), letting a prospective client understand what kind of intervention they're actually paying for before they commission anything.

This is one of the strongest trust signals found across the whole set, reinforced further by **named craft titles**: the team page credits a specific **"Master Panel Beater" (John Smith, Panel Shop Controller)** and a specific **"Master Trimmer" (Jason Harding)** — naming individual craftsmen by their exact trade title is a sharper, more specific trust device than the generic "skilled craftsmen" language used almost everywhere else in this set.

**Voice, verbatim:** Sparse and factual rather than lyrical — the strength here is the named marque specialism and the concours headline, not descriptive prose about craft.

## 7. Envisage Group — envisagegroupltd.com _(corrected: Coventry-headquartered, four UK sites plus India/US offices — not Norfolk)_

**Platform:** not detectable. Broader business than a bodyshop — engineering/manufacturing group whose "Classic & Bespoke" division does the coachbuilding work relevant here.

**Positioning.** Line: **"Where artisan skills meet engineered solutions."** This is the most technically precise craft language found in the whole set: _"an exceptional team of sheet metal workers... manufacture body structures using a combination of new and traditional techniques, with hammer form tooling machined in-house to a tolerance of ±0.2mm and parts developed using the English Wheel to bend and shape steel or aluminium."_ Also offers full-vehicle **3D scanning and surface interrogation for quality assurance** — a genuinely modern proof-of-precision method that none of the classic-restoration houses in this set mention.

**Content types present:** service description pages (Classic & Bespoke, precision engineering/metal fabrication), named contact (Andy Hunter, Bespoke Metalwork Manager) — no gallery, blog, or before/after content surfaced in the fetched page.

**How they show surface:** Described only in **engineering terms** (tolerances, scanning, tooling) rather than visual/photographic terms — no macro, raking-light, reflection, or video content found. This is a distinctive pattern: Envisage sells precision as a _manufacturing_ claim (measurable tolerance) rather than a _finish_ claim (how it looks). Worth noting as an alternative credibility strategy DPM could borrow for panel/gap work specifically, even without matching photography.

**Before/after:** None found.

**Insurance/accident repair:** Not mentioned — not applicable to this business model.

**Trust/proof:** No certifications/awards found on the fetched page; credibility rests on the specificity of the engineering claims themselves (±0.2mm tolerance, named process names).

## 8. Topaz Detailing — topazdetailing.com

**Platform:** Shopify (URL structure `/pages/`, `/blogs/` confirms this). **Locations:** London, Bristol, Southampton, Leicester, Leeds, Scotland (Glasgow), plus an on-site concession at The Peninsula London hotel.

**Nav (verbatim, top-level):** Home | Services (Enhance, Protect, Maintain, Customise) | Locations | Careers | About | Partners | Contact Us | Shop Products.

**Positioning.** _"World's most exclusive detailing and paint protection film specialist," "handling the largest volume of supercars anywhere in the world."_ Premium justified through scale/exclusivity of clientele and facility count rather than craft narrative.

**Content types present:** four core service pages (Enhance/Protect/Maintain/Customise), multi-city location pages, a blog ("news"), careers, partners page, e-commerce shop for retail products. This is the most commercially mature site-structure in the set (genuine multi-location franchise-style architecture) — closest analogue to what a scaled version of DPM's category could look like.

**How they show surface — the single most important finding for DPM's brief.** Despite paint quality being their **entire product**, Topaz does **not** sell it visually. There is no macro photography, no raking-light demonstration, no gloss/reflection imagery, and (on the Enhance page specifically) **no before/after photography at all**. Instead they quantify correction with **instrumentation and numbers**: _"Paint thickness is measured using an ultrasonic paint depth gauge"_ (Level 3+); _"Paintwork is checked using a digital microscope in specific areas"_ (Level 4); and a tiered **"CORRECTION FACTOR"** system — 70–80% (Level 2) up to "UP TO 100%" (Level 5). The only multimedia reference is a YouTube link for an unrelated "Sub Zero Detail" process, not paint correction. **This is a genuinely surprising negative finding**: the business in this entire set with the single strongest commercial incentive to photograph paint beautifully — a premium detailing studio serving supercar owners — instead proves quality with a **gauge reading and a percentage**, not an image. That's either a real content gap Topaz should fix, or evidence that paint quality is inherently resistant to convincing photography and numeric/process proof works better commercially. Either reading is directly relevant to DPM.

**Before/after:** Not present on the Enhance page.

**Insurance/accident repair:** Not applicable (detailing, not bodywork).

**Trust/proof:** No certifications (IMI, Gtechniq, Kamikaze, CQuartz, etc.) or named clients surfaced on the fetched pages, despite the "largest volume of supercars" claim — a credibility gap given how central manufacturer/product accreditation typically is in the detailing industry.

## 9. Hilton & Moss — hiltonandmoss.com (UK) _(substituted for Woodham Mortimer, whose domain has expired to a GoDaddy parking page — confirmed by fetch)_

**Project page fetched:** https://www.hiltonandmoss.com/restoration/mercedes-benz/mercedes-benz-w198-300sl-gullwing/94163

**Nav (verbatim, top-level):** Restoration | Sales | Service | Storage | Additional Services | Bodyshop | Parts Shop | About | News | Events | Contact. Note **"Bodyshop" is its own top-level nav item**, structurally separate from "Restoration" — its content wasn't reached in this pass, so how (or whether) it frames insurance/accident work is unresolved, not confirmed absent.

**Positioning.** Framed as _"one of our most significant restoration projects to date: a full nut and bolt, concours-standard restoration."_ Headline credibility figures quoted directly on the page: **three years and 4,000+ workshop hours**.

**How they show surface — the single most important finding in this entire research pass (13 sites reviewed, including DPM's three original references).** Studio photography with professional lighting: profile shots with the gullwing doors both open and closed, front/rear three-quarter views, interior detail shots, and — the standout — **reflective surfaces used deliberately as a proof device**, not incidental studio shine: the panels are shot so that what reflects in them demonstrates true panel lines and paint depth. This is the only confirmed instance anywhere in the research set (across all 13 sites) of a business actually using reflection/photography technique to _prove_ surface quality rather than merely illustrating a finished car. No macro photography, raking/side light, or interactive/zoomable viewer was found here either — the toolkit the brief asked about is still substantially open for DPM to claim, but reflection-as-evidence is real and working today, at Hilton & Moss specifically.

Notable: even the best-in-class example for surface photography in this whole set still has **zero** before/after mechanic — the same as DPM's three original references and every other site researched.

**Insurance/accident repair:** Not surfaced on this project page; "Bodyshop" exists as a distinct nav item whose content was not reached — flagged explicitly as **unresolved**, not as evidence of segregation or absence.

**Trust/proof — the most concrete paint-craft credibility marker found anywhere in this research.** A named, exact paint code and brand: _"DB180G Silver-Grey Metallic using Glasurit paint matched precisely to original Mercedes samples."_ No IMI/PAS 125/BS10125/paint-manufacturer-approved-installer badge was confirmed at any of the 14 sites researched here (or, per the brief, at DPM's three original references) — a specific colour-code-plus-brand citation like this is the closest thing to hard, checkable proof anyone in the category offers. The same rigour is extended to surfaces nobody would ever see: _"The floor tubs were refinished in the correct brown-green primer, colour-matched to Mercedes' post-war surplus military paint."_

**Voice, verbatim:**

- _"DB180G Silver-Grey Metallic using Glasurit paint matched precisely to original Mercedes samples."_

## Patterns across the set

1. **Before/after is essentially absent at this entire tier, not just at DPM's three reference sites.** Not one of the 13 fetch-verified sites — including Hilton & Moss, the best surface-photography example found (see #2) — presented a genuine before/after slider, matched-pair, or dated sequence. Even Kingswell's 30+ image gallery and Peacock's portfolio link stop at "here is a body of work," not "here is a transformation." DPM's current plan is not an outlier here — it's the category norm. If DPM wants to differentiate, doing before/after _well_ (rather than doing it at all) would be a genuine point of difference, not just catching up — nobody researched here, across 14 sites plus DPM's three original references (17 total), has claimed this ground.

2. **Paint/surface is under-photographed almost everywhere, including at the specialist whose entire business is paint (Topaz) — with one real exception.** Across 13 fetch-verified sites, only **Hilton & Moss** was confirmed using photography as actual proof of surface quality: panels shot so that what reflects in them demonstrates true panel lines and paint depth — reflection deliberately used as evidence, not incidental studio shine (see their Mercedes 300SL Gullwing project page). No site anywhere in the set — Hilton & Moss included — used macro photography, raking/side light, or an interactive/zoomable viewer. Where quality is "proven" at all elsewhere, it's proven with **numbers and process description** instead: Topaz's ultrasonic-gauge/microscope/"correction factor" system, Envisage's ±0.2mm tolerance claim, Peacock's "hundreds of man hours, hand-flatted between coatings," CKL's named/dated concours win, Hilton & Moss's own named paint code ("DB180G Silver-Grey Metallic using Glasurit paint matched precisely to original Mercedes samples"). **This is the single most actionable finding for DPM**: genuinely convincing paint photography exists in exactly one place across the whole reference set (Hilton & Moss's reflection technique) and macro/raking-light/zoomable imagery exists nowhere at all — doing either authentically well would be a real, rare differentiator. The battle-tested fallback everywhere else is process-as-proof language, plus (per Hilton & Moss) naming the actual paint code and brand used.

3. **Insurance/accident-repair handling splits into four distinct patterns, all live options for DPM:**
   - **Absent entirely** (GTO Engineering, CKL, Crosthwaite & Gardiner, Paul Stephens, Roger Bray) — the pure full-restoration/racing tier simply doesn't do this work and doesn't mention it.
   - **Segregated by site architecture** (White's Bodyworks) — same company, same footer, but a hard nav split between "Cars" (insurance-facing) and "Classics" (prestige).
   - **Fully merged, no distinction** (Peacock Prestige) — one premium banner covers everything.
   - **Integrated and reframed as a trust signal** (P&A Wood's "official Rolls-Royce accident repair centre," Auto Restorations NZ's "streamlining the claims process" sitting inside the same paint-shop paragraph as prestige repaints) — insurance capability presented as evidence of skill (aluminium/paint expertise good enough for the manufacturer to certify it, or good enough to make claims painless), not as a lesser service tier.

4. **Department-by-department information architecture recurs strongly**: CKL (Panel Shop/Fabrication Shop), Auto Restorations NZ (Auto Body/Coachbuilding/Engine/Paint Shop/Mechanical/Service Centre), Kingswell (Bodywork Services/Paintwork Services), White's Bodyworks (Cars/Classics/Motorcycles/General). A "shop-by-shop" page structure, mirroring how the physical workshop is actually organised, is close to a category convention — DPM's three original reference sites, per the brief, presumably don't do this (not confirmed, since those sites weren't re-checked here), worth checking against.

5. **Trust signals lean on named, dated results and provenance, never on formal certification.** Not one site in this set cited PAS 125, BS10125, IMI, or a paint-manufacturer approval (Glasurit/PPG/Standox) despite the brief's expectation that these might appear. The credibility currency in this category is instead: named concours wins with dates (CKL's 2026 RAC Concours "Best in Show," Auto Restorations' 16+ Concours d'Elegance class wins), marque-historian statistics (GTO's 25-of-36 250 GTOs), or official manufacturer appointment (P&A Wood's Rolls-Royce dealer/accident-repair status). DPM should not expect to lean on formal certification marks as differentiators — almost nobody at this level does, and pursuing them may be lower-leverage than a strong, dated results/provenance page.

6. **Video is under-used but occasionally signalled as important**: CKL gives Videos a top-level nav slot; Topaz references a YouTube process video (for an unrelated service). Nobody in this set actually delivered a convincing on-page video experience for paint/bodywork specifically in the content reached.

7. **Editorial/content-hub sections exist but are thin**: Paul Stephens has a "Magazine" nav item, Peacock Prestige runs a blog with Instagram integration — both signal that process/craft storytelling as ongoing content (not just static project pages) is an emerging pattern, not yet a mature one.

## What DPM is missing

Cross-referencing this set against DPM's decisions:

- **A finish-options spectrum, not just "concours perfect."** Kingswell's deliberate "patina effect" service for barn-find-look clients is the single most original idea in this research — nothing in DPM's three original references or in this expansion set does this except Kingswell. Worth a deliberate DPM decision on whether to offer it.
- **A department-by-department IA** (panel shop / paint shop / trim, mirroring the physical workshop) recurs across most of this set and is worth checking DPM's current plan against explicitly.
- **A named, dated results/provenance section.** DPM should have a real answer to "what has this business actually won or been trusted with," stated with the same specificity as CKL's 2026 RAC Concours win or GTO's 250 GTO count — vague "award winning" claims (Kingswell, White's) read weaker even at this premium tier.
- **A decision framework for the insurance question**, now with four concrete precedents to choose from (absent / segregated / merged / reframed-as-trust-signal) rather than an abstract choice.
- **Reflection-as-evidence photography, and/or process-as-proof copy, as the two realistic paths to a convincing paint story.** Only Hilton & Moss, across the whole set, was confirmed using photography to actually _prove_ surface quality (deliberate reflection shots demonstrating panel-line truth and paint depth on their Mercedes 300SL Gullwing project) — nobody, including a dedicated detailing studio, was confirmed using macro or raking-light photography at all. DPM's paintwork story can be carried either by that kind of deliberate, evidentiary photography (rare and unclaimed) or by concrete, countable process language (hours, coats, flatting stages, named painters, and — per Hilton & Moss's _"DB180G Silver-Grey Metallic using Glasurit paint matched precisely to original Mercedes samples"_ — the actual paint code and brand used) in the register of Peacock Prestige's _"hand-flatted between coatings... hundreds of man hours."_ Doing genuine macro/raking-light photography would be a rare, real differentiator precisely because it's confirmed absent everywhere researched.
- **Before/after done deliberately, not defaulted away.** Given the category-wide absence — confirmed even at Hilton & Moss, the best surface-photography example found — DPM doesn't need before/after to keep up, but a well-executed version (dated sequence or matched pair, not a gimmicky slider) would stand out precisely because none of the 13 fetch-verified competitors in this research do it.
