# Expansion assessment — does the DPM direction survive a wider look?

**Written:** 2026-09-04, from five parallel sweeps run today. Evidence files in this folder:
`uk-restoration-houses.md` (12 sites), `uk-paint-bodywork.md` (14), `us-concours-shops.md` (13),
`auction-and-commission-references.md` (5 live auction lot pages + 7 restomod/coachbuild brands +
2 non-automotive craft houses), `content-type-matrix.md` (30-site IA sweep).

Roughly **80 distinct sites and lot pages**, with some overlap between sweeps. The original three —
eaglegb, thorntonrestorations, halcyon — were deliberately excluded; they are already torn down.

**The question asked:** we designed the DPM site off three reference sites. Widen the set. Have we
missed a content type, a positioning move, or a storytelling device that matters?

**The short answer:** the central thesis got _stronger_, not weaker — but there are **six real gaps**,
and **five positioning decisions** the wider set says must be made explicitly rather than left implied.

---

## 1. What the wider set confirms — do not re-open these

### The core thesis holds, and now has ~80 sites of evidence behind it

> _The unit of the site is the documented car, not the service._

This was inferred from three sites. It now survives a much wider look. **Not one site in the UK
restoration tier, the UK paint/body tier, or the US concours tier publishes a rigorous, fielded
single-car record** — identity block, provenance, itemised scope, dated stage log, named craftsperson
per stage, photo count, documentation offered.

The near-misses are worth naming precisely, because they show where the ceiling currently sits:

| Who                       | How close they get                                                                                                                                               | Where it stops                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bridge Classic Cars (UK)  | A running per-car blog diary with a named technician and a bylined writer                                                                                        | A diary, not a template — no fielded record, no scope, no close |
| Tolman Engineering (UK)   | Blow-by-blow build narrative naming specific problems and specific fixes ("no correct-spec roof skins available… adapted one using a custom made laser-cut jig") | One-off editorial, not a repeatable page type                   |
| Motion Products Inc. (US) | Named owner, chassis number, two exhibition dates                                                                                                                | No scope of work, no craftspeople, no stage log                 |
| Rare Drive (US)           | 62 sequential photographs on one car                                                                                                                             | Zero words                                                      |
| Paul Russell & Co (US)    | The most decorated shop in the whole research set                                                                                                                | Essentially no public single-car documentation at all           |

**So this is a white space, not a catch-up move.** That is a stronger claim than we could make in August.

### Four things already in the prototype that the research now shows are rare

1. **Reflection-as-evidence photography.** Across 14 paint/body sites — including a dedicated
   detailing studio whose entire product is paint — exactly **one** (Hilton & Moss) was confirmed
   using photography to _prove_ surface quality. Macro and raking-light photography appear **nowhere
   in the entire set**. Our Bentley shut-line/waistrail frames and the Jaguar unbroken-strip-light
   highlight are, on this evidence, doing something no competitor does.
2. **Named craftspeople against specific work.** Near-absent industry-wide (2 of 12 UK houses, 1 US
   shop with two-sentence quotes). Our signed log entries — Dave, Paul, Ellis, each on their own
   stage — are a genuine differentiator and cost nothing.
3. **Process-as-proof copy.** Where the category can't photograph paint, it counts labour instead:
   Peacock Prestige's _"Multiple layers, hand-flatted between coatings. Hundreds of man hours"_;
   Envisage's ±0.2mm tolerance; Hilton & Moss naming the actual paint code and brand
   (_"DB180G Silver-Grey Metallic using Glasurit paint matched precisely to original Mercedes
   samples"_). We already write in this register. **One cheap upgrade: name the paint brand and code,
   not just the colour name.**
4. **Publishing a car that isn't finished.** Lot 03 (the Jaguar, "In the booth", record stops at the
   paint) matches a US pattern — "current project" vs "completed project" labelling — that no UK site
   in the set does. Keep it.

### Two things we can stop worrying about

- **No before/after anywhere.** Not one of 17 fetch-verified sites presents a genuine before/after
  slider, matched pair, or dated sequence. Its absence from our build is the category norm, not a
  hole. Doing it _well_ remains unclaimed ground if we want it.
- **Formal certification marks are not the currency.** Nobody across ~80 sites cites PAS 125,
  BS10125, IMI, or a paint-manufacturer approval. The credibility currency is **named, dated results**
  and **official appointment** (P&A Wood as Rolls-Royce's approved accident-repair centre). Don't
  chase certifications; chase a dated results ledger — see gap 4.

## 2. The gaps — ranked by leverage, with what it costs us

### Gap 1 — The log is retrospective. The category's biggest white space is a _living_ one.

**The finding:** across all five sweeps, **progress reporting to the owner during a build** is the
most-flagged missing content type category-wide. Only one site (Arkonik) has a true client portal;
the best public workaround is Richards of England's per-chassis build diary.

The clearest reusable copy found anywhere in this research is Motion Products Inc.'s, and it is a
sales mechanic, not a design one — it names the channels, the visit, and the deliverable:

> _"MPI will send you photos and written updates… through the communication channel you prefer"_
> (email / Dropbox / WeTransfer named explicitly), plus an _"open invitation to view your vehicle in
> person,"_ plus an explicit promise of a **per-car documentation portfolio as a deliverable**.

**What this means for us.** Our nine-stage log is written _after_ the car has gone. The same content,
dated and published as the car moves, becomes the thing nobody else has. Lot 03 already proves we're
willing to publish mid-build. **The change is small: date the stages, and let a live car's page grow.**

**Cost:** a workflow commitment from DPM, not a build cost. Worth naming to David honestly — this only
works if someone posts.

### Gap 2 — There is no "how a restoration works here" page.

Present on only ~7 of 30 swept sites, and **absent from the entire premium tier**. Everrati publishes
seven named commissioning stages; TEC Automotive (Pevensey, 20 minutes from Berwick) publishes a
plain four-step model. Restorations Ltd explains billing in plain English.

Our workshop page answers _what is in house and what goes out_ — which is a good and unusually honest
page — but it does not answer **"I have never commissioned a restoration. What actually happens, in
what order, and how am I billed?"** Those are different questions and we only answer the first.

Written once, shop-wide, it also stops every project page having to re-explain the process.

### Gap 3 — We claim documentation. We don't name what cannot be proven.

The single strongest disclosure in the whole research set is Gooding's **Saleroom Addendum** on a
£8.09m Ferrari — a live, post-catalogue correction disclosing an active dispute over whether the car's
original engine is the one in it. Collecting Cars runs the same instinct as boilerplate: _"to the best
of the seller's knowledge,"_ _"please assume that any vehicle could have had bodywork or paintwork
during its life."_

**Not one restorer site in ~80 does this.** Every one implies total certainty.

For a site whose entire argument is _documented_, a short honest paragraph — what is recorded, what
isn't, what we were told rather than saw — is the cheapest credibility on the table, and it is
structurally on-brand in a way it wouldn't be for a competitor.

### Gap 4 — Per-car concours entries, but no company-wide ledger.

MPI's Awards page is the model: a **full chronological, dated ledger** by event and year, naming
specific trophies and even numeric judging scores — not a highlight reel. Only 12 of 30 swept sites
have any dedicated awards page.

Locally this matters more than it looks: **CKL Developments, in Battle, publishes a named, dated 2026
RAC Concours "Best in Show".** Kingswell Coachworks, also in Battle, claims "award winning
restorations" with no award named — and reads weaker for it.

The decision is about **format now**, even if the list is short: the page shape itself signals
long-term credibility, and a thin dated ledger beats a vague claim.

### Gap 5 — The owner's file is our best product and it has no name.

We already say it, well: _"Every owner gets this, whether or not it ever appears on a website."_

Across the whole set, documentation is **always prose, never a named product** — with exactly one
exception: Aston Martin Works packages it as **"Assured Provenance,"** a discrete service line.

Naming DPM's file turns a background promise into something a buyer can picture receiving. Related,
and cheap: name what's _in_ it specifically (build sheets, parts invoices, before/after photo logs,
the film) rather than "full history available".

### Gap 6 — Enquiry mechanics are phone and email only.

All 30 swept sites have phone **plus a contact form**. Our contact page has no form.

The differentiator is oddly available here: **only 1 of 30 forms anywhere asks for the car's details**
(MPI's optional Make/Model/Year). A short intent-led form — the car, what it needs, where it is —
qualifies the enquiry, matches how DPM actually works (David would rather see a car than quote from
photographs), and is essentially unclaimed. Broad Arrow's named-specialist contact card per lot is the
other half of it, and our contact page already gestures at it with _"Ask for David."_

### Smaller, still worth doing

- **Photo sequence and count.** Auction convention is an explicit counter ("1/132", "View all photos
  (144)") over an ordered run: exterior → engine bay → interior → underside → detail. We cite "over
  2,000 photographs" in prose but don't show a counted, ordered gallery. This is a **shooting
  discipline instruction for DPM**, not a code change.
- **A named film index.** Video is a named franchise at the top of the market (DK Engineering's
  "DKTV", Moto Technique's "Films") and **absent at every local competitor**. DPM has 29 DPM TV films
  with rights already settled, and the site currently mentions one of them in a sentence.
- **A stated selection posture** — Roger W. Smith's register: why we take the projects we take. Absent
  from every restorer site studied. _"Bring us the car you are not willing to compromise on"_ is
  reaching for this already; it could be said outright.
- **FAQ / objection handling** — rare in the UK, effective where present.

## 3. Five positioning decisions the research says must be explicit

Each of these is a stance the wider set argues _out loud_ and our current build leaves implied.

1. **Marque specialist or generalist?** Stated as a selling point on both sides in the US
   (Hjeltness = 300SL only; White Post = explicitly "does not specialize"). We currently show a Volvo,
   a Bentley and a Jaguar and say nothing about it.
2. **Originality or improvement?** Legendary Motorcar states "modern reliability enhancements when
   appropriate" up front. We already have a strong, specific answer buried in the P1800 page — _"a
   concours restoration is not always a return to standard — more often it is a considered argument
   with the factory"_ — which deserves to be shop-wide policy, not one car's caption.
3. **Finish spectrum.** The most original idea found anywhere in this research is Kingswell
   Coachworks' deliberate **patina service** — _"processes to produce a patina effect giving the
   appearance of an unrestored barn find."_ Nobody else offers it. They are in Battle. Ask David
   whether DPM does this, or wants to.
4. **Insurance and accident repair** — four live precedents now, instead of an abstract choice:
   _absent_ (the pure restoration tier), _segregated by nav_ (White's Bodyworks: "Cars" vs "Classics",
   same workshop), _fully merged_ (Peacock Prestige), or **reframed as a trust signal** (P&A Wood's
   Rolls-Royce approved-repairer status). Given the business is called **Autobody**, the fourth is the
   interesting one.
5. **Restoration vs servicing as separate nav items** — every credible UK site in the tier separates
   them, to signal restoration is a specialist discipline priced differently. Only relevant if DPM
   wants servicing work at all.

## 4. Local competitive picture — new, and worth knowing

The August research had no local set. There is one:

| Who                      | Where     | Note                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CKL Developments**     | Battle    | Named, **dated 2026 RAC Concours "Best in Show"**; top-tier credentials, Videos in top-level nav               |
| **Kingswell Coachworks** | Battle    | 6 team profiles, step-by-step bodywork/paint process pages, patina service; "award winning" but no award named |
| **TEC Automotive**       | Pevensey  | Four-step process explainer; thin gallery, no testimonials, no video                                           |
| **Jentree Classic Cars** | ~20 miles | Est. 1981, real longevity, visibly under-built web presence                                                    |

**The regional bar on execution is low even where the craft and reputation are real** — thin
galleries, no testimonials, no video. That is the most immediately winnable ground DPM has, and it is
a different argument from the national/white-space one.

## 5. What NOT to add — so we don't get talked into it

Absent or near-absent across the whole sweep. Omitting these is not a competitive weakness:

- **Pricing or rate cards** (4/30). One useful data point exists — White's Bodyworks publishes a £20k
  average project value — but the category norm is contact-for-quote.
- **Cars for sale / inventory** (19/30) and **parts** (12/30) — a dealer/broker business model, not
  DPM's.
- Podcasts (0/30), multi-language (0/30), sustainability (0/30), client login portals, merchandise.
- Certification marks — see §1.

**Corrected 2026-09-04 (Ricky's challenge).** An earlier version of this list included
**location + service SEO pages** (~0/30) and told us to build on marque and model instead of
geography. That was a category error: 0 of 30 competitors doing something is evidence about
_competitors_, not about _search demand_. Unoccupied ground can mean no demand, or it can mean
nobody has bothered, and a navigation sweep cannot tell those apart. Deciding this properly needs
keyword volumes, Search Console data, or a live test. Ricky's further point stands on its own:
answer engines have to retrieve _something_ for "who restores classic cars near Eastbourne", which
may make an unoccupied local page more attractive rather than less. **Location pages are an open
question, not a thing to avoid.** The safe finding, and all this research supports, is the narrow
one: _the swept competitors rank on marque and model, and almost none of them run location pages._

## 6. Recommended actions

**Build changes (small, all within the existing design):**

1. Date the stage log and let in-progress cars publish as they move (gap 1).
2. Add a "how a restoration works here" / commissioning page — process and billing in plain English
   (gap 2). This is a natural companion to the workshop/contact split already in the backlog.
3. Add the honesty paragraph to the project-page template (gap 3).
4. Name the owner's documentation file, and itemise what's in it (gap 5).
5. Add a short car-detail enquiry form to the contact page (gap 6).
6. Decide the results-ledger format now, even if it's thin (gap 4).

**Asks of David — additions to the existing list:**

- The five positioning decisions in §3, with the insurance question now framed as four options.
- Whether DPM offers, or would offer, a sympathetic/patina finish.
- Paint brand and code per car — a free credibility upgrade to copy we already write.
- Concours entries, placings and any judging scores, with dates, to seed the ledger.
- A shooting discipline for future cars: the ordered exterior → engine → interior → underside → detail
  run, so a counted gallery is possible without a reshoot.
- Whether he wants the 29 DPM TV films indexed on the site as a named film section.
