Captured 2026-09-04 via live WebFetch/WebSearch (no Playwright screenshot rig used in this pass — content-level extraction only, not pixel/layout teardown). No fact is stated from model memory.

## 1. Paul Russell and Company — paulrussell.com

**Platform:** not detectable from fetch (no obvious CMS fingerprint returned).

**Nav (verbatim):** Home / About / Services / Workshop / Careers / Classics For Sale

**Positioning:**

- Headline: **"Since 1978."**
- Core line: **"Caretakers of Automotive History"**
- Founder's statement (About page): the original 1978 vision was to "have a small shop that employed the best people capable of doing the best quality work in the industry," and the company takes "the 'Service' part seriously" and aims for "customers for life."
- Philosophy line: **"A car has to work well, not just be pretty to look at. We are most satisfied when we are able to preserve a classic car at the highest level of performance as well as beauty."**
- Andy Hilton, General Manager, quoted: "it's not what you do for work that's most important but rather how you do it and who you do it with."
- Justifies price/scope via _hours and specialism_, not marketing language: "at least six technicians in various disciplines... an average of 3000 man-hours... to complete a full body-off restoration on a typical 1950s sports car," plus "a significant number of research hours to locate original parts and materials using domestic and international sources," and "extensive road testing of the finished product."
- Awards line on homepage: "40+ years of accomplishments"; current example cited: a 1954 Ferrari 375 America Cabriolet by Vignale won "First in Class award & Best of Show Runner-Up status" and a 1966 Ferrari 275 GTB received a "Third in Class award" (2026 results, per homepage).

**Content types present:** Home / About / Services (4 categories: Automotive Restoration, Preservation & Maintenance, Sales & Brokerage, Storage at the "Essex Falls Coach House") / Workshop / Careers / Classics For Sale. **No dedicated single-car project/case-study pages were found** — restoration is described only as a general service, not documented per-car.

**Team:** Office team of seven, workshop staff of ~20 technicians (restoration specialists, body/paint techs, metalworkers, parts specialists), several trained at McPherson College's Automotive Restoration Program. Paul Russell himself remains "actively involved in daily operations." Andy Hilton profiled as General Manager with a non-automotive prior career (music industry, e-commerce) — an unusual authenticity beat.

**The single-car project page:** **Does not exist.** This is notable given Paul Russell's reputation (Hagerty calls him "Mr. Best of Show," 50+ Best of Show awards over 48 years per search summary) — the most decorated shop in the set has the thinnest public documentation of its actual restorations. Trust is carried entirely by aggregate claims (years, hours, award counts) and the founder's personal authority, not by individual car dossiers.

**Storytelling/voice:** Mixed first/third person, calm and unhurried, closer to a craft-guild statement than marketing copy. No restoration diaries, no owner narratives published.

**Commissioning process content:** None found — no estimating, timeline, or progress-reporting content on the public site.

## 3. Motion Products, Inc. (MPI) — mpi-ferrari.com

This is the standout find of the set for **trust/proof content**, even though its single-car documentation is thin.

**Nav (verbatim):** Restoration / Parts / Services (Consulting Services, Service and Maintenance, Resources and Parts, Why MPI) / About Us (Our History, Our Process, Our Team, Contact Us, Awards, Portfolio) / News / Careers

**Positioning:**

- Headline: **"Award-Winning Classic Car Restoration"**; tagline **"Secure Auto Restoration Services."**
- "Motion Products, Inc. is committed to fully understanding the goals and expectations of their clients."
- Scale claims used as trust signal: "over forty highly skilled classic car restoration technicians," "45 experts in 75,000 sq. ft. of space," "complete over 98% of the project in-house."
- Awards claim: "100's of awards to our name on more than 500 cars."

**Content types present:** Restoration (marketing/overview) / Parts / Services / About Us / Our History / **Our Process** (an explicit "eleven phases of restoration," referenced by name from the homepage though the phase list itself wasn't retrievable in this pass) / Our Team / **Awards** (a genuinely exhaustive results archive) / Portfolio / News / Careers.

**Progress reporting to owner — the best example found in the whole set:** MPI's site states plainly: **"MPI will send you photos and written updates of your vehicle when you would like and through the communication channel you prefer"** (email, Dropbox, WeTransfer named explicitly), and clients get **"an open invitation to view your vehicle in person throughout the restoration process."** The Metal Fabrication phase is flagged as a specific milestone where the client is invited to see "your car in its raw form" and discuss repairs needed. And critically: **"For every project we prepare a detailed portfolio specifically for your car with everything we've done from research, to intake through all the steps of the restoration"** — i.e., MPI explicitly promises the owner a bespoke documentation binder as a deliverable, which is exactly the artifact the brief asks whether US shops offer. This is the clearest confirmed instance of "documentation/binder offered to owner" in the set.

**Awards page — the richest concours-proof content found anywhere in this research.** It is not a highlight reel; it is a full chronological results ledger, broken out by event (Amelia Island Concours d'Elegance, Ferrari Club of America, Cavallino Classic/Palm Beach, Milwaukee Concours d'Elegance, Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance, Celebration Exotic Car Festival) going back to the 1990s, each entry naming: year, car (marque/model/year, sometimes chassis s/n), specific award name (e.g. "The Borla Trophy for Best Sound on the Field," "The Denise McCluggage Award – Most Historically Significant Post War Racing Car," "Coppa di Platino," "Strother MacMinn Most Elegant Sports Car Trophy," "Enzo Ferrari Memorial Award: Overall Best of Show Ferrari"). It also flags a rarefied metric UK sites don't use at all: **judged numeric scores** — "100pt Ferraris" is tracked as its own list (e.g. 1993 212 Inter PF Coupe, noted as "first ever 100 pt Ferrari at Pebble Beach"). This is the single clearest confirmed example in the whole research set of "concours results and judging scores achieved" being published as first-class site content rather than a passing mention.

**Individual project page anatomy (as found):** `mpi-ferrari.com/portfolio/1951-ferrari-212-export-berlinetta-touring-s-n-0108e/` — identity is given as **"1951 Ferrari 212 Export Berlinetta Touring s/n 0108E"** (marque/model/year/chassis, no engine number), labelled **"Full Restoration,"** names the owner/driver (Kevin Caulfield), documents two exhibition dates (Pebble Beach Concours, August 2012; Cavallino Classic, January 2013), and notes current status ("stored and maintained at Motion Products, Inc."). No itemized scope of work, no dated log, no named craftspeople, no before/after pairs, no photo inventory on the page itself — the depth lives on the Awards page, not the Portfolio page.

**Named craftspeople:** Two found on the Our Team page — **Dave Jensen, Trim Fabricator** ("Cars were hand built back then, and are still repaired in the same fashion at MPI. Each one is slightly different due to parts of a vehicle being built for each specific car making these some of the most unique cars in the world") and **Aaron Siebers, Upholstery Technician** ("The major difference between these types of projects is that our projects here at MPI are more custom. Each car is just a little different"). Both quotes emphasize hand craft and per-car uniqueness — a voice choice worth noting.

## Patterns across the set

1. **Aggregate trust beats individual-car trust almost everywhere except MPI.** Every shop in the set leans on _counts_ — years in business, technician headcount, square footage, "hundreds of awards," man-hours-per-car — as the primary credibility mechanism. Only Motion Products Inc. converts that into a fully enumerated, dated, named ledger of results. Paul Russell, the single most decorated name in the set by reputation, has essentially no public single-car documentation at all.
2. **The single-car "project page" exists almost everywhere as a URL pattern, but its depth varies enormously and independently of the shop's actual prestige.** Confirmed URL patterns: `/project/<slug>/` (LaVine, White Post), `/portfolio/<slug>/` (MPI), `/<short-slug>` (Rare Drive). But depth ranges from **zero identity/provenance/scope data plus 6 photos** (White Post) to **62 sequential photos and zero words** (Rare Drive) to **a named owner, two exhibition dates, and a s/n, but still no scope-of-work or craftspeople** (MPI) to **effectively nonexistent** (Paul Russell, Legendary Motorcar, ARI).
3. **Marque specialization is a common and explicit positioning axis** (Hjeltness = 300SL only; MPI = Ferrari/Lamborghini/Maserati; Rare Drive = Porsche/Mercedes/Ferrari/Cobra/GT40) **and it is stated as a selling point, not hidden.** White Post is the explicit counter-position ("does not specialize in any particular make or model").
4. **Founder-obsession origin stories are common and effective** — Jerry Hjeltness buying his first Gullwing in 1960 and working from his garage for 23 years before opening in 1984; Paul Russell's 1978 founding vision quoted directly; Bruce Canepa as a former race driver. This mirrors the UK set's Eagle/Halcyon pattern rather than departing from it.
5. **Dealer/inventory and restoration are frequently the same website, sometimes the same page type**, especially at Legendary Motorcar and (per search) RM Sotheby's. DPM's "documented car, not the service" principle is a genuine point of difference from most of this set, not an assumed norm.
6. **Bot-blocking (Canepa, Motorcar Studio) is itself a finding**, not just a research obstacle: at least one credible concours-tier shop's site is hardened against automated scraping, which as a side effect also means no AI answer engine or third-party aggregator can currently surface its content either — worth a one-line note to the client if SEO/AI-visibility becomes a topic later.

## What the US does that the UK doesn't (per the eaglegb/thornton/halcyon teardowns already on file)

- **A published, dated, per-event concours results ledger as a standing page** (MPI's Awards page) — not a highlight reel, a full archive going back decades, broken out by event and year, naming specific trophy names and numeric judging scores ("100pt Ferraris").
- **Vintage Racing Services as a discrete commercial product line** — trackside support, driver coaching, chassis setup, race-car rentals (ARI). None of the UK shops sell track/competition support as a named service.
- **Explicit progress-reporting-to-owner language as marketing copy**, naming the actual communication channels (email/Dropbox/WeTransfer) and promising a per-car documentation portfolio as a deliverable (MPI). This is the most directly actionable single finding for DPM's "progress reporting to owner" question in the brief.
- **Photographer bylines on portfolio images** (Hjeltness) — crediting the photographer, not just the restorer.
- **A public motorsports museum attached to the restoration business** (Canepa, per search) as a physical trust venue.
- **"Current project" vs. "completed project" labelling that publishes work-in-progress**, not just finished cars (Farland, Vintage Car Works — non-seed but notable).
- **Explicit "we will modernize when asked" positioning stated up front** (Legendary Motorcar's "modern reliability enhancements when appropriate") rather than treating originality as an unstated absolute — a more commercially candid stance than any UK or other US shop in this set takes.
- **A marque-specialist vs. generalist axis stated as an explicit, named positioning choice** on both sides (Hjeltness/MPI/Rare Drive vs. White Post) — the UK set's shops are all marque specialists by default (Eagle=Jaguar, Thornton=presumably British marques) without needing to argue for specialization as a stance.

## What DPM is missing (relative to this set, weighted toward the strongest examples)

1. **No shop in either the UK or US sets we've reviewed does the single-car documented-project page consistently well — this is DPM's opportunity, not a gap to close by copying a competitor.** The best single element found (MPI's Awards ledger; Hjeltness's claimed perfect Pebble Beach score; Rare Drive's raw photo volume) are each partial. Nobody combines identity block + real provenance chain + itemized scope + dated stage log + named craftspeople + photo count + concours results + owner documentation in one page. DPM's stated project (making the documented car the actual unit of the site) would be, on this evidence, **genuinely novel in the category**, not a catch-up exercise.
2. **A concours-results ledger as a standing, dated, ever-growing page** (MPI's Awards page is the model to study, not copy verbatim) — DPM should decide early whether it wants this to exist even before it has decades of results to fill it, since the page format itself signals long-term credibility.
3. **Explicit progress-reporting language and channel commitments to the owner during a build** — MPI's "photos and written updates... through the communication channel you prefer" plus "open invitation to view your vehicle in person" is the single most concretely reusable piece of commissioning-process copy found in this research. Currently absent from DPM's own site per the existing UK teardowns.
4. **A stated position on marque specialization vs. generalism** — DPM should decide and say explicitly which side of this line it's on, rather than leaving it implicit.
5. **A stated position on originality vs. driveability/modernization** — Paul Russell ("has to work well, not just be pretty") and Canepa ("extensive road testing") and Legendary Motorcar ("modern reliability enhancements when appropriate") all take an explicit stance here; this is a positioning question DPM's copy should answer on purpose.
6. **A public-facing named-craftsperson content type, even minimal** — MPI's two-sentence quotes from Dave Jensen (trim) and Aaron Siebers (upholstery) are a low-cost, high-authenticity content type DPM could adopt cheaply.
