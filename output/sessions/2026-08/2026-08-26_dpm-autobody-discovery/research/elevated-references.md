# Elevated references — the layer above eaglegb / thorntonrestorations / halcyon

**Session:** 2026-08-26 DPM Autobody discovery
**Scope:** deliberately NOT the three reference sites — colleagues are tearing those down separately.
This document is the layer above them: who the customer actually is, what luxury craft brands outside
automotive do better, how an auction house presents a single car, how making-as-content is structured,
what "expensive" means technically in 2026, and three art directions to prototype.

**Evidence standard.** Every site in the gallery was captured headless with
`research/tools/capture-site.mts` and **looked at** — screenshot filenames are given so anyone can
check. Typeface names are the **computed `font-family` the browser resolved**, tallied over leaf text
nodes by the capture tool, i.e. what the site declares — not what I remember. Colours are computed
`rgb()` values from the same pass. Anything I could not verify is marked **ASSUMPTION** or
**INFERENCE**. Where a capture failed (cookie wall, mailing-list modal, Cloudflare interstitial) I say
so rather than describe a page I did not see.

---

## 1. The thesis in five lines

1. **Elevated is not darker, bigger or more animated. It is more evidenced.** Halcyon's ceiling is a
   720p loop and a stock fade; ours is not "a better loop" — it is a site that can prove things.
2. **The unit of the site is the documented car, not the service.** Every top-tier presentation of a
   single classic car in the world — RM, Bonhams, Gooding, Broad Arrow — is a lot page: identity block,
   provenance ladder, itemised originality, named hands, 50–100 photographs. Nobody in the restoration
   trade builds their project pages that way. That gap is the whole opportunity.
3. **Name every hand, state every number, declare every gap.** Auction houses name the restorer, the
   town, the duration and the cost, and admit what they cannot prove — and their prose is stronger for
   it. The moment a page stops naming, it collapses into "the highest possible standards."
4. **Photograph the customer's world, not the shop's.** The buyer's other possessions are quiet,
   old-money-adjacent and photographed in even overcast light on location, not under showroom lamps.
   The workshop appears as evidence of work, never as the brand's self-image.
5. **Restraint is the flex.** The single most expensive-looking hero in the whole reference set —
   Roger W. Smith's — is one sentence of serif type on a field of green, with no photograph at all.
   We can out-Halcyon Halcyon by removing the video, not by shooting a better one.

## 2. The customer

Evidence-backed. Sources cited; inferences flagged.

**The best single primary source is Magneto's own media pack**, because Magneto is posted to this
person by name:

> "The typical Magneto reader will be in their mid- to late-50s, and will be at the top of their
> career. Generally self-made, or second generation wealth, they now have the time and desire to build
> a collection, go racing or travel the world to take in the best global events."
> — [Magneto 2023 Media Information](https://media.magnetomagazine.com/app/uploads/2022/12/02145129/Magneto-Lifestyle-Media-Presenter-2023-3.pdf)

> "Alongside their passion for classic and collector cars, Magneto readers share a love of
> craftsmanship and quality. Watches, art and automobilia, fine wines and other collectibles are of
> keen interest." — same document

Magneto states **"The average reader has a multi-car collection valued in excess of $1million"**
([magnetomagazine.com/advertise](https://www.magnetomagazine.com/advertise/)), circulation **12,500+**,
geography **UK 45% / USA 35% / Europe 13% / RoW 7%** — a UK-majority audience, which matters for DPM.
Its distribution list is itself the demographic: Pebble Beach / Salon Privé / Hampton Court / Amelia
entrants and VIPs, Motor Racing Legends entrants, and **"Hagerty insurance clients with classic car
portfolios worth in excess of £500,000"** ([2022 pack](https://media.magnetomagazine.com/app/uploads/2021/09/14141728/Magneto-Media-information-2022.pdf)).

**Do not conflate this with the mass hobby.** FBHVC's 2025 National Historic Vehicle Survey (19,900+
responses) reports **1,934,178 DVLA-registered historic vehicles**, **£7.3bn/yr** sector contribution,
**34,500 jobs across 2,700 specialist businesses**, average owner spend **£4,567/yr**, and "the average
owner is now in their sixties" ([FBHVC](https://www.fbhvc.co.uk/news/article/britains-love-of-historic-vehicles-powers-73-billion-boost-to-the-uk-economy)).
Over a third of those owners earn under £35,000. That is not DPM's customer.

**Market climate.** Total collector-vehicle sales **rose 10% in 2025 to $4.8bn**, $2.5bn of it online
([Hagerty](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/by-the-numbers-the-collector-car-market-in-2025/)).
But Hagerty UK reports **nearly 80% of UK classic values declined or were static** vs late 2024, with
**British brands in 9 of the 10 most-declined marques** and **Jaguar down 21.4%**
([Hagerty UK](https://www.hagerty.co.uk/articles/analysis-classic-car-market-2025/)). Knight Frank's
Luxury Investment Index **closed 2025 down 0.4%**, with buyers "increasingly disciplined, favouring
rarity and value" ([Knight Frank, Apr 2026](https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/knight-frank-luxury-investment-index-luxury-holds-steady)).
**INFERENCE:** in a flat market, evidenced quality is what separates a car that holds value from one
that doesn't. That is DPM's argument, and the soft market strengthens it rather than weakening it.

**Where they go.** Goodwood Revival, 18–20 Sep 2026, admission **from £84**, 3-day **£240**, ~150,000
over three days, celebrating 1948–1966, with a paddock dress code
([goodwood.com](https://www.goodwood.com/motorsport/goodwood-revival/tickets-and-packages/admission/)).
Salon Privé at Blenheim, **entry fee £3,850 UK / from £4,800 international**, tickets £60–£645, claiming
a place "raises the provenance of every vehicle which enters"
([salonpriveconcours.com](https://www.salonpriveconcours.com/apply-to-enter/)). Concours of Elegance
Hampton Court, 80 of the rarest cars worldwide, presented by A. Lange & Söhne.

**The one fact to build a page around.** Pebble Beach: entries are vetted by a Selection Committee, and
once accepted **a car cannot re-enter for ten years — unless ownership changed _or the car has been
restored_.** Their own FAQ adds: "Many collectors spend years and hundreds of thousands of dollars
purchasing and restoring a car in hopes of being chosen"
([pebblebeachconcours.net](https://www.pebblebeachconcours.net/plan-your-visit/faqs/)). A restoration is
the mechanism by which a car re-enters the highest room in the world. That is the commercial argument,
in the organiser's own words.

**What else they buy — sponsor lists are the hardest available evidence.** Pebble Beach's current
sponsors ([list](https://www.pebblebeachconcours.net/sponsors/current-sponsors/)): Rolex, Lalique,
Goldman Sachs, Flexjet, Wheels Up, Ralph Lauren, Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Dom Pérignon,
Ruinart, Oliva Cigars. Salon Privé ([partners](https://www.salonpriveconcours.com/partners/)): Aviva
Private Clients, Lockton, Boodles, Pommery, Bell Textron, **Clements + Church as Official Tailoring
Partner**, Boat International. Goodwood Revival: Rolex, 20 years as Official Timepiece. Chantilly:
Richard Mille. Villa d'Este: BMW Group.

**Read that list back as a mood board and it is the entire brief**: watches, private aviation, private
banking, champagne, bespoke tailoring, cigars, superyachts, the FT. Not one of them is a car brand.
Sotheby's corroborates the behaviour commercially — it deliberately **"mixes cars, watches, jewellery
and handbags at auction"** to reach cross-collectors
([WatchPro](https://usa.watchpro.com/sothebys-appeals-to-cross-collectors-as-it-mixes-cars-watches-jewellery-and-handbags-at-auction/)).

**Directly relevant:** London Concours' sponsor list already includes
**"Halcyon Works — Specialist Manufacturer"** alongside Gunther Werks, Boreham Motor Works and AirX
([londonconcours.co.uk](https://londonconcours.co.uk/sponsors-partners/)). DPM's paintwork is already
inside this tier; the website is what hasn't caught up.

**The UK angle.** Berwick sits ~40 miles east of Goodwood, which is the gravitational centre — a
2.4-mile circuit on the Duke of Richmond's estate, opened September 1948 on the RAF Westhampnett
perimeter track; FoS and Revival together generate **£105m for the UK economy, £37m of it around
Chichester** ([Chichester DC study, 2023](https://www.chichester.gov.uk/media/39569/The-Goodwood-Estate-Socioeconomic-Contribution-2022-July-2023/pdf/The_Goodwood_Estate_-_Socioeconomic_Contribution_2022__July_2023.pdf)).
The competing pole is Bicester Heritage — 444 acres, 40–50 specialist businesses, combined turnover
£40m–£50m+ ([bicestermotion.com](https://bicestermotion.com/heritage)). **INFERENCE:** DPM sits at the
eastern end of a Sussex/Surrey/Hampshire cluster whose narrative centre is Goodwood; nobody owns "East
Sussex + concours paint". I found **no published study defining a south-east classic-car corridor** —
treat "corridor" as a positioning device, not a citable fact. Also note Bicester's own "£18.8bn historic
motoring industry" claim conflicts with FBHVC's audited £7.3bn; **use £7.3bn**.

**Gaps I could not close** — state as unknown, do not fabricate: car-specific Knight Frank LII
percentages (behind the Wealth Report paywall); Octane's rate card (HTTP 403); current audited ABC
circulation for Classic & Sports Car; and **no organiser among Goodwood, Salon Privé or Pebble Beach
publishes an attendee wealth profile** — the Magneto distribution list is the closest proxy that exists.

## 3. Reference gallery

Twelve sites, all captured and looked at. Screenshots live in `research/screenshots/<label>/` and are
gitignored by design.

### 3.1 Roger W. Smith — [rwsmithwatches.com](https://www.rwsmithwatches.com/) — **the single best reference here**

_Sells:_ hand-made watches, 15–20 a year, one workshop, Isle of Man.
**Take:** _the type-only hero._ No photograph, no video. A field of deep olive
(`rgb(50,65,46)`) filling the viewport, the wordmark in gold (`rgb(172,126,55)`), and one serif line —
"Twenty-five years of the hand-made watch" — at **66px, weight 250**, in pale warm grey. Below it, the
word `SCROLL`. It is the most expensive-feeling thing in this entire document and it contains no
imagery whatsoever. Then: "Reflections from The Bench" in the maker's first person, a photo-essay of
hands at the bench, and a full-width italic statement, "Hand built from start to finish."
Stack (computed): Cambon + Gilroy, **GSAP + ScrollTrigger + Lenis on Webflow** — i.e. a small British
maker is already running the toolkit Halcyon is not.
_Weakness to not copy:_ it philosophises more than it documents. There is no step-by-step.
`ref-rwsmith/desktop-hero.png`, `ref-rwsmith/desktop-full.png`

### 3.2 A Collected Man — [acollectedman.com](https://www.acollectedman.com/) — **the best project-page model**

_Sells:_ rare and independent watches, plus a genuinely serious journal.
**Take:** _give the object a title, not a description._ The homepage hero is a full-bleed macro of one
watch with the words **"TRANSIENT STATE"** set in a high-contrast serif (computed: GT Alpina Fine) at
**65px, weight 300, uppercase**, a three-line caption bottom-left, and a `1 | 5` counter bottom-right.
The journal hero is better still: a split page — left third white with the article title at 52px, an
italic byline ("By Russell Sheldrake"), and at the foot **"DECEMBER 2021 · 21 MIN READ"**; right
two-thirds a macro with the object's name reversed out across it and a hairline rule running through.
Advertising a 21-minute read is a confidence signal — depth is the product.
Type discipline: exactly two families site-wide, Helvetica Now Text + GT Alpina Fine.
`ref-acollectedman/desktop-hero.png`, `ref-acm-journal/desktop-hero.png`

### 3.3 The Naked Watchmaker — [thenakedwatchmaker.com/deconstruction](https://www.thenakedwatchmaker.com/deconstruction)

_Sells:_ nothing. It exists to take watches completely apart. Tagline: "Insight into the heart of
watchmaking."
**Take:** _the deconstruction grid._ Every single component photographed separately on neutral ground,
individually named ("Sapphire dial", "Crown wheel", "Balance cock"), click-to-enlarge, ending in
schematics showing where each part sits. **A restoration IS a deconstruction.** DPM strips cars to
bare shell; photographing every removed part on a neutral sweep, named and indexed, is a content asset
no competitor has and DPM already generates for free. Structurally perfect, visually plain (Squarespace,
Proxima Nova) — take the structure, not the styling.
`ref-nakedwatchmaker/desktop-full.png`

### 3.4 Purdey — [purdey.com](https://www.purdey.com) + [/pages/stages-of-gunmaking](https://www.purdey.com/pages/stages-of-gunmaking)

_Sells:_ bespoke guns, £100k+, 6–18 months each.
**Take:** _two IA moves, not the visual design._ Honestly: the homepage is a conventional
Shopify-style tile grid (Hoefler Text headings at 40px/400, Helvetica Neue body at 14px, burgundy
`rgb(123,38,49)`, Swiper, no scroll animation). What is worth stealing is:

- **"The Seven Stages of Building a Bespoke Purdey"** — a numbered, _named_ chapter set: Barrel Making
  · Actioning · Ejectors · **Time To Take Stock** · Engraving · Proof and Finishing · **Signed, Sealed,
  Delivered**. Chapter names with wit, not process nouns. With hard numbers: "170-plus components",
  finishing "can take up to eight weeks", 6–18 months per gun.
- **History Certificates** — Purdey sells a two-page PDF transcribing the original Dimension Book entry
  plus a photograph of it, "together with information on subsequent alterations and owners where
  recorded". They monetise the archive. DPM's "fully documented restoration" is the same artefact and
  is currently given away as an adjective.
- **"The World of Purdey"** as a homepage section — the customer's world, not the workshop. Exactly
  our positioning principle, executed by a 1814 gunmaker.
  `ref-purdey/desktop-full.png`, `ref-purdey-stages/desktop-full.png`

### 3.5 Anderson & Sheppard, The Notebook — [anderson-sheppard.co.uk/thenotebook](https://www.anderson-sheppard.co.uk/thenotebook/)

_Sells:_ Savile Row bespoke tailoring. Running since 2010.
**Take:** _byline the maker, not the company._ Every post is signed "By Martin", "By Conor", "By
Oliver", "By Audie" — apprentices and cutters, each with their own archive page, so you can read one
person's whole arc. Warm cream ground (`rgb(239,234,227)`), oxblood footer, category rail down the
right (Cutting · Mills & Merchants · Inside the shop). The register is the point:

> "I love the striking in and cutting process because it's just me and my shears getting to work."

Watch the writing flatline the instant the company voice takes over. Dave, Ellis and Paul each writing
their own posts beats any agency copy DPM could buy.
`ref-as-notebook/desktop-full.png`

### 3.6 Westley Richards + The Explora — [westleyrichards.com/theexplora](https://www.westleyrichards.com/theexplora/)

_Sells:_ Birmingham gunmaking since 1812, plus safari clothing and leather.
**Take:** _the named recurring series, and negative letter-spacing._ The Explora publishes weekly, has
1,000+ posts and ~500k page views a year, with named series — a **Gunmaker Tool Series** (one worn tool
per post, ~800 words, explained by the man who uses it) and **craftsman Q&As** ("I was born in 1962 when
everything was black and white, in Coseley, near Dudley" — actioner Peter Cain). Typographically it is
the most current site in the gunmaking set: FS Brabo serif + QuadraatSansPro, headings at 36px/300 with
**letter-spacing −1.44px**. Tight negative tracking is the modern expensive tell; the wide-tracked caps
of traditional luxury now read as dated. Runs GSAP + ScrollTrigger, 1080p hero video.
`ref-westleyrichards/desktop-full.png`, `ref-wr-explora2/desktop-full.png`

### 3.7 Hemmerle — [hemmerle.com](https://www.hemmerle.com)

## 4. The auction-catalogue pattern

**Thesis largely proven.** A lot page is the only format that treats one car as a documented object
with a chain of custody — and it names restorers, by name, as a value-adding fact. All quotes verbatim,
retrieved 26 August 2026.

### 4.1 The skeleton

Four houses, one structure. Identity block **as a labelled key/value table, before any prose** — RM's
[Cal Spider](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mc26/lots/r0015-1961-ferrari-250-gt-swb-california-spider-by-scaglietti/)
carries `Chassis No. 2955 GT` / `Engine No. 2955 GT` / `Gearbox No. 6/61` / `Rear Axle No. 382 F`.
Broad Arrow's [275 GTB](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/jc24_r125/1965-ferrari-275-gtb)
carries four numbers likewise. Then title, coachwork credit, estimate, a bulleted highlights block,
then narrative.

**Gooding's is the most reusable template** because provenance is _structured data, not prose_:
Lot / year / title / **Coachwork by Vanden Plas** / Estimate / `Chassis MS3927` / `Engine MS3930 (See
Text)` / **Car Highlights** / **Technical Specs** / **Provenance** / **Exhibition Highlights** /
**Featured Media** / narrative / **Saleroom Addendum**.

Bonhams adds one move worth stealing: **an epithet line above the title** — "'41 DPX', the ex-Peter
Sellers, 'The Wrong Arm of the Law'".

### 4.2 Provenance — the ladder

> **Provenance**
> O.D. Winterbottom, Newbury, UK (acquired new in 1931)
> F. W. Ball, Chelsea, UK (acquired from the above in 1938)
> Captain P.G. Ashton, Isle of Wight (acquired from the above in 1939)
> Michael Crowley-Milling, UK (acquired circa 1948)
> […]
> Chris Blundell, UK (acquired in 2011)
> Current Owner (acquired from the above)
> — [Gooding, Lot 139, Blower Bentley](https://www.goodingco.com/lot/1931-bentley-4-1-2-litre-supercharged-blower-sports-tourer)

Name, place, dated acquisition clause, one per line. "acquired new in" / "acquired from the above in" /
"acquired circa" for uncertainty / "Current Owner" anonymised. Sixteen rungs, no gaps.

RM does it in prose, always anchored to a month, and treats **duration of custody as a virtue**:
"Mr Mars retained possession of the 250 GT for a remarkable period of over 30 years"; "benefits from 21
years of fastidious care under current ownership." Owners are _custodians_.

**The single most transferable rhetorical move on any of these pages is admitting a gap:**

> "It is believed that Sellers owned '0157' for a period after filming, although, well known for never
> owning any one car for very long, he is likely to have kept it but briefly. Stephen Archer and
> Richard A Candee's definitive book, The Aston Martin DB4GT, published by Palawan Press, lists 13
> owners but omits Sellers, possibly because he never got around to registering the car in his name."
> — [Bonhams, Lot 225P](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/27997/lot/225P/1961-aston-martin-db4gt-coupe-chassis-no-db4gt0157r-engine-no-4000157gt/)

Naming the gap, citing the source that contradicts you, and showing your reasoning buys more
credibility than a clean claim.

### 4.3 Condition and originality

> "Factory certified in 2019 with a Ferrari Classiche 'Red Book' stipulating the presence of the
> matching-numbers engine, gearbox, rear axle, and coachwork" — [RM, Lot 164](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mc26/lots/r0015-1961-ferrari-250-gt-swb-california-spider-by-scaglietti/)

> "Retains Its Original Chassis, Steering Box, Front and Rear Axles, with Original Replacement Blower
> Engine and Gearbox per Factory Records" — [Gooding, Lot 139](https://www.goodingco.com/lot/1931-bentley-4-1-2-litre-supercharged-blower-sports-tourer)

Three rules in the grammar: **"retains" is the load-bearing verb** (it asserts survival, not
restoration); originality is always **itemised**, never just "original"; and every claim is **sourced
to an issuing authority** — "per Factory Records", "as authenticated by a Ferrari Classiche 'Red
Book'". Where originality is compromised it is flagged _in the spec block itself_ —
`Engine MS3930 (See Text)` — and explained in the text.

### 4.4 How a restoration is written — and yes, the restorer is named

**This is the finding that matters most for DPM.**

> "Refurbishment of the chassis and bodywork was entrusted to renowned marque specialists Bodylines,
> while the reassembly and repainting was completed by their equally skilful colleagues at Spray Tec
> Restorations. During the rebuild the exterior colour was changed to factory Goodwood Green. Lasting
> some four years, the restoration cost in excess of £100,000. On the mechanical side, Aston Martin
> specialists Rex J Woodgate rebuilt the engine and gearbox to ensure that '0157' was in tiptop
> condition at an additional cost of £26,000."
> — [Bonhams, Lot 225P](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/27997/lot/225P/1961-aston-martin-db4gt-coupe-chassis-no-db4gt0157r-engine-no-4000157gt/)

Four firms named, work divided, duration stated, **cost stated as two separate figures**. Note that
**the paint shop is named separately from the body shop** — precisely DPM's position in the Halcyon
relationship.

> "In preparation for a showing at Pebble Beach, the owner turned to Jeff McDonald's team at McDonald
> Vintage Restoration in Candby, Oregon, whose workmanship on Rolls-Royce and Bentleys has consistently
> garnered major concours awards. The refurbishment respected this Blower's intrinsic authenticity and
> utilized sister car MS3926, owned by Dr. Fred Simeone, to ensure accuracy in the details. […] As a
> testament to the quality and exacting detail of this restoration, the Bentley won its class at Pebble
> Beach in 2022." — [Gooding, Lot 139](https://www.goodingco.com/lot/1931-bentley-4-1-2-litre-supercharged-blower-sports-tourer)

The full chain: **named principal → named firm → named town → the firm's own award record → the method
→ the specific decisions → the award that validates it.** Gooding puts the restorer _in the highlights
bullets, above the fold_: "Concours Restoration by Marque Specialists VBE and McDonald Vintage
Restoration."

The model paragraph for a restorer's own site — what was kept and why, what was replaced and why, and
by what method:

> "…with particular attention to preserving the chassis sub-frames (which were deemed to be original).
> Because the rear clip and the top surface of the front bonnet had been battered and stress-hardened
> during years of use, these sections were removed and replaced with new ones pounded on the original
> body bucks. The opportunity was also taken to install a new rear tailfin, and the restored coachwork
> was treated to a bare-metal refinish in British Racing Green."
> — [RM, Jaguar D-Type XKD 546, Lot 338](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo23/lots/r0098-1955-jaguar-dtype/)

**The counter-example proves it.** Bonhams'
[1926 Bentley 3-Litre](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/24876/lot/353/1926-bentley-3-litre-speed-model-sports-tourer-chassis-no-de1220-body-no-1292-engine-no-de1225-see-text/)
describes a total rebuild in loving detail — "repainted in Brewster Green using traditional and correct
nitro-cellulose paint… all brightwork was exquisitely re-plated in contrasting nickel" — and **names
nobody**. Within a sentence the prose degrades into "carried out to the highest possible standards",
the exact unfalsifiable phrase the named examples never need.

### 4.5 The opening pitch

> "Claiming a role as one of Maranello's special period show cars, and boasting the presence of its
> matching-numbers components as authenticated by a Ferrari Classiche 'Red Book', not to mention a
> recent refurbishment by a world-renowned Italian restoration shop, this scintillating short-wheelbase
> California Spider is an exquisite example." — [RM, Lot 164](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mc26/lots/r0015-1961-ferrari-250-gt-swb-california-spider-by-scaglietti/)

One sentence, three verifiable claims, one adjective per claim. It is a **thesis statement** — every
word below it is evidence.

### 4.6 Photography

**Counts from on-page labels:** Bonhams DB4GT "VIEW ALL PHOTOS (68)"; Bonhams Bentley S-Type 44;
Broad Arrow 275 GTB **100**. **Roughly 50–100 images per car is the top-tier standard.**

**Backgrounds are location, not studio** (images downloaded and inspected):

- **RM** — aerial/drone of the car on a mountain hairpin, plus **extreme macro of the cast "FERRARI"
  engine marking and the stamped number**. RM shoots the _numbers_ as hero images.
- **Broad Arrow** — dead-square rear elevation on a Paris street against a Haussmann façade.
  Environmental, composed with orthographic rigour.
- **Bonhams** — pure side profile on a concrete pad in flat overcast UK light; and in the gallery tail,
  **low-resolution snapshots of the bare underside and suspension, clearly from the restoration**. The
  gallery deliberately degrades in production value as it moves from presentation to evidence. That is
  a licence to publish DPM's real in-progress photography next to the finished hero shots.

[Barrett-Jackson's consignor guide](https://www.barrett-jackson.com/consignment/photography-guides)
states the canonical shot list verbatim — "Front 3/4 view / Rear 3/4 view / Side view / Interior view /
Engine view" — with lighting: "Take photos in open shade, overcast weather, or late afternoon sunlight
if possible." **Full inferred shot list** from the galleries: 3/4 front, both profiles, 3/4 rear, dead
front, dead rear, wheels, badges, interior wide, dash, gauges, seats, boot, engine bay wide, engine
details, **chassis/engine number stampings in macro**, underside, tool kit, documentation laid flat.

### 4.7 The history file — never "fully documented"

> "The accompanying extensive history file contains a substantial quantity of invoices for servicing,
> repairs and maintenance dating back to the late 1970s… These include bills for a restoration
> undertaken by David Scott-Moncrieff & Son Ltd in 1981/1982… In 2011 the engine was completely rebuilt
> by English Automotive Services Ltd at a cost in excess of £35,000 (bills on file)."
> — [Bonhams, Lot 168P](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/28312/lot/168P/1956-bentley-s-type-continental-sports-saloon-chassis-no-bc4lbg/)

> "The extensive file with the car, comprising three full leather-bound volumes, charts photos of the
> car from the 1920s onwards." — [Bonhams, Lot 353](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/24876/lot/353/1926-bentley-3-litre-speed-model-sports-tourer-chassis-no-de1220-body-no-1292-engine-no-de1225-see-text/)

Documentation is **enumerated as discrete named artefacts with physical extent** — "bills on file",
"three full leather-bound volumes" — and the authenticating expert is named as a person (Marcel
Massini, Dr Clare Hay). **DPM's "fully documented restorations" is currently an adjective. The
auction-house version of the same promise is a list.**

### 4.8 Where the pattern fails for a restorer — do NOT borrow

1. **The marque-history essay.** RM's `CALIFORNIA EVOLUTION` educates a buyer about a marque. DPM's
   prospect already knows what a DB5 is, and it buries DPM's own work under 800 words about somebody
   else's achievement. Keep the car-specific chapter; drop the marque essay.
2. **The valorisation of non-intervention.** The auction value system rewards untouchedness —
   "sympathetically maintained", "retains", "preserving the original sub-frames". Lifted uncritically,
   that register makes DPM's own work read as _damage_. Reframe: what the houses actually esteem is
   **evidenced, reference-checked, reversible** intervention (the sister-car MS3926 method), not the
   absence of it.
3. **The terminal grammar.** Every lot page closes a transaction — "worthy of any collection". A
   restorer's project page must end _open_, at the start of a relationship. This section has to be
   invented; the auction pattern offers nothing for it.

### Failure modes — what makes a process page boring

- **The four-icon strip.** "Consult → Strip → Paint → Deliver" with line icons. A _summary_ of
  documentation presented _instead of_ documentation. Every competitor has the identical one.
- **One page instead of a body of work.** `/our-process` is a claim; a hundred dated posts are
  evidence. The unit of trust is the archive, not the page.
- **Anonymous brand voice.** "We pride ourselves on meticulous attention to detail" versus "it's just
  me and my shears getting to work."
- **Adjectives where numbers belong.** "Painstaking", "meticulous", "however long it takes" are
  unfalsifiable and therefore unpersuasive.
- **Finished-object-only galleries.** The car under studio lights on a black sweep is the least
  distinctive image in the sector. The bare shell on the rotisserie is what only DPM has.
- **Before/after with nothing between.** Two images imply a transaction. The middle 500 images are the
  product.
- **No hands, no faces.** Empty workshop wide-shots read as stock.
- **Undated, unattributed, unordered.** No date = no proof. No name = nobody to trust. No sequence = no
  reason to keep reading.
- **The abandoned blog.** A "Latest News" whose newest post is 2019 actively damages credibility.
  Weekly-and-boring beats brilliant-and-dead — The Explora has published every Tuesday for a decade.
- **The update with no photograph.** Royal Huisman's dated milestone posts run with no imagery. If it
  isn't photographed, it didn't happen.
- **Music-and-montage video with no words.** Slow-motion sparks over a piano track says "we bought a
  film". A craftsman explaining what he is doing says "we make things."
- **Documentation hidden behind a nav label nobody clicks.** Spirit Yachts buries boatbuilder diaries
  in "News"; Gaziano & Girling has nine journal categories and no journal. If documentation is the
  product, it belongs in the primary nav and on the homepage.
- **Over-styling.** Colour-graded shots of a "workshop" nobody works in. The Explora and the Notebook
  are visibly real rooms with visibly real mess, and that is exactly why they convince.

### Typography: the `opsz` question, resolved

**Axes checked against Google Fonts' own metadata endpoint** (`fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts/<Family>`),
which returns the real axis list — not specimen marketing copy. Verified 26 Aug 2026:

| Family               | Axes actually present                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Newsreader**       | `opsz` **6–72**, `wght` 200–800                      |
| **Playfair**         | `opsz` **5–1200**, `wdth` 87.5–112.5, `wght` 300–900 |
| **Playfair Display** | `wght` 400–900 — **no `opsz`**                       |
| **Inter**            | `opsz` **14–32**, `wght` 100–900                     |
| **Fraunces**         | `opsz` 9–144, `wght` 100–900, plus `SOFT` and `WONK` |

Three findings that would otherwise get quoted back at us:

1. **Playfair Display ≠ Playfair.** They are different families and only the shorter name carries
   `opsz`. Specifying "Playfair Display for its optical sizing" specifies the wrong font by one word.
2. **Inter's `opsz` caps at 32.** Above 32pt a 96px hero gets exactly the same outlines as a 32px one.
   It is a small-text legibility refinement, **not a display-contrast axis** — do not sell it as one.
3. **High-end commercial foundries generally ship optical sizes as separate licensed families, not as
   an axis** — Klim splits Tiempos into Text/Headline/Fine, and Canela and GT Sectra follow the same
   shape. **UNVERIFIED NEGATIVE:** foundries don't publish machine-readable axis data, so confirm with
   Klim/Grilli directly before quoting this. But do not write a brief that assumes buying Canela gets
   you an axis.

**Working recommendation: Newsreader + Inter.** Newsreader's 6–72 `opsz` range genuinely works across
caption-to-display, its 200–800 weights cover hairline standfirst to heavy plate-caption, and its
slightly ink-trapped news character reads as _archive_ and _provenance_ rather than fashion — the
closest open-source analogue to the Tiempos/Sectra register the §3 references use. Drop any requirement
that the grotesque also have a display-range `opsz`; no restrained Söhne-register grotesque on Google
Fonts has one. Add **Playfair** at `opsz` 144+ if Direction A or C wants a coachbuilder's-brass-plate
moment.

**`next/font` semantics that the docs do not state.** `axes` only selects _which axes get downloaded_ —
you cannot assign a value through it; the full range is always requested and you set values in CSS.
`axes` requires a variable font and you must **omit `weight` or set `weight: 'variable'`** (pinning
`weight: '400'` alongside `axes` is a build error). `wght` is filtered out of valid axis names. Fonts
are still self-hosted at build time — _"No requests are sent to Google by the browser."_ And
**`adjustFontFallback` defaults to `true`**, generating metric-matched `size-adjust`/`ascent-override`
fallbacks: it is the single biggest CLS lever on the page and it is already on — don't switch it off.

```ts
const newsreader = Newsreader({
  subsets: ["latin"],
  axes: ["opsz"],
  display: "swap",
  variable: "--font-serif",
});
```

**Footgun:** `font-variation-settings` resolves _after_ `font-optical-sizing`, so writing
`font-variation-settings: 'wght' 500` **silently wipes optical sizing**. Prefer `font-weight` and leave
`font-optical-sizing` at its default `auto`.

### Four editorial-grid traps

- **`text-wrap: balance` only operates on ≤6 wrapped lines in Chromium and ≤10 in Firefox** (MDN,
  verified). Scope it to headings and pull quotes; Chrome explicitly warns against `* { text-wrap:
balance }`.
- **Shorthand/longhand version trap:** `text-wrap: balance` is Chrome 114 but `text-wrap-style:
balance` is Chrome 130. Quoting the wrong one understates support by sixteen releases.
- **`text-wrap: pretty` is not one feature.** WebKit runs a genuine full-paragraph optimiser (evening
  the rag, reducing hyphenation); Chrome's is _"currently focused on orphans"_, adjusting only the last
  few lines; **Firefox has not shipped it**. The same declaration therefore produces materially
  different typography in each engine. For a brand where typography _is_ the product, treat it as
  progressive enhancement — never art-direct against it.
- **The CLS hazard is `container-type`, not the query.** `container-type: size` applies containment in
  both axes, so the element stops sizing to its content and collapses unless given an explicit block
  size. **Use `inline-size`.** And where no eligible container exists, `cqi`/`cqb` silently fall back to
  **small viewport units** — the same `svh` failure already documented in this repo's CLAUDE.md,
  arriving through a different door.

### House rules already verified in this monorepo — apply regardless of direction

These are not general advice; they are bugs this codebase has already been bitten by, with the fix in
tree.

- **`<video autoPlay>` ignores `preload="metadata"`.** Autoplay forces the browser to start buffering,
  so `preload` only governs behaviour when autoplay is absent. DCS's homepage eagerly fetched 7
  below-fold videos totalling 9.7MB, inflating simulated mobile LCP to ~4.3s even though the LCP
  element was server-rendered text. Gate loading behind `IntersectionObserver`.
- **Gate the `poster` too, not just `src`.** `<video poster>` has no `loading="lazy"` equivalent and is
  fetched eagerly the instant the element is parsed. A first pass at the DCS fix gated only `src` and
  still put all 7 posters on the wire. Reference implementation, read in tree:
  `sites/dcs/components/home/lazy-video.tsx` (uses `rootMargin: '200px'`).
- **CSP: `base-template` ships with no `media-src`.** CSP falls back to `default-src 'self'`, so the
  first `<video>` anyone adds is silently blocked — an empty box, no visible console error. Add
  `media-src 'self' https://*.r2.dev;` and cover it with a test that imports the real `headers()` array.
- **Third-party stylesheets in the root layout are render-blocking on every route that layout covers**,
  including routes that never use them. Load non-critical ones after mount from a client component.
- **Sticky sections break in-page anchors.** A `position: sticky` element reports its _pinned_
  position, so once you scroll past it `offsetTop` reads as 0 and native anchor navigation does
  nothing. Also: the last item in a sticky stack silently never pins, because it has no in-flow content
  after it — neither `margin-bottom` on the element nor `padding-bottom` on the container gives it any
  room.
- **Never set `tabular-nums` — or a monospaced face — on anything containing a thousands comma.**
  `£1,995` renders as `£1 , 995`. It inherits, so check `font-variant-numeric` _and_ `font-family` up
  the ancestor chain. Directly relevant here: restoration costs and auction estimates are exactly the
  comma'd figures this corrupts.
- **Use `lvh`, not `svh`,** for any section that must always cover the viewport.

### The accessibility floor

`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` must switch off items 1, 2, 3, 4 and 10 entirely, and reduce 9 and 11
to a plain crossfade or nothing. The Next.js docs give the recipe verbatim for view transitions:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  ::view-transition-old(*),
  ::view-transition-new(*),
  ::view-transition-group(*) {
    animation-duration: 0s !important;
    animation-delay: 0s !important;
  }
}
```

Their own note is the right principle for the whole site: **directional movement across the viewport is
the most common motion-sensitivity trigger; morphs, crossfades and opacity changes carry much less
risk.** So the design should prefer opacity and scale over translation wherever it can.

### What actually beats Halcyon

Not one of these techniques, individually. Halcyon's measured weaknesses are a **720p** hero and a
**stock fade on 30 elements**. The cheapest decisive win is a properly encoded, properly gated
full-bleed hero (item 8) plus editorial grid discipline (item 6) plus **one** well-authored reveal
(item 11) — all three are ACHIEVABLE with no CWV risk. Everything in the RISKY column is a
differentiator to spend on _once_, in one place, on the thing DPM actually sells: paint depth.

**A useful negative result on view transitions.** A grep of production stylesheets on porsche.com,
astonmartin.com, bringatrailer.com and several developer-audience sites found **no view-transition CSS
on any major luxury automotive site reachable**; it was confirmed in production only on developer
blogs. So calling view transitions an industry standard would be false — calling them a
**differentiator** is accurate.

### One premise in the brief to correct

The brief specifies **Next.js 15**. npm `latest` is **16.3.3** (verified 26 Aug 2026, `npm view next
dist-tags`), Next 16 is stable, and this repo's own docs already reference 16.3.3. Two consequences:
Next 16 no longer overrides `scroll-behavior` during navigation by default (restore with
`data-scroll-behavior="smooth"` on `<html>`), which interacts with both view transitions and the
sticky-anchor bug already in CLAUDE.md. **A 2026 positioning document pinned to Next 15 dates itself on
page one** — write it against 16.

## 7. Three candidate art directions

Deliberately distinct on three axes: **ground** (paper / black / saturated colour), **lead content**
(document / surface / person), and **motion** (near-zero / scroll-scrubbed / slow reveal). All named
typefaces were **observed in live use** in the captures above; none is asserted from memory. Licensing
must be checked before any of them ships.

### Direction A — "The Catalogue"

The auction lot page, built properly, for the first time in this trade. Ground is paper, not black:
a warm near-white with black ink and one oxidised accent. Every restoration is a **lot** — an epithet
line, an identity block as a labelled table (chassis, engine, body, registration, colour code, paint
system), an intervention ladder in Gooding's dated-rung format, itemised originality, named hands with
their towns, and a documentation index that enumerates the file as physical artefacts. The homepage is
an index of lots and nothing else — no hero video, no services grid, no "why choose us". Project pages
run 50–100 images and are unashamedly long; a "24 MIN READ" stamp is a feature. The bravery here is
subtractive: it is the only direction in which DPM ships a site with **no hero image at all**, opening
instead on a contents page.

- **Palette:** paper `#F4F1EA` · ink `#16150F` · rule `#C9C3B4` · single accent, oxide red `#7B2631`
  (observed at Purdey) used only for lot numbers and awards.
- **Type:** high-contrast serif display + neutral grotesque text. _Reference_ pairing is A Collected
  Man's (GT Alpina Fine + Helvetica Now Text), set 52–65px/300 uppercase for titles — both are
  commercial licences. _Buildable now_ per §6: **Newsreader** (`opsz` 6–72, `wght` 200–800) for
  display and **Inter** for spec tables and captions; add **Playfair** at `opsz` 144+ for lot numbers
  if a brass-plate register is wanted. Hemmerle's one-family alternative also works here.
- **Motion:** near-zero. Cross-fades, a sticky image column against scrolling text, and nothing else.
  `prefers-reduced-motion` changes almost nothing because there is almost nothing to reduce. Note the
  sticky-column trap in §6's house rules — the last panel in a sticky stack never pins, and sticky
  sections break in-page anchor links.
- **Draws from:** RM Sotheby's / Gooding / Bonhams lot pages, A Collected Man, Hemmerle, Purdey's
  History Certificates.

### Direction B — "Wet Coat" _(the brave one)_

The site is a painted surface. Ground is near-black and the **only light in the design is a single
specular highlight that the visitor moves**. Scroll does not move content past a camera; scroll moves
the light across the panel. Paint depth — the thing DPM actually sells, and the thing no competitor
photographs well — becomes the interaction model rather than a gallery subject. A project opens as a
full-bleed macro of one panel with the highlight raking across it as you scroll, resolving into the
whole car only at the end of the section. Colour is inherited **per project from the car being shown**:
the accent token is the actual paint code, so a Healey page is British Racing Green and an E-Type page
is Opalescent Silver Blue, and the site re-skins itself car by car. This is the direction that needs
the technique menu in §6 to sign it off, and the one that fails hardest if executed as stock parallax.

- **Palette:** ground `#0B0B0C` · surface `#141416` · bone `#E8E4DC` · per-project accent drawn from
  the paint code.
- **Type:** large tight-tracked grotesque + a text serif — the Westley Richards logic (headings
  36–75px/300 at **−1.44px** tracking, observed) or Spirit Yachts' 75px/300 uppercase at −1.5px, over
  miller-banner-class serif for body.
- **Motion:** scroll-scrubbed light; everything else static. Reduced-motion serves a single well-lit
  still and loses nothing.
- **Technical route, per §6:** this is the one place to spend the RISKY budget. Preferred build is a
  single full-screen fragment shader over one high-resolution still, via **`ogl` 1.0.11** rather than
  three.js (far smaller; note its last publish was Jan 2025 — maintenance risk), dynamically imported,
  desktop-gated, with a static image fallback. The scroll driver is `IntersectionObserver` + rAF, not
  a smooth-scroll library. **Not** scroll-scrubbed video (§6 item 1: AVOID). A CSS-mask-and-gradient
  fake of the same highlight should be prototyped first — if it convinces, the shader is unnecessary.
- **Draws from:** Spirit Yachts' black field, Roger W. Smith's nerve, Devialet's product-surface
  treatment, Halcyon's ambition executed at ten times the fidelity.

### Direction C — "The Green Room"

The Roger W. Smith move, made British and made warm. No photograph above the fold: a saturated field
of deep racing green, the wordmark small in brass, and one serif sentence — _Artists of Automotive
Restoration_ — at 66px, weight 250. The site then becomes a **maker's journal**: Dave, Ellis and Paul
each byline their own posts, in first person, with their own archive pages; a numbered stage-set names
the seven or nine stages of a DPM restoration in DPM's own language; a materials index swatches every
paint, primer and finish they work in; and a tool portrait series photographs the guns, blocks and
rubbing pads they actually use. Where A is institutional and B is sensory, C is **human** — and it is
the direction that most directly answers "we do everything hand-crafted in house."

- **Palette:** ground deep olive-green `#32412E` (observed at Roger W. Smith) · bone `#DFDDD6` · brass
  `#AC7E37` · cream secondary ground `#EFEAE3` (observed at A&S The Notebook) · oxblood `#180000`.
- **Type:** old-style serif + humanist sans — the Roger W. Smith pairing (Cambon + Gilroy) or the
  Goodwood pairing (EB Garamond + Gill Sans Nova, both observed), avoiding the Revival's +8.4px
  period-poster tracking, which would read as pastiche. **Newsreader + Inter** is the buildable
  equivalent and carries a real `opsz` axis, which matters most in this direction because the journal
  runs long-form text at several sizes.
- **Motion:** slow, few, deliberate reveals — GSAP ScrollTrigger (free for commercial use, §6 item 11),
  one reveal type, real stagger, on few elements. **No smooth-scroll library**: §6 item 10 rules Lenis
  out on INP and accessibility grounds, which means this direction deliberately _declines_ one of the
  things Roger W. Smith does. View transitions between project pages (§6 item 9) suit this direction
  best, because the archive is browsed rather than scrolled.
- **Draws from:** Roger W. Smith, Anderson & Sheppard's Notebook, Westley Richards' Explora,
  Sebastian Cox's Material Sourcebook.

**Cross-cutting, whichever wins:** the hero must work with no video (David's clip does not exist yet);
project pages carry the identity block and the intervention ladder in all three; and the Halcyon
paintwork credential is named only once David confirms Halcyon are happy to be named.
