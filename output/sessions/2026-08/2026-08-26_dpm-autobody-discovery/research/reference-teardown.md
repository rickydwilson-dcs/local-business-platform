## 1. Halcyon — halcyon.works — _the closest match to what David is asking for_

DPM does Halcyon's paintwork, so David knows this site and this standard from the inside.

- **Nav:** minimal horizontal — HOME · ABOUT · CARS · NEWSROOM · More… with a `CONNECT` CTA pinned right.
- **Hero:** tagline over an image carousel — **"TIMELESS ELEGANCE, REMASTERED"**.
- **Palette:** dark. Blacks and deep greys, white type, high contrast; colour comes only from the cars.
- **Type:** large all-caps headline treatments.
- **Rhythm:** a repeating unit — headline, short paragraph, image carousel, discovery link — used over
  and over. Sections: "REAWAKENING THE SPIRIT", "PRINCIPLES IN MOTION", then rotating attribute
  modules **HANDCRAFTED / REMASTERED / BESPOKE**, then "TAKING THE BEST", "MAKING IT BETTER",
  "UNIQUE TO YOU".
- **Photography:** heavy — multiple angles, interiors, process shots, warm lighting.

**Steal:** the dark ground, the repeating headline+carousel unit, the three-word attribute triplet
(DPM's own would be something like HANDCRAFTED / IN-HOUSE / DOCUMENTED).

## 2. Eagle — eaglegb.com

- **Structure:** flagship-product-led. "Discover the Eagle Lightweight GTR" repeats as the dominant CTA;
  secondary nav for Special Editions, E-Types for Sale, Company, News.
- **Hero:** one hero car dominating the page hierarchy.
- **Copy tone:** enthusiast-first, conversational, confident —
  > "It's a driving experience that lives up to the looks. And that's saying something."
  > "THE HOME OF THE EAGLE E-TYPE"
- **Proof:** leans hard on third-party validation — embedded video reviews from motoring journalists
  (Henry Catchpole, Dan Prosser), award news ("Lightweight GTR triumphs at the 2025 International
  Historic Motoring Awards").

**Steal:** third-party proof as a first-class homepage section. DPM's equivalent is the Halcyon
paintwork relationship, the NEC Classic Motor Show presence, and the DB6 press. **Not** the
flagship-product structure — Eagle sells its own cars, DPM sells a service.

## What the three have in common

1. **Restraint.** Few colours, lots of black or white space, one idea per screen.
2. **The work carries the page.** Photography is large, uncropped and given room; copy is short.
3. **A single memorable line** at the top: "Because everything matters" / "TIMELESS ELEGANCE,
   REMASTERED" / "THE HOME OF THE EAGLE E-TYPE". **DPM already has one: "Artists of Automotive
   Restoration."**
4. **Heritage/credential proof** early — a date, an award, a named client.
5. **No stock imagery, no gradients, no busy UI.**

## Implication for DPM

The elegance David is asking for is mostly _subtraction_: fewer sections, bigger pictures, shorter
sentences, and a dark restrained palette that lets paintwork read. Since paintwork is literally what
DPM sells, the site's whole visual argument should be **surface** — reflection, depth, light rolling
over a panel. That is a real differentiator against all three references, none of which are paint
specialists.
