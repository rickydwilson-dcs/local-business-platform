## The Core Problem Being Solved

Most small business owners:

- Don't know where to start with getting a website
- Can't articulate what they want it to look like
- Don't have many (or any) professional images
- Don't know how to write copy or content for their industry
- Are overwhelmed by technical choices (hosting, platforms, security)
- Can't afford an agency but don't want a DIY builder

**DCS fills this gap.** They don't just build websites — they handle the thinking, the writing, the design decisions, and the ongoing maintenance. The business owner provides some basic information; DCS turns it into a polished, professional online presence.

The result is the same quality you'd get from a big agency, but with someone who actually knows your business and is personally invested in getting it right.

1. **You're hiring a person, not a company** — Ricky does the work; nothing is outsourced or offshored
2. **Agency-quality output** — professional design, well-written copy, fast and secure builds
3. **Low effort for the client** — DCS extracts what they need through conversation, not lengthy briefs

6. **All-in pricing** — setup fee + low monthly retainer covers hosting, security, updates, and support
7. **Ongoing relationship** — not a hand-off, a long-term managed service

## Strategic Direction (Future State)

DCS is transitioning away from WordPress as the delivery platform toward a proprietary **local business platform** — a custom Next.js/Tailwind stack with pre-built themes and content pipelines. This allows:

- Faster delivery per client
- Better performance (static builds vs. WordPress CMS overhead)
- More consistent design quality across clients
- Scalable white-label model

The website itself should begin to reflect this shift — positioning DCS as a modern, technology-forward small business partner, not just a "WordPress guy". WordPress should be mentioned as a legacy capability, not a core identity.

## Target Audience

**Primary target:** UK tradespeople and local service businesses — plumbers, electricians, builders, scaffolders, gardeners, decorators, roofers, handymen, locksmiths, flooring fitters, and anyone who does skilled work and needs customers to find them.

**Secondary (can/will serve, not actively marketed):** Other sole traders and micro-businesses — tutors, therapists, coaches, salons, studios. Show in portfolio to demonstrate range, but don't lead with them.

**Not the focus:** eCommerce. DCS can build eCommerce sites and has done so (significant portfolio), but they are more time-intensive, lower-margin, and outside the core positioning. Show in portfolio, don't promote as a primary offer.

**Client profile:**

- Time-poor — they're out on jobs, not sitting at a desk
- Not technical — never want to touch a website backend
- Has a real business but no web presence, or an embarrassing outdated one
- Knows they need a website but has been putting it off (for years)
- Price-conscious but not cheap — will pay for something professional that works and is looked after
- Gets most work by word of mouth today — wants to add "being findable on Google" to that

## Tone of Voice

- **Plain-spoken** — no jargon, no agency-speak
- **Confident, not apologetic** — the work is genuinely good; own it
- **Personal** — "I" not "we" where appropriate; this is one person, and that's a strength not a weakness
- **Reassuring** — we've done this many times, we'll handle it
- **Honest** — transparent pricing, no surprises
- **Warm but professional** — approachable, not casual; friendly, not sloppy

**The balance to strike:** DCS is not a faceless agency (that's a selling point), but the output is indistinguishable from one (that's also a selling point). Never downplay quality to seem humble. Never hide the personal nature to seem bigger.

**Avoid:** Corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "bespoke solutions", "leveraging"), vague promises ("transform your business"), self-deprecating language ("just a small operation", "nothing fancy"), over-designed complexity.

**Service model:** DCS owns and maintains the site. Pay-monthly clients are paying for an ongoing service — if they stop paying, the site comes down. This is not a product handoff; it is a managed service.

### Add-Ons

- Blog post writing: £75/post
- Custom theme design: £350
- Google Ads setup + management: £200/month + ad spend
- Monthly SEO retainer: £200/month
- Photography coordination: at cost

### What Every Site Gets (regardless of tier)

Full service pages, location pages, contact form, mobile-responsive design, local SEO (Schema, meta, sitemap), custom domain, SSL, hosting, security updates, uptime monitoring. Tiers differ on **ongoing service level**, not site size or page count.

### What Clients Do NOT Get

A CMS. There is no WordPress dashboard, no backend login, no "login and edit it yourself." DCS delivers a website as a managed service. Content updates, blog posts, and changes go through DCS. This is a deliberate choice — the client's job is to run their business, not to maintain a website.

## The New Site Must Accomplish

1. Clearly communicate the low-effort value proposition in the first 10 seconds
2. Demonstrate credibility through portfolio (real work, real businesses)
3. Show "what you get" without overwhelming with features — emphasise the managed service model, not a list of features
4. Make it easy to take the first step (low-commitment inquiry)
5. Build trust through transparency (pricing, process, examples of end output)
6. The "Examples" section is a key differentiator — potential clients can browse real theme options and imagine their own site
7. Never mention the underlying platform, the tech stack, or Next.js — clients don't care and shouldn't need to
