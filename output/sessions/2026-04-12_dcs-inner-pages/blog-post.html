Why Tradespeople Need a Website in 2025

12 April 2025·By Ricky Wilson·5 min read

If you're a tradesperson running a business in the UK, you've probably heard "you need a website" more times than you can count. But most of the advice out there is vague. This post explains exactly why it matters, what you're losing without one, and what a proper website actually does for trade businesses in 2025.

Most customers search before they call

According to Google's own consumer research, over 70% of people search online before hiring a local tradesperson. That number is higher for first-time customers — people who don't have a personal recommendation. These are the leads that come from nowhere and that keep your diary full during quieter months.

If someone searches "plumber Lewes" or "electrician East Sussex" and you don't have a website, you simply don't exist to them. You're not even in the running. The work goes to whoever shows up on page one of Google — and that person has a website.

Google visibility is compounding

Every piece of content on your website — service pages, location pages, blog posts — is indexed by Google and continues working for you around the clock without you doing anything. A page you published two years ago can still generate enquiries today.

The power of local SEO

Local SEO is about showing up when someone in your area searches for your service. A properly built website with schema markup, location-specific pages, and the right keyword structure can get a trade business appearing in Google search results and Google Maps for dozens of relevant search terms — not just your business name, but service + location combinations that prospects are actively searching for.

Your competitors already have one

In 2025, a website is table stakes for any serious trade business. The tradespeople winning the most work online aren't doing anything exotic — they have a clean, fast, mobile-friendly website with clear contact details, a list of services, and a few good reviews. That's it. The bar is low, which means there's still a real opportunity if you get there before the competition in your area does.

Generating enquiries while you sleep

Your website works evenings and weekends when you can't answer the phone. A customer browsing at 10pm can fill in a contact form and wake up to find you've already started the conversation. For emergency trades (plumbers, electricians, locksmiths), this is especially powerful — someone with a burst pipe at midnight will Google for help, and your contact form becomes a lead capture tool that never switches off.

A well-configured site can also display your Google reviews, show photos of recent work, and answer common questions before a prospect even picks up the phone. By the time they call, they already trust you. The conversion rate from enquiry to quote improves noticeably.

Professional credibility

A Gmail address is a credibility killer. Quoting from yourname@gmail.com signals to customers that you're a part-timer, not a professional business. A domain email (yourname@yourcompany.co.uk) costs almost nothing extra and makes a material difference to first impressions.

The same goes for your website's design. A slow, poorly designed site creates doubt. A fast, professional site creates trust before you've said a word. In a market where customers are choosing between you and several competitors, perception matters.
