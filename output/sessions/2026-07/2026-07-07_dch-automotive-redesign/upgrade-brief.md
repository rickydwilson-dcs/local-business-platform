# DCH Automotive — Redesign & Car Remaps Upgrade Brief

**Date:** 2026-07-07
**Source:** Parallel sub-agent research across 8 competitor/adjacent sites (2 categories)
**Client brief:** [tasks/clients/dch-automotive.md](../../../../tasks/clients/dch-automotive.md)

---

## 1. Purpose

DCH Automotive is migrating from a standalone WordPress site to LBP with a full visual redesign, and simultaneously launching a new **Car Remaps** service line aimed at corporate fleet customers (fuel efficiency and/or extra power) — explicitly not the performance-tuning/enthusiast market. This brief translates competitor research into concrete direction for both the redesign and the new service.

**Hard constraint carried into every recommendation below:** orange, black, and white remain the brand palette. Everything else — layout, typography, imagery, component style — is open.

---

## 2. Current Site Assessment (existing WordPress)

- Nav: Home, Vehicle Security (Trackers, Plant Machinery, Alarms, Immobiliser), Bike Security, Tow Bars, Parking Aids, Fleet Solutions, Accessories, Contact Us
- Strong certification set already held: City & Guilds, IMI, Thatcham, Autowatch, Witter, Smartrack, Thinkware
- **No testimonials, no case studies, no fleet-specific trust content** despite Fleet Solutions being a named service and the business explicitly serving the motor trade
- Purely informational architecture — no visible pricing, no lead-qualification form, no differentiated fleet vs. consumer journey

This last point turns out to be the single biggest opportunity: **every competitor researched has the same gap.**

## 4. Cross-Cutting Findings

1. **No competitor in either category shows real testimonials or case studies on their actual service pages.** This is a wide-open differentiation lane for DCH, which already has motor-trade relationships (a ready-made source of trade testimonials) and a stronger certification set than several competitors claim.
2. **Thatcham accreditation is the universal trust anchor** across every vehicle-security competitor. DCH already holds it, plus Autowatch/Witter/Smartrack/Thinkware approvals — these should be surfaced as prominently as SmartFits/Vehicle Track Solutions do, not buried in a footer line.
3. **Fleet remap providers consistently fail to separate fleet content from enthusiast branding** — either bolting a corporate page onto a supercar-hero site (Kustom, Layton) or not bothering to differentiate at all (Fast Lane). JF Automotive is the only one that fully commits to a split vocabulary, and even they lack a fleet-specific enquiry form or case studies.
4. **No fleet remap competitor has built an interactive savings calculator**, despite two of them (Kustom, HGV Fuel Saving) having exactly the worked-example numbers that would power one. This is a genuine white-space feature.
5. **Progressive/qualifying lead forms (Rewire's 4-step "Request Demo") clearly outperform generic contact forms** for fleet buyers — they signal seriousness and pre-qualify the lead before a phone call.
6. Every competitor in the security category uses a **navy/blue "trust" palette** — DCH's orange/black/white is a genuine visual differentiator in this category, not a constraint to work around. Blue reads "safe but generic"; orange (used with discipline, not garishly) can read "confident, distinct, energetic" if the redesign leans into it deliberately rather than treating the palette as a limitation.

## 5. Recommendations

### Trust & credibility architecture

- Build a **certifications/accreditations stat-bar** (City & Guilds, IMI, Thatcham, Autowatch, Witter, Smartrack, Thinkware) as a persistent, scannable strip — not a footer afterthought.
- Add a genuine **testimonials + case studies section**, prioritising trade/fleet references (DCH's motor-trade relationships are an asset none of the researched competitors are using).
- Consider a **"Switch to Us"**-style page for Fleet Solutions, modelled on Fleetview's de-risking journey — directly addresses fleet managers currently locked into an incumbent installer.

### Information architecture

- Keep navigation organized by **customer need** (Vehicle Security, Bike Security, Tow Bars, Parking Aids, Fleet Solutions, Accessories, **Car Remaps**), not by brand/manufacturer name — avoid SmartFits' mistake of a jargon-first nav.
- Fork the **Fleet Solutions** and **Car Remaps** sections into a clearly distinct, fleet/trade-oriented IA branch (own hero imagery, own CTA, own proof points) — never sharing a template with consumer-facing pages.

### Conversion pattern

- Replace the generic "Contact Us" form with a **progressive, qualifying enquiry form** for fleet/trade leads (vehicle type → fleet size → services of interest → contact details), modelled on Rewire Security's 4-step pattern.
- Retain a simple phone-first path for individual consumer enquiries (tow bars, parking aids, bike security).

### Car Remaps — positioning & page design

- **Total vocabulary split from any enthusiast register.** Never use "power," "WOW," "driving experience," 0-60 times, or bhp bragging anywhere Car Remaps is discussed. Stick to: running costs, £-per-mile, CO2 reduction, MPG improvement, ROI/payback period, fleet-size scalability.
- Use **commercial-vehicle imagery only** (vans, light commercials, plant/agricultural where relevant) — never adjacent to sports-car or supercar photography anywhere in the site's nav path, unlike Layton's one-click-away supercar hero problem.
- Build the **interactive fuel-savings calculator** (fleet size × annual mileage × fuel cost × expected % saving) that no competitor has executed — this is the single clearest white-space feature identified in this research.
- Borrow JF Automotive's **scale-flex credibility line** ("from single vehicles to fleets of any size") and free-assessment/no-obligation CTA over a hard "book a remap" ask.
- Surface a **guarantee/warranty** line (several competitors use lifetime map warranty + money-back guarantee as trust anchors) — confirm with client what DCH can actually offer here.

### Visual direction (palette constraint noted, decisions deferred to UX-UI-Max pass)

- Treat orange/black/white as a **differentiator against a sea of navy-blue competitors**, not a limitation to design around apologetically.
- Favour real installation/job photography over stock/graphic imagery — nearly every competitor researched leans on stock or badge-driven visuals; authentic photos of DCH's own installs, vans, and technicians would stand out.
