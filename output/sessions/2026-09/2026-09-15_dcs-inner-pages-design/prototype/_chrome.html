G13 — .btn--ghost, decided

Decision: confirm kit.css §06 as written. home-r9.css:128 writes the ghost button for a light ground (ink text, ink-tinted border); shared.css:67 rewrites it for a dark one (inherit, white border). Neither is wrong — it is the same button on opposite grounds, and grounding the selector resolves it without inventing a value or discarding one.

What needed checking was the four grounds the conflict never mentioned. .p--ink, .p--navy and .p--magenta take the white-bordered rule; .p--white, .p--paper and .p--aqua fall through to the base ink-bordered rule, which is right on all three — aqua is a light ground in this design (kit.css:196 gives it --bar-fg:var(--ink)). The set is complete; there is no seventh case. No CSS change.

G5 — Long-form prose, the complete set

Round 1 gave the measure, the section rhythm and the paragraph (project-post.html:25-28) and stopped. Everything else here is new, and every value in it is borrowed from a pattern that already exists — the h3 is .qa summary's type spec, the blockquote is .cards--2 .card__t's, the inline-link underline is .wpanel__l's 1.5px at .5 alpha, the ordered-list numeral is .step__k's weight and accent, the figure caption is .card__s. The full accounting, including the six values that had no source, is in ../phase2-notes.md.

One structural fix. Round 1 wrote .body{max-width:74ch; padding:… var(--pad)}, and with this design's border-box reset that gutter sits inside the 74ch — the real measure was about 60ch at 1380px and about 40ch at the --pad cap. The numbers are unchanged; the gutter moved out to .sec and the measure to .measure.

The same markup is shown on white and on ink. Rules, bullets, numerals, the blockquote accent and the inline link all swap by the accent rule with no duplicated content.

A sub-heading, one clear step down

The global h3 is clamp(1.5rem,2.6vw,2.2rem), which collides with prose h2's 2.4rem cap — inside a 74ch column the two read as the same level. This is .qa summary's spec instead: an existing pair in the scale that sits a clear step below.

A bulleted list hangs on the same 28px edge the aqua check list uses, so the two line up when a page carries both:

  • Gap is 12px, which is .detail__l's.

A figure in flow takes .card's 18px radius and .card__well's navy backdrop, so an image that has not loaded shows the same ground a card well does rather than a white hole. The caption is .card__s verbatim.

Honesty, as a component. A figure whose media does not exist takes .slot — the designed “awaiting footage” box — not a stand-in screenshot of a real client’s site. Ten of the thirteen case studies need this.

And the one that needs a warning

Inline code is the only monospaced face anywhere in this design. A mono face gives a thousands comma a full digit advance exactly as tabular-nums does, so £1,995 would render as “£1 , 995”. It inherits. Never wrap a price in it. A price belongs in running prose — a larger multi-page build starts from £1,495 — or in .tier__f, which deliberately carries no numeric font styling at all.

.prose code{font-family:ui-monospace,SFMono-Regular,Menlo,Consolas,monospace}

G2 — The page-level footer

.end is the homepage's closing chapter: stretched across a full sticky viewport with clamp(170px,22vh,260px) of top padding. Dropped at the bottom of a service page it reads as a sixth section rather than as the end of the document. .pagefoot keeps the closing move and loses the chapter — the same navy ground, the same oversized .big contact links, the same .end__foot rule and microcopy row, at round 1's own shortened clamp(90px,12vh,140px).

It adds the one thing a footer has that a closing chapter does not: the link map. Per decision D2 the eight location pages live here and not in the primary nav — worth keeping, worth finding, and they must not pull the brand back toward local trades by sitting at the top of every page. Every value is reused: the column gap is .price's, the rule and the distance to it are .end__foot's, the link size, opacity and aqua hover are .menu__foot's.

The section shell, and why the cut still works off the homepage

The signature boundary of this design is a panel's rounded top corners revealing the colour of the pane above. On the homepage that works because .panel is sticky and .cornerfill paints the revealed strip. An inner page is not a chapter stack, so .panel is non-sticky here — and a non-sticky rounded section reveals its parent's background through those corners, which is the body's paper. Every boundary would flash paper.

.sec gets the same cut without the sticky machinery: it pulls itself up over the section above by exactly --r, so the previous section's own colour is what shows through. No cornerfill element, no colour to pass in, and none of chapter-panel.tsx's “the fill is the pane above, not the panel being opened” trap to get wrong. Safe because --r caps at 44px and every block that can precede a .sec has at least 48px of bottom padding to be overlapped into.
