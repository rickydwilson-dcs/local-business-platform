Why tradespeople need a proper website

Most people looking for a tradesperson start with Google. They type in something like “electrician near me” or “local plumber” and pick from the first few results. If you’re not showing up — or if your site looks rough on a phone — you’re losing jobs to someone who just has a better website.

A Facebook page isn’t enough. Checkatrade and MyBuilder send you leads shared with every other tradesperson in your area, and you’re paying for them whether you win the job or not. A website you own means enquiries that come directly to you, at no cost per lead.

But the website needs to actually work. That means loading fast, looking professional on a phone, being clear about what you do and where you cover, and making it easy for someone to get in touch. These things aren’t complicated, but they matter.

What makes a good tradesperson website

A tradesperson website isn’t the same as a website for a shop or a restaurant. Your customers are looking for trust signals fast: are you local, are you qualified, can I see examples of your work, how do I call you right now.

A good tradesperson website does a few things well:

  • It loads in under two seconds on a phone. Slow sites get abandoned before anyone reads a word.
  • It tells visitors immediately what you do and where you work. No guessing.
  • It shows your qualifications, accreditations, and any relevant memberships (Gas Safe, NICEIC, CHAS, etc.).
  • It has clear calls to action — phone number prominent at the top, a short contact form that actually works.
  • It has separate pages for each main service, so Google understands what you offer.
  • It has pages for the towns and areas you cover, so you show up in local searches.

Why bespoke beats a template

Template-based website builders like Wix or Squarespace can produce a serviceable website. But they come with real drawbacks for tradespeople.

First, the SEO. Template platforms often produce bloated code that loads slowly, with limited control over the technical SEO elements that matter for local search. Second, they all look like templates. A visitor can spot a generic Wix site in seconds, and it doesn’t inspire confidence in someone who’s about to let you into their home.

A DCS website is built from the ground up to your brief. It reflects your business — the services you actually offer, the areas you actually cover, the tone that suits your customers. And it’s built on a fast, modern tech stack that performs well in Google’s eyes.

Common questions

How long does it take to build?

Most sites are live within 2–3 weeks of me receiving your content and sign-off. I send you a simple intake form to gather everything I need — business info, services, areas covered, and any photos you have. From there, I handle everything.

What if I want changes after it’s live?

Content updates — adding a new service, updating your phone number, changing a photo — are included in your monthly management. Just send me a message and I’ll make the change, usually within one business day.

Do I need to buy a domain?

No. Domain registration is handled as part of your website setup. If you already own a domain, I can transfer it or point it to your new site — whichever you prefer.

Is it mobile-friendly?

Yes, every site I build is mobile-first. That means it’s designed for phones before anything else, because that’s where most of your enquiries will come from. I test on real devices before going live.

Will it show up on Google?

I build local SEO into every site from the start — service pages, location pages, Schema markup, and a proper sitemap. I’ll also guide you through setting up or optimising your Google Business Profile. You won’t rank overnight, but you’ll be set up correctly from day one.
