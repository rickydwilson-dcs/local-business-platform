## 1. What you asked for

A mobile-friendly website that properly represents Autcobel's brand and the full range of services, with the agency leading and as little input needed from you as possible. {.lead}

The live site doesn't load reliably. On some browsers it doesn't render at all, which means some customers open it and never see the services, sectors or contact details behind it. On top of that, everything sits behind one URL with an in-page menu, so even when it does load, nobody can send a colleague a link to just the gas detection page, or find "Turnkey Electrical & Data" in a search. On a phone specifically, that meant six services competing for space in one long scroll instead of each getting a page of its own.

So could a site built from what's already true, nothing invented to fill a gap, do that: work properly on mobile, and hold up if someone actually checks?

## 2. What we did

We read every word of your live site directly in a browser, including the sections that only exist behind JavaScript-driven, in-page navigation rather than a real URL. Looked at two direct competitors in the same trade, Synecore (retail M&E, rebranding as Turnpower) and KDM Group (nationwide contracting and fit-out), reading their own live navigation and positioning rather than trusting a memory of either.

From that, we built a real fifteen-page structure and a custom set of six hero images: home, two company pages, six services, sectors, contact, and three legal pages, up from your one.

## 3. What we found

Both competitors organise their sites the same way, and it isn't a coincidence. Synecore's live navigation runs About, M&E Services, Maintenance, Sectors, Case Studies, News, Contact Us. KDM Group's runs Home, Who We Are, What We Do, Our Projects, News & Insights, Contact Us. Different order, the same handful of ideas: who they are, what they do, who they do it for, proof they've done it, and how to reach them.

**Your current site already has content for five of those ideas**: Who We Are, Our Approach, Our Services, Sectors We Serve, Contact. It's thin, and it sits behind one URL with in-page navigation that doesn't render reliably (see section 1), which is almost certainly why Google has never properly read or ranked any of it: the sitemap it's been given lists a single page. The one thing both competitors have that you don't, even in thin form, is a case-studies or projects page. We can't build that yet. More on that in section 8.

## 4. The proposition

You needed more content, on pages that could be found and read properly by people and search engines. {.claim}

Splitting one page into real pages doesn't change what Autcobel does. It changes what a visitor, or a search engine, can actually find.

### 4.1 Depth over decoration

Every one of your six services gets its own page: what it includes, who it's for, what actually happens between enquiry and handover, and the questions a buyer would ask before committing. Where we didn't have a real answer, we didn't invent one.

### 4.2 Say only what's checked

Every fact that could be checked against your own site was: the sectors, the phone number and email already sitting in your contact section. Everything we couldn't check (accreditations, named clients) is tracked openly rather than guessed at. See section 6.

## 5. How that shows up in the prototype

- **Six services, six pages,** not six paragraphs stacked on one page.
- **A "who this is for" box on every service page,** tying the service to a real scenario, such as a distribution centre commissioning or a cold-storage conversion, instead of leaving a buyer to guess whether it applies to them.
- **A short FAQ on every service and company page,** answering what a buyer would actually ask (the F-Gas compliance scope, whether a feasibility study is chargeable) up front instead of leaving it for an enquiry call.
- **Sectors cross-linked from every service, and back again.** Someone reading about cold chain sees exactly which two services apply to it; someone reading a service page sees which sectors it's built for.
- **Your own words, not just ours, on Who We Are and Our Approach.** The "foundation of every successful project is trust" line and the five delivery principles are pulled straight from your live site's own copy, not agency drafting.
- **A phone number and email that were already true,** found in your live site's contact section and carried straight across rather than left as a placeholder.

## 6. Health warning

Some of what's on the site isn't from you. The "who this is for" sections, the FAQs, the step-by-step on each service page: we wrote all of that ourselves, based on what a buyer in this trade would want to know. None of it has been checked against you or your site.

Same goes for the founding story on Who We Are and the five-step process on Our Approach. Educated guesses, not confirmed facts.

One thing we left out on purpose: case studies. There's nothing real to put on the page yet, so rather than invent one, we didn't build it.

## 7. What would make it more solid

**Proof: making "50+ projects" and "certified partners" checkable**

Two things on the site already make claims that only you can back up.

1. **Any accreditations you hold.** NICEIC, CHAS, SafeContractor, ISO, F-Gas, whichever apply. The Gas Leak Detection page already says "delivered with certified partners"; naming them turns that from a claim into something a buyer can verify.
2. **One named client or project,** even vague. "50+ projects delivered" is on your own homepage already, and one example gives it somewhere to point.

**Distance: the one page both competitors have that you don't**

3. **A case-studies or projects page.** Neither Synecore's site nor KDM Group's site can be beaten on service list alone: that's the same handful of ideas every site in this trade runs. What actually separates one from the other is proof of delivery, and that's blocked entirely on 1 and 2 above.

## 8. Case studies

We're not writing case studies for a site that has none to draw on. A made-up project doesn't survive a follow-up question, and once someone catches one, nothing else on the site reads as trustworthy.

What we need: an outline of as many real projects as you can give us, even a few paragraphs each, even without the customer's name. We'll turn each one into a finished page.

## 9. Choices we've made for you

- **Cookie Policy stays its own page,** not folded into Privacy Policy. The cookie-consent banner needs somewhere to link directly.
- **"Consultation & Collaboration,"** the fourth pillar named on your live site, is folded into Our Approach rather than getting its own page. It's about how you work with clients, which Our Approach already covers.
- **The three original service pages kept their drafted names** (Turnkey Electrical & Data, Project & Design Management, Temporary Plant Systems) rather than being renamed to match your live site's exact wording for the same three services.

One open call: keep those three names as they are, or match your own site's exact wording (for example, "Electrical & Data Installations" instead of "Turnkey Electrical & Data")? Either works. We picked a side rather than leave it undecided.

## 10. What we need from you, in order

1. **Any accreditations.** NICEIC, CHAS, SafeContractor, ISO, F-Gas, or similar. Tell us which, or confirm there are none.
2. **One named client or project,** even something as vague as "a major UK supermarket chain."
3. **An outline of as many real projects as you can give us, for case studies,** even a few paragraphs each. We'll turn each one into a finished page. No rush on this one.
4. **The naming call in section 9.** Keep the three drafted service names, or match your own site's wording. No rush on this one.
