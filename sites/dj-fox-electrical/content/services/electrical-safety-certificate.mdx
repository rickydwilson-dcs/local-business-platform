## What is an EICR?

An EICR (Electrical Installation Condition Report) is:
- A formal inspection of electrical installation
- Testing of all circuits, outlets, switches, and consumer unit
- Electrical safety certificate for compliance
- Detailed report identifying any faults or concerns
- Professional recommendation for remedial works
- Recognition of installation condition at time of test

Also known as: Electrical Safety Certificate, Periodic Inspection Report, Electrical Condition Report.

## Why You Need an EICR

**Rental Properties (Legal Requirement):**
- Must complete EICR before letting property
- Repeat every 5 years during tenancy
- At tenancy change (new tenants)
- Legal requirement under Electrical Safety Standards 2020
- Failure to comply: fines up to £30,000

**Owner-Occupied Homes:**
- Insurance may require EICR
- Property sales/conveyancing often require certificate
- Planning to do electrical work
- Home over 10 years since last test
- Peace of mind about electrical safety

**Commercial Properties:**
- Health & Safety at Work Act requirement
- Insurance compliance
- Employee safety responsibility
- Regular compliance essential

## Classification of Faults

**Code C1 (Dangerous):**
- Immediate hazard present
- Must be rectified immediately
- Could cause electrocution or fire
- Work must stop until repaired

**Code C2 (Potentially Dangerous):**
- Defect likely to become dangerous
- Should be remedied soon
- Poses significant risk
- Recommend repair within 3 months

**Code C3 (Minor Issue):**
- Not serious but shows wear
- Should be noted for future reference
- Remedial work optional
- Can be addressed during other work

**Code FI (Further Investigation):**
- Unable to fully test circuit
- Requires further professional assessment
- May indicate access or capacity issues

## Legal Requirements for Landlords

**Electrical Safety Standards 2020 requires:**
- EICR before property let
- Repeat every 5 years maximum
- At end of tenancy (test before new tenant)
- Maintain records for 7 years
- Provide copy to tenant within 30 days
- Non-compliance: criminal liability + fines

Compliance is essential for landlords.
