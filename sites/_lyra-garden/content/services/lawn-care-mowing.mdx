  - question: "How often should a lawn be mowed?"
    answer: "During the growing season (April–October), most lawns benefit from mowing every 1–2 weeks depending on grass type and growth rate. In winter, mowing frequency drops significantly. We tailor the visit schedule to your lawn's actual growth rather than a rigid calendar."
  - question: "What is scarification and does my lawn need it?"
    answer: "Scarification is the mechanical removal of thatch — the layer of dead grass, moss, and organic matter that builds up at the base of the grass stems. Heavy thatch prevents water and nutrients from reaching grass roots. Most lawns benefit from annual scarification in autumn or spring."
  - question: "What is lawn aeration?"
    answer: "Aeration involves making holes in the lawn surface to relieve compaction and allow air, water, and nutrients to penetrate deeper into the root zone. Hollow-tine aeration (removing plugs of soil) is most effective for compacted lawns. We recommend this annually for heavily used lawns."
  - question: "Do you offer lawn treatment programmes?"
    answer: "Yes. We offer seasonal treatment programmes covering spring feed, summer feed and weed control, autumn feed, and winter preparation. Treatments are timed to the growing season and East Sussex conditions. We can combine treatments with a regular mowing contract."
  - question: "Can you repair a lawn that has bare patches or moss problems?"
    answer: "Yes. Bare patches are overseeded with appropriate grass seed. Heavy moss is treated chemically then removed by scarification, followed by overseeding. We assess the underlying cause — shade, drainage, compaction — and address it alongside the repair."
