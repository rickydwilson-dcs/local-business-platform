  - question: "What is a planting scheme?"
    answer: "A planting scheme is a designed selection of plants for a specific area — chosen for their height, spread, flowering season, colour palette, soil requirements, and maintenance needs. A good scheme creates interest across all four seasons and is sized correctly so plants fill the space at maturity without overcrowding."
  - question: "How do you select plants for my soil and aspect?"
    answer: "We assess your soil type (clay, sandy, chalky — all common in East Sussex), drainage, aspect (north, south, east, west facing), and light levels before selecting plants. East Sussex soils and conditions suit a wide range of species, but matching plants to the specific site is fundamental to success."
  - question: "Do you supply the plants or do I buy them?"
    answer: "We supply all plants as part of the service, sourced from trade nurseries for quality and value. Buying direct saves you the effort of sourcing and transporting — and we take responsibility for the plants arriving in good condition and being correctly specified."
  - question: "Can you advise on plants for pollinators?"
    answer: "Yes. We design planting schemes that support pollinators — bees, butterflies, and other insects — as a standard consideration. Species like lavender, salvia, allium, verbena, and achillea are heavily used in our schemes for their dual value of visual appeal and wildlife benefit."
  - question: "Do you provide ongoing maintenance for planted borders?"
    answer: "Yes. We offer seasonal maintenance contracts that cover weeding, cutting back, division of established plants, and replacement of any that have not established. A maintenance contract ensures the border develops as designed rather than reverting to weeds."
