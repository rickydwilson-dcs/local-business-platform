## The Project

A Georgian town house in central Lewes — listed Grade II — came with a walled rear garden that retained its original layout but had suffered decades of neglect. The garden walls, brick paths, and some structural planting were original features. The brief was to restore the garden to a high standard while maintaining period authenticity and complying with listed building requirements.

## Heritage Compliance

Works to a listed building's curtilage — which includes the garden — require care even when the building itself isn't being altered. We worked alongside the owner's heritage consultant to confirm which elements were considered historic fabric (the paths, the boundary walls, the mature apple espalier) and which could be renewed.

Several practical limitations followed from this:
- Brick paths were original 18th-century stock brick — replacement bricks had to be matched from a reclamation yard, and the beds were reset with non-hydraulic lime mortar rather than modern cement
- Boundary wall capping stones could not be replaced — they had to be carefully lifted, cleaned, and reset in lime mortar
- No use of powered equipment near the protected apple espalier on the east wall

## Invasive Species Discovery

During initial clearance, Japanese knotweed was identified in the north-west corner — a significant finding. Japanese knotweed requires specialist treatment under the Wildlife and Countryside Act and cannot simply be dug out. We arranged a survey and multi-year herbicide treatment programme through a specialist invasive species contractor. This added to the project timeline but was addressed correctly — improper knotweed disposal is a legal offence.

## TPO Trees

Two mature fig trees in the garden were identified as potentially covered by a Tree Preservation Order. We made the necessary application to Lewes District Council for permission for minor remedial surgery (one deadwood removal and a crown reduction on the other). Consent was received within eight weeks. The surgery was carried out by our qualified arborist.

## Planting Scheme

The planting design was led by period appropriateness. Heritage rose varieties (Rosa gallica, Rosa × damascena cultivars, Rosa 'William Lobb') were selected for the border beds. Box (Buxus sempervirens) edging was established to define the path network. A new pear espalier was established on the south wall to complement the existing apple.

A mown grass path — replacing a weedy and uneven grass strip — was levelled, improved, and sown with a hard-wearing mix appropriate for a formal garden path.

## Result

The restoration preserved all the original features of the garden — the paths, walls, espaliers — while bringing them to a maintained and presentable standard. The planting scheme will take two to three years to fully establish. The Japanese knotweed treatment programme continues on the agreed schedule.
