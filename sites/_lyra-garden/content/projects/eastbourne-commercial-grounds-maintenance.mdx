## The Contract

A property management company managing a 14-unit commercial estate on the outskirts of Eastbourne approached DCS Garden following a change in their grounds maintenance provider. The estate had a mix of office units and light industrial units, totalling approximately 2,500m² of grounds including lawns, hard standing, entrance beds, and approximately 180 metres of boundary hedging.

The previous contractor had been inconsistent — mowing had been missed on multiple occasions during the summer and the hedging had been left untrimmed for over a year when we took the contract on.

## Contract Scope

**Weekly (April–October):** Grass mowing to all lawn areas. Strimming around fence lines, obstacles, and building bases. Litter pick before mowing. Edging of all grass/hard standing interfaces every second visit.

**Monthly:** Inspection and reporting to estate management. Report covers: work completed, any items requiring the estate manager's attention (damaged boundary fencing, litter accumulation, irrigation issues, health and safety hazards).

**Quarterly:** Hedge maintenance to all boundary and internal hedging. The estate has a mix of privet (which requires two to three cuts per year) and established beech hedging (one cut per year in August).

**Spring and Autumn:** Seasonal bedding to the main entrance beds — 40 plants per bed, two beds. Spring planting (May) and autumn planting (October) to maintain colour at the primary visitor access point throughout the year.

**October–March:** Winter gritting to the main access road and car park following a Met Office frost alert protocol. Grit stored on-site in weatherproof storage provided by DCS Garden.

## First Year Delivery

The first year of the contract involved restoring the estate to a maintained standard before regular service intervals could be effectively maintained. The first three visits included remedial work — hedge cutting that had been deferred for 18+ months, and a significant litter accumulation clear.

Once the baseline standard was established, regular service intervals were achieved consistently. No service visits were missed across the growing season.

## Value to the Client

The estate management company reported that tenant feedback on the grounds had shifted from complaints to compliments within the first season. Several tenant lease renewals during the year were preceded by a site walkthrough — the maintained condition of the grounds was cited as a positive factor in at least two cases.

The winter gritting contract was specifically welcomed as addressing a liability gap the client had identified but not previously acted on.
