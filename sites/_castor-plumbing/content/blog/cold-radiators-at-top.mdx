## The Classic Symptom

You've turned the heating on and noticed that one or more radiators are warm — or even hot — at the bottom, but cool or cold at the top. This is a very specific pattern with a very specific cause: trapped air inside the radiator.

Understanding why this happens, and how to fix it, is one of the most useful bits of plumbing knowledge a homeowner can have.

## Why Air Gets Trapped in Radiators

Central heating systems are sealed and pressurised, but small amounts of air enter the system over time from several sources:

- Air dissolved in the water gradually comes out of solution when heated
- Small amounts of air enter when the system is topped up (repressurised via the filling loop)
- Older systems with open-vented tanks can admit air through the feed and expansion tank
- Any repair work that opens the system will introduce air

Air is lighter than water, so it rises to the top of each radiator and becomes trapped there. The hot water circulates in the lower portion of the radiator, which is why the bottom heats up but the top doesn't.

## Confirming the Diagnosis

Before bleeding, confirm air is the cause rather than a seized valve or a circulation problem:

1. **Touch the top corners of the radiator.** Both sides cold at the top, both sides hot at the bottom — classic air trap.
2. **Check the thermostatic valve.** Turn it to a higher setting. If the radiator begins to warm up evenly, the TRV may have been set too low rather than there being an air problem.
3. **Check adjacent radiators.** If multiple radiators have the same pattern, there may be a system-wide air ingress issue rather than individual bleeds needed.

## How to Bleed a Radiator

You'll need a radiator bleed key (available for about £1 from any DIY shop or hardware store) and a cloth.

**Step 1:** Turn the heating off and let the system cool for 30–60 minutes. Bleeding a hot radiator can result in scalding water.

**Step 2:** Locate the bleed valve — it's at the top of the radiator, usually on the side away from the thermostatic valve. It looks like a small square or hexagonal fitting in a circular cap.

**Step 3:** Hold the cloth under the bleed valve. Insert the bleed key and turn it anti-clockwise by about a quarter turn. You'll hear air hissing out.

**Step 4:** Keep the key in the valve and the cloth in place. When the hissing stops and a trickle of water appears, tighten the valve by turning the key clockwise. Don't overtighten — firm but not forced.

**Step 5:** After bleeding all affected radiators, check the boiler pressure gauge. Bleeding releases pressure from the system. If it has dropped below 1 bar, repressurise using the filling loop (refer to your boiler manual).

**Step 6:** Switch the heating back on and check the radiators again.

## When Bleeding Doesn't Fix It

If a radiator is still cold at the top after bleeding, or if it fills with air again quickly, consider:

**Sludge or scale in the system.** Black iron oxide sludge accumulates in ageing systems and can block the upper portion of a radiator. The fix is a power flush — a high-velocity flush of the entire system that removes sludge, scale, and debris.

**A faulty or seized TRV.** A thermostatic radiator valve that is stuck closed will prevent circulation regardless of how much you bleed the radiator.

**Imbalanced system.** In some cases, air accumulates in a particular radiator because the system isn't balanced correctly and that radiator has low flow. A plumber can balance the system by adjusting the lockshield valves.

**Microbial hydrogen generation.** In neglected systems, bacteria in the water can generate hydrogen gas — a different problem from ordinary air, but with the same symptom. A system inhibitor added to the water suppresses this.

## Preventing Air Buildup

- Add a central heating inhibitor (such as Fernox F1 or Sentinel X100) to the system — it reduces corrosion and microbial activity that cause gas generation
- Check system pressure monthly during the heating season
- Have the system serviced annually — a Gas Safe engineer will identify developing issues before they affect performance
