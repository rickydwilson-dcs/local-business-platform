## The Situation

A homeowner in Eastbourne noticed a persistent damp patch appearing under the kitchen sink area, and occasionally felt that the floor was slightly soft when walking near the dishwasher. The boiler pressure required repressurising more often than usual — every few weeks rather than every few months.

Rather than calling a plumber, they bought a dehumidifier and assumed the problem would resolve itself. Six months later, a more obvious water stain appeared on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen, and the floor had begun to creak badly in the affected area.

At that point, they called DCS Plumbing.

## What We Found

On inspection, the source was a slow weep from a compression joint on the cold water supply to the dishwasher. The compression olive had partially rotated over the years, creating a hairline gap. The drip rate was approximately one drip every two seconds — enough to saturate the subfloor structure over months.

The water had tracked under the vinyl floor covering and into the subfloor — a chipboard deck sitting on timber joists. The joists themselves showed early-stage wet rot on the upper surface in the area directly below the leak.

Secondary damage included:
- Saturated chipboard subfloor across approximately 0.8 square metres (chipboard does not dry out — it swells and degrades permanently once wet)
- Early wet rot in two timber joists
- Mould growth on the underside of the floor covering
- Ceiling staining in the room below from water that had tracked through the joists

## What the Repair Involved

**Plumbing repair:** The compression joint was replaced with a push-fit connection — a 20-minute job. The actual plumbing repair was straightforward and inexpensive.

**Remediation required:**
- Removal of the vinyl floor covering
- Removal and replacement of the saturated chipboard subfloor section
- Treatment of the affected timber joists with fungicidal wood hardener and preservative
- Drying of the joist void with a desiccant dehumidifier over two weeks
- Replacement of the ceiling panel below
- Redecoration of the ceiling and wall in the room below

**Estimated total cost of the remediation work (illustrative):** £2,800–3,500 (not including the plumbing repair itself, which was under £150).

## What Early Repair Would Have Cost

If the homeowner had called us when they first noticed the damp patch:
- Leak detection and investigation: £90–150
- Replacement of the compression joint: £80–120
- Possibly no further remediation required if caught within the first few weeks

**Illustrative saving: approximately £2,600–3,200 by acting promptly.**

## The Lessons

**Persistent damp has a source.** Damp doesn't appear in kitchens spontaneously. A patch of damp under a sink, near a dishwasher, or below an upstairs bathroom has a cause. Finding and fixing the cause early is always cheaper than addressing it after damage has accumulated.

**Boiler pressure loss is a symptom.** A system that requires frequent repressurising is losing water somewhere. That water is going somewhere it shouldn't. This symptom alone warranted investigation.

**Chipboard is highly moisture-sensitive.** Many modern homes have chipboard subfloor decking. Unlike solid timber, chipboard swells, delaminates, and loses structural integrity when wet and does not recover when dried. Any sustained leak above chipboard subfloor requires urgent action.

**Dehumidifiers treat the symptom, not the cause.** A dehumidifier can help manage humidity in a wet space but it cannot fix a leak. If the source of moisture is not eliminated, the damage continues.
