## The Problem with Hidden Leaks

A dripping tap is easy to spot and easy to ignore. A hidden water leak is different — it can go undetected for months, quietly saturating floor joists, rotting timbers, promoting mould growth, and running up your water bill. By the time a hidden leak becomes visible, the damage is often substantial.

East Sussex has a mix of older Victorian and Edwardian properties alongside 1930s–1970s housing stock. Many of these homes have original or once-modified pipework that is now ageing. Knowing the warning signs of a hidden leak can save you from an expensive repair bill.

## Warning Sign 1: Unexplained Rise in Water Bills

If your household water usage hasn't changed but your bill has crept up, water is going somewhere. Meter-measured water that doesn't reach a tap or appliance is going through a leak.

**What to check:** Turn off all taps and appliances that use water, then check your water meter. If the dial is still moving, you have a live leak.

## Warning Sign 2: Damp Patches, Staining, or Bubbling Paint

Damp patches on walls or ceilings that appear without an obvious external cause — no rain, no condensation — often indicate water tracking through a structure. Look for:

- Yellow or brown staining on ceilings, especially below bathrooms or kitchens
- Paint or wallpaper that is bubbling, peeling, or lifting from the wall
- Soft or spongy plasterboard when pressed

These signs don't always appear directly above or below the leak — water follows the path of least resistance and can travel significant distances before appearing.

## Warning Sign 3: Musty Smell or Visible Mould

Mould requires sustained moisture. If you're noticing musty smells or visible mould growth in areas that aren't normally damp — inside cupboards, behind furniture, in a corner of a room — look for a moisture source. A slow leak in a concealed pipe is a common cause.

Mould in bathrooms and kitchens is normal and usually surface-level. Mould appearing in a bedroom, hallway, or lounge warrants investigation.

## Warning Sign 4: Warped or Damaged Flooring

Timber floors and laminate are vulnerable to moisture. If floorboards are lifting, warping, or if laminate is swelling and separating at the joints, water is getting in. This is particularly common in properties where water pipes run under suspended timber floors.

Tile adhesive failure — tiles becoming loose or hollow-sounding — can also indicate moisture beneath.

## Warning Sign 5: Foundation or Garden Ground Subsidence

A significant underground leak — from a mains supply pipe running under the property or garden — can wash out soil over time, causing localised ground subsidence or unusually lush, permanently wet patches in the garden even during dry weather.

If a section of your garden stays green and damp through a dry summer, and your water bills are elevated, suspect a supply pipe leak.

## Warning Sign 6: Reduced Water Pressure

A leak in the supply side of your system will reduce water pressure at the tap, because some of the water is escaping before it gets there. If pressure has dropped noticeably and nothing else has changed (neighbours aren't reporting issues, no known works in the street), a pipe leak is worth investigating.

## What to Do If You Suspect a Leak

1. **Check your meter** — confirm water is moving when nothing is running
2. **Isolate areas** — turn off the stopcock to the bathroom, then kitchen, to try to localise where the leak is
3. **Check the boiler pressure gauge** — a system losing pressure between resets often has a leak in the heating circuit
4. **Call a plumber with leak detection equipment** — thermal imaging and acoustic detection can locate leaks without opening walls unnecessarily

Modern leak detection avoids the need to speculatively chase through walls. At DCS Plumbing, we use non-invasive techniques to locate the source before any remediation begins.

## How Long Before a Leak Causes Serious Damage?

It depends on the leak rate and what materials are affected. A slow seep into a timber floor can cause rot within weeks in wet weather. A drip into a cavity wall can run for months before staining appears. If you have any of the above warning signs, get it investigated quickly — the cost of leak detection is always less than the cost of water damage remediation.
