## The Brief

An independent café in Brighton's North Laine had built a loyal following over three years of trading, but their brand identity was entirely self-produced — a font downloaded from a free font site, inconsistent colours applied across signage and print, and no defined visual language.

The owners wanted to invest in a proper brand identity before expanding to a second location. The brief was to produce a brand that felt authentically Brighton — warm, independent, unpretentious — without looking handmade or amateurish.

## Discovery and Strategy

We started with a two-hour strategy session with both owners. The session covered brand values (three words: "welcoming, curious, honest"), target customer (professionals, locals, regulars rather than tourists), and brand references (references they liked and why, references they disliked).

From this session, we identified that the existing instinct — to use warm, earthy colours — was correct, but the execution was too flat and inconsistent. The typeface choice was forgettable. The logo concept (a coffee cup) was generic.

The strategic direction was to lead with the café's name as a wordmark, supported by a custom illustrated icon referencing a local landmark detail that regulars would recognise, set in warm terracotta and deep olive on cream.

## Design Process

**Round 1:** Three wordmark directions — one with a geometric sans-serif, one with a humanist serif, one with a custom lettered feel. Presented without colour to assess the structure.

**Round 2:** Two directions developed with colour and the supporting icon. The humanist serif direction with the illustrated icon was selected unanimously.

**Refinement:** Spacing, weight, and proportion refinements over two rounds of feedback. Final mark approved in week 4.

**Brand guidelines:** A 16-page brand guidelines document defining the logo, its exclusion zone, the colour palette (CMYK, RGB, and hex values), the typography system (two typefaces, scale, usage rules), and application examples.

## Applications

**Shopfront signage:** A heritage-compliant hanging sign in powder-coated steel with the wordmark in painted lettering, and individual acrylic letters mounted to the fascia. Planning consent was obtained through the council's listed building application process (the street is in a conservation area).

**Print:** A5 menu cards, business cards, and A6 loyalty cards all produced at DCS Graphics to the new brand standard.

**Takeaway packaging:** Labels for takeaway cups and bags designed and printed in 1,000-unit runs for cost efficiency.

## Outcome

The café's owners reported that the visual consistency across the shopfront, print, and packaging immediately drew comment from regulars — "it looks like a proper café now" was a phrase they heard repeatedly in the weeks after launch. At the time of writing, they are in the early planning stage for a second location.
