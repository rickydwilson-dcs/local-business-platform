## The Business

A Hailsham-based groundworks and civil engineering contractor had grown from one van to a fleet of six vehicles over five years. The branding had accumulated rather than been designed — each vehicle had been livered at a different time by different providers, resulting in inconsistencies in colour, typography, and layout. The company logo existed in four different variations across the fleet.

With the company winning more commercial contracts where fleet appearance mattered to clients, the owner decided to invest in a consistent, professional fleet identity.

## Discovery and Design Brief

We started with a site visit to see all six vehicles together. The fleet comprised three Ford Transit Custom vans, two larger Sprinters, and one tipper truck — each with different panel configurations and proportions. A livery design that works on a Transit Custom needs to be adapted for a Sprinter and further adapted for a tipper.

The brief was to create a design system — not just a design for one vehicle — that could scale to different formats while remaining instantly recognisable as the same fleet.

We also recommended a logo refresh. The existing mark was structurally fine but inconsistently executed across its various versions. We standardised a single definitive version with a consistent type treatment and colour specification.

## Design

**Phase 1 — Logo standardisation:** A single definitive logo file created in vector format with three lockups (horizontal, stacked, symbol only) and full colour specifications (CMYK, RAL for powder coating, RGB for digital).

**Phase 2 — Fleet design:** The primary livery design was developed for the Transit Custom as the most common vehicle, then adapted for the Sprinter and tipper proportions. The design uses a bold diagonal graphic element that carries across all three vehicle sizes in a different proportion but the same visual language.

**Phase 3 — Approval:** The client approved via a digital mockup before any cutting began.

## Application

Vehicles were processed in groups of two to avoid having more than two vehicles off-road simultaneously — important for a working contractor.

Minor bodywork repairs (a dented panel on one Sprinter, paint scuffs on the tipper) were addressed by the client's bodywork contractor before we received the vehicles.

Cast vinyl with a 5-year rated matte laminate was used throughout. Application time per vehicle ranged from 3 hours (Transit Custom) to 6 hours (tipper).

## Result

All six vehicles completed over four weeks. The fleet design template was supplied to the client for use with future vehicle additions, ensuring any new vehicle can be livered consistently without requiring a new design commission.
