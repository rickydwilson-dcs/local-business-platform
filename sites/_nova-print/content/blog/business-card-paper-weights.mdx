## Why Card Weight Matters

When someone hands you a business card, the first impression is tactile — before they've read the name, they've felt the card. A limp, thin card signals a different kind of business than a solid, premium card. This isn't superficial — it's a communication. The card says something about how the business values its materials and presentation.

Understanding the options — paper weight, coating, and finish — allows you to make an informed choice rather than defaulting to whatever the cheapest option on a website happens to be.

## Paper Weight: What GSM Means

GSM stands for grams per square metre — a measure of paper density. Higher GSM means heavier, stiffer paper. For business cards:

**300gsm** is the standard entry level for digital print business cards. It's adequate but thin — cards at this weight flex noticeably when held and feel lightweight. Suitable for high-volume, short-term use (exhibition cards, event cards), but not ideal for a premium impression.

**350gsm** is the most common weight for quality digital print business cards. It has a confident feel without being excessively heavy. This is a solid choice for most small businesses.

**400gsm** is the premium digital print weight. It has a noticeably firm, solid feel. At this weight, the card feels permanent rather than disposable. This is our standard recommendation for professional services, consultants, and anyone whose card needs to make a strong impression.

**450gsm and above** enters ultra-premium territory — typically achieved by laminating two sheets back-to-back (known as a double-sided laminate or "double-thick" card). Some premium products use a coloured core to create a distinctive edge colour. These are choice items rather than standard working cards.

## Uncoated vs Silk vs Gloss

**Uncoated** is paper with no surface coating. It has a natural, slightly textured feel. It accepts handwriting well (important for notes on the back of a card). It feels natural and unpretentious — associated with craft businesses, creative industries, and anything that wants to feel approachable rather than corporate. The downside is that photographic images print with less punch on uncoated stock.

**Silk** (also called satin) has a smooth, flat surface coating that enhances colour density and sharpness. It's neither the high shine of gloss nor the roughness of uncoated — it sits in between. Silk is the most versatile option: images reproduce well, the surface feels refined, and it can be overprinted with both matte and gloss spot UV. This is the most commonly specified stock for quality business cards.

**Gloss** is a high-shine coated stock. Images and colours appear vivid and saturated. The downside is that gloss shows fingerprints and can feel slightly plastic. For cards with large photographic areas, gloss is appropriate. For text-heavy or minimal designs, silk is usually more refined.

## Lamination: Matte vs Gloss vs Soft-Touch

Lamination is a film applied over the printed card, which affects both the tactile feel and the finish.

**Gloss laminate** intensifies colours and creates a hard, glossy surface. It's durable and slightly moisture-resistant.

**Matte laminate** creates a flat, non-reflective surface. It's very popular for professional and premium applications — matte laminates tend to feel expensive and are associated with luxury brands. Matte laminated cards fingerprint more readily than gloss.

**Soft-touch (velvet) laminate** is the most luxurious finish available. It has a suede-like surface that feels tactile and premium in a way that both gloss and standard matte do not. It's increasingly common among premium businesses. Soft-touch laminate is more expensive than standard lamination but produces a distinct, memorable impression.

## Spot UV

Spot UV is a localised gloss coating applied to specific design elements — a logo, a border, a texture pattern — on top of a matte laminated surface. The contrast between the matte base and the high-gloss spot UV creates a striking visual and tactile effect.

Spot UV works best with simple, bold design elements. Fine detail (small text, hairlines) doesn't reproduce the spot UV effect cleanly. If you're considering spot UV, discuss the design implications before finalising artwork.

## Making the Right Choice

A simple decision framework:

- **Professional services (solicitors, accountants, financial advisers):** 400gsm silk or matte laminate, or soft-touch laminate
- **Creative industries:** 350gsm uncoated (natural feel), or 400gsm soft-touch
- **Trades and local services:** 350gsm silk — professional without unnecessary premium cost
- **Retail and hospitality:** 350gsm gloss or silk with spot UV if budget allows
- **Exhibition / events / high volume:** 300–350gsm silk — functional, economical
