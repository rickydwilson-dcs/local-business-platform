## The Bulk Print Trap

Print pricing follows a simple curve: unit cost falls as quantity increases. A run of 1,000 flyers might cost 8p per unit. A run of 5,000 costs 3p per unit. The apparent logic is to always print more.

The problem is that flyers have a lifespan. A seasonal offer, a current phone number, a price — any of these can change. Print that was produced in bulk and becomes incorrect is waste. Bulk print sitting in a box for two years before it's used hasn't saved you money if the content was out of date six months after printing.

The right quantity isn't always the largest quantity. It's the quantity that matches your actual distribution need within a sensible time horizon.

## Understanding Print Pricing Curves

Print costs have two components: **setup cost** (the cost of getting the press ready, preparing plates or RIP processing, handling) and **variable cost** (the per-unit cost of paper and ink).

For a simple digital print job, setup cost might be £15–30. Variable cost might be 2–5p per unit. At low quantities, the setup cost dominates — a run of 100 units at £30 setup + £2 variable cost = £32 total = 32p/unit. At 1,000 units: £30 + £20 = £50 = 5p/unit. At 5,000 units: £30 + £100 = £130 = 2.6p/unit.

The steepest savings come in the jump from very small quantities to moderate quantities. The jump from 1,000 to 5,000 units saves considerably less per unit than the jump from 100 to 1,000.

## The Time Horizon Calculation

A practical approach is to calculate your time horizon first:

**How long before this content will need updating?**
- A flyer with a specific seasonal offer: 3–6 months
- A flyer with your general services and a stable phone number: 2–3 years
- A business card: depends on how often your details change, but typically 1–3 years

**How many will you distribute in that time?**
Estimate your monthly distribution rate and multiply by the time horizon. If you drop 200 flyers a month and your content is good for 12 months, you need approximately 2,400 flyers. Print 3,000 for a comfortable buffer without excess.

Avoid printing 10,000 "because the unit price is so much cheaper" unless you have a plan to distribute 10,000 within the content's useful lifespan.

## When to Use On-Demand / Short-Run Print

**The content changes frequently:** Menu inserts with seasonal dishes, offer leaflets for specific promotions, event listings, price lists in markets where prices move.

**The product is personalised:** Any content with a specific name, event date, or custom detail needs on-demand or variable-data printing.

**You're testing a new approach:** Before committing to 5,000 flyers with a new message or design, test with 250–500. If they work, print more.

**You can't store large quantities:** Print storage requires dry, clean, accessible space. Not all businesses have it. On-demand print avoids the storage problem entirely.

**You're a new business:** Until you know your distribution channels and rates, short-run print avoids overcommitting to content that may need to change as the business evolves.

## When Bulk Print Makes Sense

**Stable content and high volume:** Business cards for established businesses with stable contact details. Letterhead stationery for an established address. Branded envelopes and compliment slips with no time-sensitive content.

**Trade and exhibition print:** Quantities of a consistent capability document or product brochure distributed over 12–18 months at trade events.

**Items with long shelf life:** Branded merchandise, bags, and presentation materials can be produced in bulk runs without the same content-obsolescence risk as marketing collateral.

## The True Cost of Waste

If you print 5,000 flyers and discard 2,000 because the content became outdated, the effective unit cost of the 3,000 used is the full cost of 5,000 — significantly higher than if you'd printed 3,000 in the first place.

Wasted print also has environmental cost. Producing and landfilling print with no purpose is a predictable outcome of over-ordering. Thoughtful quantity planning reduces this.
