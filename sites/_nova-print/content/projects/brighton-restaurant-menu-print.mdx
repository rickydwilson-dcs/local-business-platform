## The Project

A new independent restaurant in Brighton's Kemptown area was preparing for a September opening and needed a complete print package — from menus to marketing materials — coordinated as a single production run to maintain colour consistency.

The restaurant's graphic designer supplied print-ready artwork files for each item. DCS Print's role was production and project management, ensuring all items were produced to schedule and within the colour parameters the designer had specified.

## The Print Package

**Menus:** The primary challenge. Dinner menus were specified as A4 landscape, 400gsm silk with soft-touch matte laminate — a premium specification that would look and feel appropriate for the restaurant's price point. Lunch menus were A3 folded to A4, 170gsm silk. Both were produced on the same press session to ensure colour matching.

**Table tent cards:** 100mm × 148mm folded tent cards on 350gsm silk for daily specials boards. Unlaminated to allow writing with a dry-erase marker on the back panel. These required a different stock treatment from the main menus, which is why they were quoted and priced separately.

**Promotional flyers:** 500 A5 single-sided flyers for distribution in the surrounding residential streets and adjacent businesses. 130gsm silk, full-colour. The designer supplied the artwork incorporating opening date, location map, and social media handles.

**Business cards:** 400 business cards in two name versions (head chef and general manager). 400gsm soft-touch matte with spot UV on the logo element. The spot UV treatment was the one element that required 48-hour additional production time due to the specialist process.

**Wine stickers:** 200 self-adhesive labels for wine bottles (150mm × 50mm) with the restaurant's name and a bar code placeholder. Printed on white vinyl with gloss laminate for moisture resistance — essential for a product that sits in an ice bucket.

## Schedule Management

The content change issue (kitchen finalising the menu twice during the design process) was managed by agreeing a final artwork approval deadline with the client: no further content changes after the confirmed deadline, regardless of circumstances. This allowed production to proceed on schedule.

All items were delivered to the restaurant seven days before the opening date — two days ahead of the stated requirement.

## Result

The restaurant opened successfully. The print quality and colour consistency across all items was confirmed by the designer as matching the screen proofs within acceptable tolerance. The soft-touch menu was specifically mentioned by early reviewers as part of the overall dining experience.
