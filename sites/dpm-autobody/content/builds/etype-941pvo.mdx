## The record | A car we did not build alone

This one is not DPM's restoration. It belongs to Mark Antwis, who did the mechanical rebuild and
the metalwork himself and sent us the bodyshell for finishing and paint — the way a restorer
sometimes hands one part of a job to the people they trust most with it.

He wrote the story himself for the E-Type Owners Club Magazine — nine pages, April 2026, headlined
"A Promise Kept". He came to us because of a different car first — his own Aston Martin V8, which
we painted the year before.

- **Marque:** Jaguar
- **Model:** E-type Series 1, 3.8 Fixed Head Coupé
- **Built:** 1962 — the 787th FHC made
- **Colour:** Opalescent Silver Blue
- **Registration:** 941 PVO
- **Owner & restorer:** Mark Antwis
- **DPM's part:** Panel gapping, levelling, flatting and paint
- **Published in:** E-Type Owners Club Magazine, April 2026 (9 pages)

## Provenance | A forty-seven-year promise

Brian Bray bought 941 PVO in 1978 for £410, with his nine-year-old son Matthew, as its third
owner. It was already sixteen years old and needed a full restoration. The engine was rebuilt, the
body stripped and sand-blasted — and then, as families and decades do, the project stalled. It sat
dismantled in boxes for the best part of forty years.

By 2017 Brian had retired to a farm in Leicestershire and Matthew was forty-eight. Reluctantly,
the family decided to sell, and the car went under the hammer at H&H Auctions, Duxford, on 29
March 2017. Mark Antwis was the fourth bidder and the winner.

He already had a Ferrari 308 and a self-restored Aston Martin V8 in the workshop; the E-type
completed what he calls his "dreamed-of stable of classics". His method, in his own words:
*"conserving and restoring rather than replacing"*.

## The build | He trusted us with the Aston. Then the E-type.

Mark did the mechanicals and the metalwork himself — four hundred and twenty hours on the
metalwork alone, by his own account. The shell and panels came to us for what came next.

Primer, then Opalescent Silver Blue.

- **450 hours** — Panel work through primer, colour, five coats of lacquer and machine polish —
  the magazine's figure for the whole job, over three months.

## The trust

> "I had interviewed many body shops for the painting of my concours-winning Aston Martin. The
> only person I would trust with the E-type was the man who had laid down that paint with such
> astonishingly beautiful results."

The E-type job did not come from a stranger finding us. It came from a repeat customer — his first
car with us was the Aston Martin V8 that won at Sandringham.

## The other car | The Aston Martin that came first

Before the E-type, Mark restored a 1974 Aston Martin V8 himself, nut-and-bolt, from a bare shell —
and sent it to David for the same thing: paint.
