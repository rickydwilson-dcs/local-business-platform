When this Eastbourne-based trade business came to us, they had a fleet of six vans that had been signed over the years in an ad hoc way — different fonts, mismatched colours, some with outdated contact details. It gave the impression of a company that had grown without ever quite catching up with its own branding.

The brief was clear: consistent livery across all six vehicles, with a clean, professional look that would work on both the smaller vans in the fleet and the larger panel vans. And they needed it done fast — a client-facing event was coming up within the fortnight, and they wanted every vehicle on site looking the part.

## The Design Process

We started by understanding what the business needed the livery to communicate: trade credentials, contact details, and a sense of reliability. The client had a logo but no established vehicle livery, so our in-house design team built a layout from scratch, working within their brand colours and producing scaled mockups on each vehicle type before anything was committed to production.

The approach we settled on used cut vinyl for the lettering and solid colour elements — giving crisp, durable results — with a digitally printed panel for the logo where it appeared at larger sizes. This combination keeps costs sensible while delivering a professional finish.

After two rounds of revisions on the initial mockups, the client signed off the artwork, and production began the same week.

## Production and Application

With six vehicles of varying sizes, consistency is the real challenge on a job like this. A layout that looks perfectly proportioned on a short-wheelbase Transit can look cramped on a long-wheelbase Sprinter if you simply scale it up without adjusting the spacing and positioning. Our team adapted the layout for each vehicle individually, keeping the visual impression consistent even where the physical dimensions differed.

All preparation, production, and application was carried out in-house at our Polegate workshop. Vehicles were cleaned down and inspected before any material went on — this step matters because vinyl applied to a poorly prepared surface will lift prematurely, especially on vehicles used for outdoor work.

The fleet was turned around in four and a half working days — ahead of the client's deadline.

## The Outcome

The client collected the vehicles and had all six on site at their event. The feedback was immediate: their customers noticed the change, and the consistent presentation reinforced the professional image the business had been working to establish. Within a month, they were back in touch to add two recently acquired vehicles to the fleet.

It's a project that illustrates what fleet graphics can do when approached properly — not just sticking names on vans, but building a consistent visual identity that travels with the business every day.
