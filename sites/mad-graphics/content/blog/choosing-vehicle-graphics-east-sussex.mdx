Your van or car is one of the most visible things your business owns. Every mile it covers is a potential impression, and every job site it parks outside is a chance to be noticed. Vehicle graphics done well are some of the most cost-effective advertising available to a local business. Done badly, they reflect poorly on your brand and can be expensive to correct.

This guide walks through the key decisions so you know what to ask for — and what to watch out for.

## Cut Vinyl vs. Printed Graphics: What's the Difference?

The two main types of vehicle graphics are **cut vinyl** and **printed graphics** (also called digitally printed vinyl), and they serve different purposes.

**Cut vinyl** is exactly what it sounds like: coloured vinyl sheeting cut into letters, logos, and shapes with a computer-controlled plotter. There's no printed layer — the colour comes from the vinyl itself, which is available in a wide range of stock colours. Cut vinyl is extremely durable, typically lasting 5–7 years outdoors, and is ideal for clear, bold lettering and simple logos. It's also usually the most cost-effective option for straightforward van signwriting.

**Printed graphics** allow for photographic imagery, gradients, complex multi-colour logos, and detailed artwork. The design is printed onto a clear or white vinyl film which is then laminated for protection. Printed graphics offer more visual flexibility, but the quality of the print, ink, and laminate matters significantly — cheap unlaminated print will fade and peel within a year or two.

For most trade vehicles — plumbers, electricians, builders, landscapers — a combination of cut vinyl lettering with a printed panel for the logo is a practical and cost-effective choice. For businesses where visual impact is critical, such as promotional vehicles or retail delivery vans, full printed graphics make more sense.

## Design Considerations

Good vehicle graphics start with good design, and good design for a vehicle is different from good design for a website or a leaflet. A few principles worth keeping in mind:

**Legibility at speed.** Most people will see your van moving or from a distance. A phone number in a clean, bold typeface at a readable size will get more calls than an elaborate design that looks great in a mockup but can't be read at 30mph.

**Hierarchy.** What do you most want people to remember? Usually it's your trade, your phone number, and your name — roughly in that order. The design should make these the dominant elements.

**Contrast.** Your graphics need to work against your vehicle's base colour. White vans give the most flexibility, but darker base colours can look very striking with the right combination of colours and materials.

**Don't overcrowd it.** More information is not always better. A van covered edge-to-edge with text and images can actually be harder to read than a well-considered, spacious layout.

A reputable graphics company will ask about your existing branding, discuss what you want people to do (call you, visit your website, remember your name), and produce a proper scaled mockup before anything is cut or printed. If a supplier skips the mockup stage, that's a warning sign.

## Material Quality: Why It Matters

Not all vinyl is created equal. The industry standard for professional vehicle graphics is cast vinyl from manufacturers such as Avery Dennison or 3M. Cast vinyl is made through a process that gives it flexibility and dimensional stability — it conforms to compound curves and panel edges without lifting, and it resists UV degradation for years.

Cheaper calendered vinyl is thicker and less flexible. It works fine for flat, indoor applications, but on a vehicle it can lift at edges, crack in cold weather, and fade within a couple of years. If you're paying for van signwriting that you expect to last the lifetime of the vehicle, ask what material is being used.

For printed graphics, lamination is essential. An unlaminated print has no surface protection — it scratches and fades quickly. A good gloss or matte laminate adds UV resistance and mechanical protection, and extends the life of the graphics significantly.

## What to Look for in a Supplier

When you're evaluating vehicle graphics companies in East Sussex, a few things are worth checking:

**Portfolio.** Can they show you completed work on vehicles similar to yours? Photos of finished installations — not just design mockups — tell you a lot about application quality.

**In-house production vs. outsourced.** Companies that design, print, and apply in-house have tighter quality control and can fix problems faster. If a company outsources the print and gets someone else to apply, there are more variables that can go wrong.

**Preparation process.** Proper application requires a clean, properly prepared surface. A professional installer will clean the vehicle thoroughly before application. Rushing this step is a common cause of premature lifting.

**Aftercare advice.** Good suppliers will tell you how to look after your graphics — what cleaning products to avoid, how long to wait before washing the vehicle, and what to do if you notice a lifting edge.

## Local Considerations for East Sussex Businesses

East Sussex has its own mix of conditions worth factoring in. The coastal areas see salt air and spray, which can accelerate the deterioration of graphics applied to a vehicle with any existing rust or paint damage. It's worth addressing any bodywork issues before applying graphics — vinyl applied over damaged paint won't adhere well and will lift prematurely.

The county also has a high proportion of rural and semi-rural routes, where vehicles encounter dust, mud, and road debris more than they would in a city. This puts more wear on graphics, particularly on lower panels. Some customers choose to leave the lower 20cm of a van ungraphed for exactly this reason.
