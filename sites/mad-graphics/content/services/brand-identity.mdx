## Brand Identity & Guidelines

Brand consistency is not just about aesthetics. It is about how confidently and professionally your business presents itself across every touchpoint — the van, the website, the sign above the door, the business card, the workwear, the social profile. When each of these uses the same logo, the same colours, and the same typographic style, the cumulative effect is a business that looks established, trustworthy, and serious.

Mad Graphics has developed brand identities for East Sussex businesses from our Polegate studio since 2004. Our background in print and signage means we design brand systems that work in the real world — on vehicles, on signs, on fabric, and on screen — not just on a presentation slide.

A brand identity project starts with discovery: understanding your business, your customers, your competitors, and what you want your brand to communicate. From that foundation, we develop a visual identity system. This begins with the logo — the primary mark and any required variants (horizontal, stacked, icon-only). It then extends to a colour palette defined with precise references for print (CMYK, Pantone) and screen (RGB, HEX). Typography is specified with primary and secondary typefaces and defined usage rules.

The output is a brand guidelines document: a reference that records exactly how each element of the identity should be used — spacing, minimum sizes, colour combinations, typographic hierarchy, and don't-do examples. This document serves you when briefing any supplier in the future, ensuring your brand is reproduced consistently whether the job is a van, a banner, a website, or a business card.

We serve brand identity clients across Eastbourne, Hastings, Lewes, Bexhill-on-Sea, Seaford, and the wider East Sussex area.

## How It Works

### 1. Discovery & Brief
We discuss your business, audience, and visual direction. We review competitor brands and agree a written brief before any design work begins.

### 2. Identity Development
We develop the logo and brand system — presenting concepts, refining the chosen direction, and building the complete visual toolkit through structured review rounds.

### 3. Guidelines Document
Approved brand elements are compiled into a brand guidelines document with clear usage rules, colour references, and typographic specifications.

### 4. File Delivery & Rollout
You receive a complete file set for all brand elements and the guidelines document. We can then apply the new brand to your vehicles, signage, print, and workwear.
