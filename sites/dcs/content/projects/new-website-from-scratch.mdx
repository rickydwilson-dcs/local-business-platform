# New Website — Painter & Decorator in Brighton

Mark has been decorating professionally in Brighton for seven years. In that time he's built a loyal base of repeat customers and gets a steady flow of referrals from them — enough to stay busy, but not enough to grow. When he came to DCS, he was turning down larger interior projects because he didn't look established enough to win them. His entire online presence was a personal Gmail address.

## The Challenge

The work Mark wanted to take on — interior design consultations, full room makeovers, period property restoration — tends to come from clients who research carefully before getting in touch. They look at photos, read about the decorator's approach, and check that the business looks credible before committing to an initial conversation. Without a website, Mark was invisible to this market entirely.

There was also the practical problem of his contact details. Sending a quote from a personal Gmail account doesn't inspire confidence in a client who's about to spend several thousand pounds on their home. A business email address is a small detail, but it signals professionalism in a way that matters.

The referral network was a ceiling, not a strategy. Mark wanted to expand his service area beyond his immediate network, take on the higher-value interior work he was capable of, and stop relying on word of mouth for every new customer.

## The Solution

We built Mark a 20-page website on the DCS platform, designed to represent the quality of his work and convert visitors into enquiries. The design was clean and image-led, with space to showcase room transformations as his portfolio grows.

The service architecture covered every offering clearly: interior painting, exterior painting and weatherproofing, wallpaper hanging, feature walls, and decorator consultation for clients who want help choosing colours and finishes before committing to a full project. That last service page was important — it speaks directly to the interior design market Mark wanted to reach, and gives him a lower-barrier way for those clients to get started.

We built out location pages for Brighton & Hove, Hove, Portslade, Shoreham-by-Sea, Worthing, Burgess Hill, and Haywards Heath — covering the realistic service radius for a sole trader with a van. Each page targets the local search terms for that area and links to the relevant services.

On the practical side, we set up a Google Workspace account with a professional business email address (mark@[business].co.uk) and migrated his existing contacts across. The site was submitted to Google Search Console and Google Business Profile was created and verified at launch.

## The Results

Three weeks after launch, Mark received his first enquiry through the website contact form — a homeowner in Hove who had found him by searching for a decorator on Google. Not a referral. A stranger who found him, looked at the site, decided he looked like the right person, and got in touch.

That kind of cold enquiry wasn't possible before the site existed. Now it's happening consistently: Mark is getting two to three organic leads per month from the website, giving him a reliable source of new work that doesn't depend on who his existing customers happen to know.

The most significant result came a few months after launch. A client in Brighton found the decorator consultation service page, booked an initial consultation, and ended up commissioning Mark for a full interior refurbishment of a Victorian terraced house — the type of project he'd wanted to take on but couldn't reach without looking established. That single booking covered the cost of the website.

The professional email address is a small change that's had a noticeable effect on how Mark presents himself when quoting for work. Clients respond differently to a business email than a personal one — it's a signal that carries weight before they've even seen the quote.

For a sole trader, a well-built website isn't a luxury. It's the thing that makes the next stage of growth possible.
