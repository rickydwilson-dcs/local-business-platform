Getting traffic to your website is only half the job. The other half is making sure that when someone lands on your site, they actually get in touch. A lot of tradesperson websites are losing potential customers at this stage — not because the person wasn't interested, but because something about the site made it hard or uninviting to make contact.

Here are seven specific things that make a real difference.

## 1. Put Your Phone Number in the Header and Footer

This sounds obvious, but a surprising number of websites bury the phone number on the Contact page. If someone has to search for your number, most of them won't bother — they'll go back to Google and click the next result.

Your phone number should be in the header of every page, large enough to read easily, and on mobile it should be a clickable link (a `tel:` link) so people can dial directly by tapping it. Repeat it in the footer too. The goal is that wherever someone is on your site, they can see how to call you without having to navigate anywhere.

This one change alone can meaningfully increase the number of calls you receive from website visitors.

## 2. Make Sure Your Site Loads Quickly

If your website takes more than three seconds to load on a mobile connection, a significant proportion of your visitors will leave before they see anything. This isn't an exaggeration — the data on this is consistent. Slow websites lose people.

The most common causes of slow sites are uncompressed images, cheap shared hosting, and bloated page builders loaded with unused features. A well-built site on decent hosting typically loads in under two seconds.

You can test your current site speed for free at PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev). If your mobile score is below 70, it's worth addressing.

## 3. Make Your Contact Form Easy to Find and Simple to Fill In

A contact form is essential for capturing enquiries outside of business hours. But it needs to be prominent and it needs to be short.

If the only contact form is at the bottom of a long Contact page that's two clicks away, people won't find it. Put a simple form — name, phone number, what they need, and a submit button — on your homepage, and link to a full contact page from every service page.

Keep the fields to a minimum. Every extra field you add reduces completions. Name, phone, and a brief message is enough. You'll get the rest of the information when you call them back.

## 4. Include Location Names in Your Service Pages

This does two jobs at once. It helps Google understand where you work, which improves your local search rankings. And it reassures potential customers immediately that you cover their area.

Instead of a page titled "Boiler Installation," a page titled "Boiler Installation in Derby and South Derbyshire" is more useful to everyone. It tells Google what to rank you for, and it tells the reader that they're in the right place.

If you cover several towns, either mention them all on your main service pages or — better — create individual pages for the biggest areas. "Emergency Plumber in Tamworth," "Emergency Plumber in Lichfield," and so on. Each page is an extra opportunity to appear in local searches.

## 5. Show Real Evidence of Your Work

The biggest question any new customer has before contacting a tradesperson is: "Can I trust this person?" Abstract claims like "quality workmanship" and "professional service" don't answer that question. Evidence does.

What works: photos of actual completed jobs (before and after where relevant), genuine customer testimonials with the person's name and ideally the town they're in, and visible indicators like how long you've been established or any professional accreditations you hold.

If you have photos on your phone from recent jobs, those can go on your website right now. They don't need to be professionally shot. Real photos of real work are more persuasive than stock imagery.

## 6. Make Sure the Site Works Well on Mobile

More than 70% of local searches happen on phones. If your website is hard to navigate on a small screen — small text, buttons too close together, content that doesn't reflow properly — you're losing the majority of your potential customers before they can contact you.

Test your own site on your phone right now. Can you read it easily? Can you tap the phone number to call? Is the contact form usable without zooming in? If not, this needs fixing.

A well-built modern website should be mobile-first — designed for phones and then adapted for larger screens, not the other way around.

## 7. Have One Clear Call-to-Action on Every Page

Every page on your website should have a clear, obvious next step for the visitor. Usually that's "Call us" or "Get a free quote." One call-to-action per page, not four competing ones.

When there are too many options, people don't take any of them. A single prominent button or panel — with your phone number, or a link to the contact form — at the top and bottom of every page removes any friction. The visitor knows exactly what to do next.

Keep the wording direct: "Call us now," "Request a free quote," "Get in touch today." Nothing clever, nothing vague. Just a clear instruction and the means to follow it.

## Putting It Together

None of these seven things is technically complicated or expensive. Most of them are about clarity: making it easy for people to contact you, reassuring them that you're the right choice, and removing any friction between landing on your site and getting in touch.

If your website currently gets visitors but not many enquiries, work through this list and fix each one. The impact of all seven together is significant — but even fixing the first two or three will move the needle.
