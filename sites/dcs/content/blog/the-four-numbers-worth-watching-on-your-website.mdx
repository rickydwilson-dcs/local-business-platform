# The Four Numbers Worth Watching on Your Website

Most small business owners handle website analytics one of two ways. They ignore it completely, because nobody ever showed them why it matters. Or they log into Google Analytics once, see a dashboard full of graphs and bounce rates and engagement scores, and log straight back out again none the wiser.

Both reactions make sense. Analytics tools are built for marketers, not tradespeople, and most of what's on screen doesn't tell you anything useful about your business. A handful of numbers in there do matter, though, and once you know which ones to look at, the rest of the dashboard can stay closed.

Here are the four worth watching, and the one question they're all answering.

## The question underneath all of it

Before the four numbers, one question. It's the one I'd ask if I only had time to ask one thing about your website: how many enquiries did it generate this month?

Enquiries means people who picked up the phone or filled in your contact form because of something they saw on your site. That's the number that connects your website to your business, and the other three exist to help you improve it.

## 1. How many people are visiting, and where they're coming from

This is the number everyone checks first, and on its own it doesn't tell you much. A busy month of traffic from the wrong people is worth less than a quiet month of traffic from people ready to book a job.

What matters more is the breakdown: how much of that traffic came from a Google search, how much came from people typing your web address in directly, and how much came from social media or another site linking to you. If nearly all your traffic is direct, your search visibility might be weaker than you think. If it's almost all search, that tells you which channel is doing the work for your business, useful to know before you spend money trying to grow a different one.

## 2. Which pages people actually look at

Total visits tell you how many people showed up. Top pages tell you what they did once they got there.

If your services page is getting plenty of visits but your contact page barely gets any, that's worth knowing. It might mean visitors aren't finding a clear reason to get in touch, or the path from service to contact isn't obvious. If one particular service page consistently outperforms the others, that's a signal about what people are searching for, and it can shape what you write about next.

## 3. Whether you're moving up for the searches that matter

This one comes from Google Search Console rather than Analytics, and it's the number that tells you whether your SEO work is going anywhere.

Search Console shows which search terms your pages appear for, how often they get clicked, and roughly where you rank. If you've had new pages written or a location added, this is where you'd see whether it's making a difference. A page for "boiler repairs" climbing slowly from page three to page one of Google is a page that's about to start sending you real enquiries, even before it does.

## 4. Enquiries, the number that matters most

This is the one that answers the underlying question, and it's also the one most websites get wrong, because it needs setting up properly before it works at all.

By default, Analytics can tell you how many people visited your site. It has no way of knowing how many of them filled in your contact form or tapped your phone number unless someone has specifically configured it to track those actions as conversions. Without that setup, a business owner can be sitting on a website that's quietly generating plenty of enquiries a month, or one generating none at all, and the raw traffic numbers alone won't tell them which. Setting up this conversion tracking, as I've written about on the [Analytics & Reporting](/services/analytics) page, is the piece that turns a pile of numbers into a real answer.

Once it's in place, the other three numbers fall into perspective. Traffic and top pages tell you who's arriving and what they're looking at. Search Console tells you whether you're becoming easier to find. Conversions tell you whether any of that is turning into work.

## Reading these together

None of the four numbers is much use on its own. Traffic without conversions just tells you people are looking. Rankings without traffic tell you Google knows about you but nobody's clicking through yet. The value comes from reading them as a chain: are people finding the site, are they landing on the right pages, are the rankings moving in the right direction, and is any of that turning into an enquiry.

That's the whole point of setting analytics up properly in the first place: being able to answer one plain question every month, is the website doing its job?
