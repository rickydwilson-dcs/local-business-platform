SEO gets sold hard to small businesses. You'll have received the cold emails: "We can get you to page one of Google, guaranteed." It's one of the most oversold services in digital marketing, and tradespeople in particular tend to be targeted because they're often not sure what to make of the claims.

So let's be straight about what SEO actually is, what it can realistically do for you, and when it's worth paying for.

## What SEO Is (and Isn't)

SEO — search engine optimisation — is the work of making your website show up when people search for relevant terms on Google. For a tradesperson, that means things like "emergency plumber in Derby" or "electrician near Nottingham."

It is not magic, it is not instant, and it cannot be guaranteed. Anyone who tells you they can guarantee a specific Google ranking is either lying or doesn't understand how search engines work — probably both.

What SEO can do, done properly over a sustained period, is move your site up in the rankings for searches that are relevant to your work. That increased visibility leads to more people clicking through to your site, and if your site is well built, more of those visitors become enquiries.

## Local SEO vs National SEO

This distinction matters enormously for tradespeople, and it's one that a lot of SEO sellers gloss over.

**National SEO** means competing for terms like "best electricians in the UK" or "top-rated plumbers." This is a crowded, competitive space dominated by large comparison sites, directories, and major chains. For a sole trader or small local business, it's not worth chasing — you'll spend a fortune and likely never meaningfully rank.

**Local SEO** is about ranking in your specific geographic area. "Plumber in Wolverhampton." "Emergency electrician Stafford." "Boiler installation Derby." This is entirely achievable for a well-built website. Google actively tries to surface local businesses for local searches — that's the behaviour it's designed to encourage.

So when you're evaluating whether to pay for SEO, the question isn't "can I rank on Google?" It's "can I rank for [my trade] in [my town or area]?" That's a very different and much more achievable goal.

## What Actually Works

The foundations of local SEO that actually move the needle are relatively straightforward.

**Individual service and location pages.** A page titled "Boiler Installation in Derby" with genuine content about that service in that area performs better than a single generic "Services" page. More pages = more opportunities to appear in relevant searches.

**Google Business Profile.** This is the listing that shows up in Google Maps and the local pack (the three businesses that appear with the map at the top of search results). Keeping this accurate and collecting genuine reviews on it has a direct impact on local visibility.

**Genuine content.** Pages that actually describe what you do, where you work, and why customers choose you perform better over time than thin, templated content. There are no shortcuts here.

**Technical basics.** Fast load times, mobile-friendly design, correct page titles and meta descriptions, structured data. None of this is exotic — it's table stakes for a properly built website.

## What's Snake Oil

A few things to be skeptical of.

**Ranking guarantees.** No one controls Google's algorithm. Any guarantee of specific rankings is hollow — and often the tactics used to chase short-term results can harm your site's long-term performance.

**Monthly retainers with no transparency.** If an SEO company can't tell you specifically what they're doing each month and show you measurable progress, you're paying for nothing. Ask for plain English reporting: what they did, what moved, what's next.

**Massive link-building packages.** Getting listed in hundreds of low-quality directories used to work a decade ago. It doesn't now, and it can actively hurt you.

**Overnight results.** SEO takes time. Legitimate work typically starts showing results in three to six months. If someone's promising you page one in a week, walk away.

## Is It Worth Paying For?

For most tradespeople, the most cost-effective approach is to get a properly built website where local SEO is baked in from the start — not charged as an expensive monthly extra.

DCS builds local SEO into every site as standard. The service pages, the location targeting, the Google Business Profile setup guidance, the structured data — that's part of what the site is, not an upsell. You don't need a £500/month retainer to have a website that's configured to rank locally.

Where ongoing paid SEO does make sense is if you're in a highly competitive local market, you're actively trying to expand into multiple towns or areas, or you want to produce regular content that builds your authority over time. In those cases, a good SEO consultant — one who shows you exactly what they're doing and why — is a worthwhile investment.

But if someone approaches you cold, promises rankings they can't guarantee, and wants a large monthly retainer with vague deliverables, the answer is no.

A well-built site doing the basics well is more valuable than an average site propped up by expensive ongoing work. Get the foundations right first.
