# Service Pages vs Location Pages: What's the Difference?

When local tradespeople ask why their website isn't showing up on Google, the answer usually comes down to one of two things: they have no service pages, no location pages, or they've tried to combine both into one cluttered page that does neither job properly.

Understanding the difference between these two page types — and why you need both — is probably the single most useful thing you can learn about local SEO. So here it is, in plain English.

## What a Service Page Is

A service page is a dedicated page for one specific thing you do.

If you're a plumber, that might be: boiler installation, boiler servicing, power flushing, unblocking drains, bathroom fitting, emergency call-out. Each of those is a service. Each of those should be its own page.

The purpose of a service page is to rank when someone searches for that specific service — without a location qualifier. Someone searching "how much does a power flush cost" or "what is a power flush" should land on your power flush service page. Someone in your area searching "power flush plumber" is also a candidate.

A good service page explains what the service is, what's involved, how long it takes, what it costs (or a ballpark), and why a customer should choose you. It should answer the questions a prospective customer is actually asking. The more thoroughly it does that, the better it ranks — and the more it converts.

## What a Location Page Is

A location page is a dedicated page targeting a specific geographic area.

If you work across Brighton, Hove, Worthing, and Eastbourne, you should have a page for each of those. Not a single "Areas We Cover" page with a list of towns. Individual pages, each one focused on one area.

The purpose of a location page is to rank when someone includes a place name in their search. "Plumber in Hove", "emergency plumber Worthing", "boiler installation Eastbourne" — these searches need location-specific pages to compete for.

Google's local search algorithm pays a lot of attention to relevance. A page that's explicitly about your service in a specific town is far more likely to rank for searches in that town than a generic homepage that happens to mention the town in a footer somewhere.

## Why You Need Both

Here's where people go wrong. A common approach is to have service pages that never mention specific towns, and a single "Service Areas" page that lists everywhere you cover. That structure leaves a huge gap.

The searches you're missing are the high-intent ones — the person searching "boiler installation Brighton" or "power flush plumber Hove". Those searches have both a service and a location in them. A pure service page doesn't target the location. A pure location page doesn't target the specific service. You need pages that do both.

The way to handle this properly is to have a matrix of pages. Each of your key services, combined with each of your key locations. "Boiler installation in Brighton." "Boiler installation in Hove." "Power flush plumber Brighton." These are separate pages, each targeting a specific combination that real customers search for.

## What Goes on Each Type

**Service pages should include:**
- A clear explanation of the service
- What's involved in the job
- Typical timescales and pricing (or a range)
- Why customers should choose you for this service
- Testimonials from customers who had this specific work done
- A clear call to action (call, contact form, or quote request)

**Location pages should include:**
- Confirmation that you cover that area and surrounding postcodes
- Services you offer there
- Local references that make the page feel genuinely relevant to that location
- Any specific experience with local housing types, infrastructure, or issues
- Testimonials from customers in that area if you have them
- A clear call to action

The location page doesn't need to be long, but it does need to be specific. A page that just swaps one town name for another isn't good enough — Google can tell, and so can visitors. Include a sentence or two about the area, the types of properties you work in there, or anything that makes it feel like a page written by someone who actually works in that place.

## Why Combining Them Doesn't Work as Well

Some sites try to solve this with a single page per location that lists all services. "Plumber in Brighton — boiler installation, power flushing, emergency call-out." That's better than nothing, but it's not as effective as separate pages.

A single page targeting multiple services and a single location can only rank for a limited number of things. It competes with itself for different search terms. A dedicated page for "boiler installation in Brighton" will nearly always outrank a page trying to cover all plumbing services in Brighton, because it's more specific and more relevant to that exact search.

The maths on this is straightforward. If you offer five services and cover six locations, you have thirty potential pages. Each one targets a specific, real search that people are making. Each one can rank independently. That's thirty opportunities to appear in search results, not one.

## How Many Pages Is Enough

Start with your top three or four services and your top three or four locations. That gives you nine to sixteen pages to build out. They don't all need to go live on day one — a phased approach works fine.

Focus first on the combinations that represent your most profitable work, or the areas where you're most actively trying to build a customer base. Add more pages as you go, and revisit old ones to improve them as you learn which searches are sending you traffic.
