# Nobody Is Searching for Your Business Name Yet

Most advice about getting found online assumes you've already got something to build on: a few reviews, a Facebook following, some trickle of traffic from people who've heard your name and typed it into Google to check you out. That advice is useful, right up until it isn't, because it skips the stage a lot of businesses are actually in.

If you've just started out, or you've been trading quietly for years without ever putting yourself online, nobody is searching for your business name. Nobody's heard of you yet, and that's the whole reason, nothing to do with the quality of your work. Brand name search, the easiest traffic any business gets, because someone already knows who you are and just wants your number or your website, isn't a channel you have access to yet.

That changes what the right first move actually is.

## Why Brand Name Search Matters So Much

When an established business shows up in Google, a good chunk of that traffic isn't someone typing "plumber in Hastings" and comparing ten options cold. It's someone who already has a name (a recommendation from a neighbour, a van parked on their street, a card left after a job three doors down) searching that name directly. Matching a search to a business is easy once there's a name to go on, and the person searching has usually already decided before the page even loads.

That's the search a brand-new or genuinely obscure business doesn't get: there's no name to search yet. The only door in is the harder one, showing up for what you do rather than who you are.

## You're Starting on the Hardest Search Terms There Are

"Plumber in Hastings" is a generic term, and generic terms are the most competitive ones going. Everyone who's ever worked in the area is trying to rank for it, including businesses that have been trading, reviewed, and linked to for years. A brand-new business steps straight into that fight with none of the things that usually help you win it: no history of reviews, no existing citations, nothing Google has watched build up over time.

That's a different starting position from a business with some traffic already, worth stating plainly rather than glossed over. Going straight for generic search terms is simply where a business with no name recognition starts: there's no name-search stage to skip first. You compete on the hard terms from day one, with less behind you than the businesses already ranking there.

## What Zero Reviews Looks Like to a Stranger

A business with zero reviews and a business that might genuinely not be trustworthy look identical to someone searching cold. There's no way for a stranger to tell "this business just hasn't asked anyone for a review yet" apart from "this business has something to hide." Both show up as an empty list.

That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to treat your first handful of reviews as urgent, not optional, because until they exist, you're being read the same way as a business someone should be wary of, whether that's fair or not.

## The Fundamentals That Actually Move You Off Zero

None of this is complicated, and none of it is fast. Three things matter more than anything else at this stage.

### A Google Business Profile, Set Up Properly

This is free, and it's usually the first thing that shows up when someone searches your trade and area, often before any website does. Correct categories, your real service area, and photos all matter here.

### A Website With Clear Service and Location Pages

Not a one-page site that says "we do everything, everywhere." A page for each service you actually offer, and a page for each area you actually cover, written plainly enough that both Google and a real person searching can tell exactly what you do and where. This is the [local SEO](/services/local-seo) groundwork: it's what gives you something to rank for once you stop relying on a name nobody's searching yet.

### Your First Genuine Reviews

Not a lot. A handful of real customers, asked directly, is enough to stop your listing looking blank. After that, more still helps, but the jump from zero to a few is the one that changes how you're perceived.

## This Takes Weeks to Months, Not Days

There's no shortcut through this stage, and it's worth being upfront about that. Nobody can make search volume for your business name appear before people have heard of you, and nothing earns trust faster than real reviews and consistent work over time. Ranking for even a moderately competitive local term takes weeks at the low end, and months where the competition is stronger. There isn't a faster route round that, only a slower one if you skip the fundamentals.

What does work is doing the visible things consistently: profile set up right, service and location pages in place, reviews requested after every job, from the start. It's slow, unglamorous work, and it compounds. The businesses that get found six months from now are the ones doing this now.
