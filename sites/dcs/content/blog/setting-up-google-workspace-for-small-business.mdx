# Google Workspace for Small Businesses: What You Need to Know

If you run a trades or contractor business and you're still quoting customers from a Gmail or Hotmail address, you're probably not thinking much about it. It works, emails go through, what's the problem?

The problem is the signal it sends. This guide explains what Google Workspace is, how it works, what it costs, and why switching to a professional email address is one of the easiest wins a small business can make.

## What Google Workspace Actually Is

Google Workspace (previously called G Suite) is Google's paid business product. It takes the tools you probably already use — Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar, Google Meet — and ties them to your own domain name.

Instead of `daveplumbing1987@gmail.com`, you'd have `dave@daveplumbing.co.uk`.

Same interface, same apps, same Google you already know. The difference is that the email address carries your business name, not Google's.

## What You Get

A Google Workspace subscription includes:

**Professional email.** This is the main reason most small businesses sign up. Every account gets a custom email address on your domain. You can create multiple addresses — one for you, one for your office manager, one for enquiries — all managed from the same dashboard.

**30GB cloud storage per user** (on the starter Business Starter plan). This covers Drive, Gmail, and Photos. For most small trades businesses, this is more than enough.

**Google Meet.** Video calls, screen sharing, conference calls with customers or suppliers. Included in every plan.

**Google Calendar.** Shared calendars across your team, appointment scheduling, and calendar links you can send to customers.

**Google Drive.** Cloud storage for your documents, contracts, photos, and anything else you need accessible from phone or laptop.

**Admin controls.** You get a central admin panel to manage all your team's accounts — reset passwords, add new users, see who has access to what.

## Free Gmail vs Google Workspace: The Real Difference

The functional difference is smaller than you might think. Gmail and Workspace Gmail are basically the same product. The meaningful differences are:

| | Free Gmail | Google Workspace |
|---|---|---|
| Email address | yourname@gmail.com | you@yourbusiness.co.uk |
| Storage | 15GB | 30GB+ |
| Support | Community forums | Google support |
| Admin controls | None | Full |
| Custom email alias | No | Yes |

The biggest difference isn't features — it's the email address itself. `enquiries@smithselectrical.co.uk` looks professional. `smithselectrical2019@gmail.com` does not.

## How the Pricing Works

Google Workspace is priced per user, per month. You pay for every active account.

As of 2026, the main tiers are:

**Business Starter** — the entry level, and what most small trades businesses need. You get 30GB pooled storage per user, all the core apps, and custom email. One user for a sole trader is an easy decision.

**Business Standard** — adds more storage, longer Meet recordings, and some collaboration features. Worth it if you have a team and rely heavily on shared files.

**Business Plus** — adds enhanced security, eDiscovery, and audit logging. Relevant for businesses with compliance requirements.

For a sole trader or small team of two or three, Business Starter is almost always the right choice.

## How DCS Handles the Setup

The setup process splits into two parts: the Google side and the DNS side.

**What you do:** Sign up for Google Workspace directly at workspace.google.com. Google bills you directly — we're not a reseller. You pay Google, and the subscription is yours regardless of whether you stay with DCS.

**What we handle:** The technical DNS configuration. When you set up Workspace, Google gives you a set of DNS records — MX records for email routing, verification records, and optionally SPF and DKIM records to stop your emails being marked as spam. These need to go into the DNS settings for your domain. It's not complex, but it's easy to get wrong if you've never done it before, and getting it wrong means your email doesn't work. We take care of this as part of any website project.

Once the DNS is set up correctly and propagates (usually a few hours), your professional email is working.

## Why a Professional Email Matters More Than You Think

This might seem like a small thing. It isn't.

When you send a quote to a new customer, your email address is one of the first things they see. `dave@daveselectrical.co.uk` immediately signals: this is a proper business, they have a website, they're set up professionally. `daveelectrical77@gmail.com` signals something different — not necessarily bad, but less established.

The same applies to suppliers, commercial clients, and anyone you deal with professionally. A custom email address makes a difference to how seriously people take you, especially for larger jobs where multiple contractors are quoting.

It's also a basic trust signal. If someone Googles you and finds your website, then gets an email from the domain that matches — that consistency builds confidence. An email from a free account breaks that consistency.

For the price of a few coffees a month, it's an easy upgrade.

## Getting Started

If you're ready to set up Google Workspace for your business, the process is:

1. Register a domain name if you don't have one (we can help with this)
2. Sign up for Google Workspace at workspace.google.com
3. Verify your domain and set the DNS records (this is where most people get stuck — we handle this for you)
4. Create your email addresses
5. Set up Gmail on your phone — it works exactly like regular Gmail

Most businesses are up and running with a professional email within a day of starting the process.
