# What Makes a Great Website for Electricians?

An electrician's website has different requirements to a plasterer's or a painter's. The work is regulated, customers have specific questions about accreditation and compliance, and emergency call-out availability is often the deciding factor in who they call. A generic "professional website" template doesn't address any of that.

This guide covers what an electrical contractor's website specifically needs — from the trust signals that matter to the pages that drive the most enquiries.

## Accreditation Badges: Non-Negotiable

The first thing that separates a credible electrician's website from a basic one is visible accreditation.

**NICEIC and ELECSA** are the two main certification bodies for UK electrical contractors. Customers increasingly know to look for these logos, particularly for larger jobs like consumer unit replacements, new circuits, or rewires. If you're a registered contractor, your certification logo should appear on your homepage, in your footer, and on your key service pages.

**Part P compliance** deserves a specific mention too. Part P of the Building Regulations requires that certain electrical work in domestic properties is notified to building control. Customers who've done any research know about this. A line on your website — "All notifiable work certified under Part P" — removes a potential concern before they've even had to ask.

These aren't just trust signals. They're proof that you're qualified to do the work legally and safely. Display them prominently.

## Emergency Call-Out Messaging

A significant proportion of electrical enquiries are urgent. Tripped fuse boards, failed circuits, power outages at a business premises, safety concerns — people search for an electrician when they need one now.

Your website needs to speak directly to this. That means:

- Clear statement of whether you offer emergency or same-day call-outs
- Approximate response time if you can quote one ("typically within 2 hours")
- Phone number prominently displayed — not buried in a footer, front and centre at the top of every page
- A click-to-call button that works on mobile, because emergency searches almost always happen on a phone

If you don't do emergency work, that's fine — but you need to be equally clear about your typical lead times so customers know what to expect.

## Domestic vs Commercial: Should You Split Them?

If you work across both domestic and commercial clients, it's worth considering separate sections for each audience. A homeowner looking to get lights fixed has completely different questions to a facilities manager needing a periodic inspection for a commercial building.

A domestic customer wants: how quickly can you come out, how much does it roughly cost, are you registered.

A commercial client wants: do you have the right insurance, can you work to a schedule, do you understand their compliance requirements.

If commercial work is a meaningful part of your business, a dedicated commercial page (or section) speaks to that audience in their terms. It also gives you a distinct landing page to send commercial enquiries to from email or LinkedIn.

## The Pages That Actually Drive Calls

Generic websites list services vaguely — "electrical installations, repairs, testing." The websites that rank well and convert visitors into enquiries have specific, named service pages. These work because people search for specific things.

**Consumer unit / fuse board upgrades.** One of the most common electrical jobs. High-value, frequently searched, and often driven by necessity (old fuse boxes, house purchase requirements). A dedicated page for this service will pick up significant search traffic.

**EV charger installation.** Growing rapidly. Customers searching for this are typically making a specific purchase decision, making them high-intent leads. A clear page covering installation process, compatible charger brands, and timelines positions you well for this search.

**Landlord Electrical Safety Certificates (EICRs).** Legally required for rental properties on a five-year cycle. Landlords often need these arranged quickly, and many search specifically for this service. A clear, informative page wins these enquiries.

**Rewires.** High-value jobs that homeowners research carefully before committing. A detailed page covering what a full rewire involves, how long it takes, and what signs indicate it might be needed will rank for longer research-intent searches.

**Periodic inspection reports (EICR).** Different audience to landlord certificates — this covers homeowners who've been recommended an inspection or are buying a house.

## Why Location Pages Matter Especially for Electricians

Electricians typically serve a fairly tight geographic area — the nature of the work means you're rarely travelling more than 30 or 40 minutes for domestic jobs. That means local SEO is particularly valuable.

Location pages — individual pages for each major town or area you cover — help you appear in searches from those areas specifically. "Electrician in Lewes," "EICR Eastbourne," "fuse board replacement Seaford." These searches have high intent: someone searching for an electrician in their town is likely to call one of the first results they see.

A well-structured electrician website might have location pages for eight to twelve different towns within the coverage area, each optimised for that area's search terms.
