# How to Get More Google Reviews as a Tradesperson

If you want to rank higher on Google Maps, there's one thing that matters more than anything else: reviews. Not website tricks, not paid ads — reviews. The more you have, the more recent they are, and the higher your average rating, the better you rank. And the better you rank, the more people call you.

Most tradespeople know they should be getting more reviews. The problem isn't motivation — it's not having a simple, repeatable process that makes it easy to ask and easy for customers to respond. This guide gives you exactly that.

## Why Reviews Are the #1 Ranking Factor

Google Maps uses reviews as a proxy for trust. A business with 60 reviews at 4.8 stars has proven it consistently does good work. Google rewards that with higher placement in the Local Pack — the three listings that appear at the top of local searches.

The numbers matter more than most people realise. In a competitive market, appearing in those three spots depends heavily on your review count relative to local competitors. Generally:

- **10+ reviews** — you start appearing consistently in local searches
- **30+ reviews** — you can compete for top three in most local markets
- **60+ reviews** — you're in a strong position, especially if they're recent

The other factor is recency. A business with 50 reviews, all from three years ago, will often lose out to one with 20 reviews, half of which are from the last six months. Google treats recent reviews as a signal that you're still active and still doing good work.

## The Process: Short Link, Right Moment, Simple Ask

The process that works best is straightforward. Here's each step.

**Step 1: Get your review link.** Log in to your Google Business Profile at business.google.com. Go to your profile, click "Ask for reviews," and copy the short link Google generates. It looks something like `g.page/r/[your-id]/review`. Save this somewhere you can access it quickly — in your notes app, or as a saved WhatsApp message template.

**Step 2: Ask at the right moment.** The best moment to ask is when the job is done and the customer has just expressed satisfaction. You've packed up, they've seen the finished work, they've said something positive. That's your window. Don't wait until you're back in the van. Do it while you're still there.

**Step 3: Send the link directly.** A face-to-face ask followed by a text or WhatsApp message with the link is the most effective combination. Something like:

> "Really glad you're happy with it. If you get a couple of minutes, a Google review makes a big difference for the business — here's the link: [your review link]"

That's it. Short, not pushy, and gives them everything they need. Most people who are happy will leave a review within 24 hours if they're going to at all — the link removes the friction of searching for you themselves.

**Step 4: Make it a habit.** The biggest mistake is asking occasionally rather than consistently. Build it into how you close every job. If you have a follow-up text you send the next day to check everything's okay, add the review link to that message. The more consistently you ask, the more predictably reviews come in.

## What to Say When Asking in Person

Some tradespeople find the ask awkward. It doesn't need to be. A few lines that work well:

- "We rely on Google reviews more than anything else for new customers — if you're happy, it would genuinely help us out."
- "If you ever use us again or recommend us to someone, a review just helps people find us more easily."
- "I don't do paid advertising — reviews are how we get our name out there."

Being straightforward about why it matters tends to land better than a vague request. People understand supporting a small local business.

## What to Do With Bad Reviews

At some point, you'll get a negative review. It happens to every business. The way you handle it matters almost as much as the review itself.

**Never delete or ignore a negative review.** You can't delete a genuine review anyway — Google doesn't allow it unless it violates their policies. Ignoring it looks worse than responding.

**Respond calmly and professionally.** Every response you write is seen by every future customer who reads your profile. A measured, polite response to a complaint shows that you take your work seriously and handle problems like an adult. Something like:

> "Thank you for the feedback. I'm sorry this didn't meet your expectations. I'd welcome the chance to discuss this further — please contact me directly."

Don't get defensive, don't go into detail publicly, and never be rude. If the review is factually incorrect or violates Google's guidelines (it's fake, it's from a non-customer, it contains prohibited content), you can flag it for Google to review.

**Let good reviews outnumber bad ones.** The best defence against an occasional poor review is having enough positive ones that the overall picture is clearly positive. One three-star review among 50 five-star reviews doesn't hurt you. One bad review out of six reviews can sink you.

## The Compounding Effect

This is the part that makes reviews so powerful for local businesses: they compound.

More reviews → better ranking → more customers find you → more jobs → more reviews.

A tradesperson who gets into the habit of asking for reviews after every job, even just a couple per week, will accumulate 50 or 60 reviews in a year. That puts them ahead of almost every local competitor who doesn't have a system. And once you're in the top three on Google Maps, the work keeps coming without spending anything on advertising.

Most trades businesses don't do this. It's not complicated — they just haven't built the habit. The ones who do build it tend to see a significant difference within three to six months.
