# Moving Your Old Emails Into Your New Business Inbox

One thing stops people moving off a personal address longer than anything else: it feels like starting from nothing. Years of quotes, supplier threads, and customer conversations sit in that old inbox, and the idea of setting up a new one feels like walking away from all of it. It isn't. Done properly, the old mail comes with you.

## A new account, not an upgrade

Setting up `you@yourbusiness.co.uk` means creating a new Google Workspace account tied to your domain. It doesn't touch the personal Gmail, Outlook, or other account you're currently using. That account stays exactly as it is, still yours, for as long as you want to keep it.

The new account runs on the same Gmail interface you already know, just addressed at your own domain instead of somebody else's. Nothing to relearn. The only real change is what's printed after the @.

## Bringing the old mail across

This is the part that actually matters: the new mailbox doesn't have to start empty. Google's tools can pull mail in from an existing account rather than leaving you to rebuild years of history by hand.

If the old account is another Gmail address, Gmail's own import settings handle it directly, pulling across mail and contacts in one go. For most other providers, an Outlook account, a Yahoo address, an existing business inbox on IMAP, Google Workspace's own migration tools do the same job from the admin side. Either way, the quotes and customer threads that built up over years land in the new mailbox instead of sitting orphaned in an account nobody's checking anymore.

## Why it's worth checking the source first

Not every provider makes this equally easy. How much access a provider gives to an outside tool trying to pull mail out varies, and some restrict that access behind a paid tier or don't offer it at all on a free account. Before telling a client their full email history is moving across in one clean step, it's worth checking what their specific provider actually allows rather than assuming every inbox behaves the same way. Where a full import isn't possible, forwarding new mail and keeping the old account open for reference usually covers what's actually needed day to day.

## The overlap period still matters

Importing the old mail solves the history problem. It doesn't solve the transition problem, which is a separate thing: contacts, suppliers, and the paperwork trail that already has the old address printed on it don't update themselves the moment the new one goes live.

I've written before about [getting the DNS and mail routing changed over without losing anything mid-switch](/blog/getting-off-gmail-without-breaking-your-email), which is the mechanical side of the move. This is the other half: even with years of mail sitting safely in the new inbox, keep the old address forwarding, or checked, for a few weeks while everyone who has it catches up to the new one.

## How long it actually takes

The import itself runs in the background once it's set up. A few years of mail for one person is typically done within a day, longer for a bigger mailbox or several accounts moving at once. None of that blocks using the new address in the meantime. The new mailbox is live and usable from day one, and the older mail just keeps arriving alongside it until the import catches up.

## What this actually gets you

A professional address at your own domain, all the history that mattered from the old one sitting in the new inbox, and nothing lost in the switch.
